## Hardware that lasts

Hinges, drawer channels, handles and locks take the most wear. Soft close hinges and full extension channels from reliable brands cost more at first but outlast cheap ones many times over. Ask the carpenter which brands they trust, and keep a few spare hinges and handles from the same batch for future repairs.

## Choosing the right material

A good furniture carpenter in Gujranwala helps you match material to budget and use. **Solid wood** such as sheesham, deodar, kail or imported oak is strong, repairable and lasts for generations, but costs more and must be well seasoned or it will warp. **Plywood** is stable and strong for wardrobes and cabinets, and marine ply resists moisture in kitchens and bathrooms. **MDF** gives a smooth surface for paint or lacquer but swells if water gets in, so every edge must be sealed. **Chipboard** is the cheapest and best kept for light, dry uses. **Laminate and veneer** are surface finishes: laminate is tough and easy to clean, veneer gives a natural wood look. Ask to see samples before you decide.

## Quality signs in good woodwork

Doors and drawers that open smoothly and close without gaps, joints that are tight and square, edges that are sealed, hinges and channels from reliable brands, screws that are hidden or neatly capped, and a finish that is even with no runs or rough patches. A careful carpenter measures twice, protects your floor while working and cleans up sawdust before leaving.

## Warning signs of a poor carpenter

Be careful if a carpenter will not give a written quote, pushes cheap board while charging for better material, asks for most of the money upfront, uses nails where screws or joints are needed, or leaves edges raw. Good carpenters explain what they will use and why, and are happy for you to inspect the material before work starts.

## Caring for wooden furniture

Keep wooden furniture out of direct sun and away from heaters and AC vents, wipe spills quickly, use coasters and mats, and dust with a soft dry cloth. Polish solid wood once or twice a year, oil hinges and channels, and tighten loose handles before they damage the wood. In the monsoon, keep cupboards slightly open on dry days so moisture can escape.
