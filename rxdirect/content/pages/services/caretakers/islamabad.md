## Elderly caretaker Islamabad: what a caretaker does

An elderly caretaker in Islamabad usually helps with walking and moving safely around the house, getting in and out of bed, chairs and the car, bathing, dressing and grooming, toileting and continence care, preparing simple meals and helping the person eat, giving tablets on time from a medicine box prepared by the family or doctor, light exercises suggested by the doctor or physiotherapist, accompanying the person to doctor visits, the mosque, the park or family gatherings, keeping the room clean and comfortable, and, just as important, providing company and conversation.

A caretaker does not give injections, manage IV lines or care for feeding tubes and catheters. Those tasks need a qualified [home nurse in Islamabad](/services/nurses/islamabad).

## Caring for parents with dementia

Memory loss is one of the hardest situations for any family. A parent may forget names, repeat questions, become anxious in the evening, refuse food or try to leave the house. A good dementia caretaker stays calm, follows a steady routine, gently redirects rather than arguing, keeps doors and gates secure and keeps the family informed. We shortlist caretakers whose references specifically mention dementia care, and we encourage families to share the parent's life story, favourite foods and routines so the caretaker can connect with them.

## Preventing falls

Falls are a major danger for elderly people. A caretaker helps by walking beside the person, making sure they use their walker or stick, keeping floors dry and clear, using a night light and helping them get up slowly. Simple changes at home, such as grab bars in the bathroom, a raised toilet seat, a non slip mat and a bed at the right height, make a big difference. Many Islamabad homes have stairs, so consider moving an elderly parent to a ground floor room.

## Seasons and elderly health

Islamabad winters are cold, and elderly people need warm rooms, warm clothing and safe heating, with gas heaters never left burning in closed rooms at night. Spring pollen can worsen breathing problems. In summer, the caretaker watches fluid intake carefully. A caretaker who notices small changes early, such as less appetite, more sleepiness or new confusion, helps the family act before a problem becomes an emergency.

## Companionship and dignity

Many elderly parents are lonely, especially when children live abroad. A caretaker who sits and talks, reads the newspaper aloud, plays a game of ludo, takes them for a walk in the park or to Friday prayers and helps them call family on video does as much good as any medicine. Ask the caretaker to treat your parent with respect, to let them do what they can for themselves and to protect their privacy during bathing and toileting.

## Updates for families abroad

Overseas families can ask the caretaker for a short daily WhatsApp message and a weekly video call with their parent. A trusted relative should visit regularly and check the house, the medicine box and the parent's wellbeing.

## Caretaker, nurse or helper?

Families often ask which role they need. A **helper** suits parents who are still largely independent and need company, errands and help around the house. A **caretaker** suits parents who need hands on help with walking, bathing, toileting and eating. A **nurse** is needed when there are medical procedures such as injections, wound care, catheters or feeding tubes. Many families move from a helper to a caretaker as a parent grows older, and add a nurse for a few weeks after a hospital stay. Describe your parent's day and we will suggest the right fit.

## Food, fluids and medicines

Elderly people often eat and drink too little, which leads to weakness, confusion and infections. A caretaker offers small, soft meals and drinks through the day, follows any diet the doctor has set for diabetes, blood pressure or kidney problems, and keeps a simple note of what was eaten. Tablets should be given from a weekly pill box filled by a family member, with the caretaker ticking each dose on a chart.

## Keeping a good caretaker

Pay on time, give a weekly day off with relief cover, provide proper meals and a clean place to rest, and treat the caretaker with respect. Caring for an elderly person is emotionally and physically demanding, and a caretaker who feels valued stays longer and gives better care.
