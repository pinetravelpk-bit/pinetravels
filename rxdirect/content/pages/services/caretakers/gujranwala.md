## Elderly caretaker Gujranwala: what a caretaker does

An elderly caretaker in Gujranwala usually helps with walking and moving safely around the house, getting in and out of bed, chairs and the car, bathing, dressing and grooming, toileting and continence care, preparing simple meals and helping the person eat, giving tablets on time from a medicine box prepared by the family or doctor, light exercises suggested by the doctor or physiotherapist, accompanying the person to doctor visits, the mosque or family gatherings, keeping the room clean and comfortable, and providing company and conversation.

A caretaker does not give injections, manage IV lines or care for feeding tubes and catheters. Those tasks need a qualified [home nurse in Gujranwala](/services/nurses/gujranwala).

## Managing care from abroad

Overseas families can manage a caretaker well with a few simple habits. Join the interview by video and ask practical questions. Ask a trusted relative to be present on the first days and to visit at least weekly. Agree a short daily WhatsApp message from the caretaker, with a photo of your parent and a note on meals, medicines and mood, and a weekly video call where you can see and talk to your parent directly. Keep the doctor's number with both the caretaker and the relative. CCTV in the living areas, with the caretaker told openly, gives extra reassurance. When you visit for Eid or a wedding, spend time with the caretaker and thank them.

## Male or female caretaker?

Most families choose a female caretaker for a mother and a male caretaker for a father, especially because bathing and toileting are involved. When both parents need care, a [domestic couple](/services/couples/gujranwala) can sometimes work well. Tell us your situation.

## Caring for parents with dementia

A parent with dementia may forget names, repeat questions, become anxious in the evening, refuse food or try to leave the house. A good dementia caretaker stays calm, follows a steady routine, gently redirects rather than arguing, keeps doors and gates secure and keeps the family informed.

## Preventing falls

A caretaker walks beside the person, makes sure they use their walker or stick, keeps floors dry and clear, uses a night light and helps them get up slowly. Gujranwala's large houses often have stairs and marble floors, so grab bars, non slip mats and a ground floor bedroom make a big difference.

## Caretaker, nurse or helper?

A **helper** suits parents who are still largely independent. A **caretaker** suits parents who need hands on help with walking, bathing, toileting and eating. A **nurse** is needed for medical procedures. Many families start with a [helper](/services/helpers/gujranwala) and move to a caretaker as a parent grows older.

## Food, fluids and medicines

A caretaker offers small, soft meals and drinks through the day, follows any diet the doctor has set and keeps a note of what was eaten. Tablets should be given from a weekly pill box filled by a relative, with each dose ticked on a chart.

## Companionship and dignity

Loneliness is often the biggest problem for parents whose children are abroad. A caretaker who sits and talks, takes them to the mosque or to visit relatives and helps them call their children on video does as much good as any medicine.

## Signs the caretaker is working well

After the first month, look for simple signs: your parent seems calmer and cleaner, eats better, takes medicines on time, has fewer falls and talks about the caretaker warmly. The medicine chart is filled in and the caretaker reports small changes without being asked. If your relative notices unexplained bruises, sudden fear, weight loss or a parent who goes quiet when the caretaker is near, act at once and tell us.

## Heat, fog and elderly health

Gujranwala summers are hot, and power cuts put elderly people at risk of dehydration, so the caretaker offers water often and keeps the room cool. In winter, fog and cold bring chest infections, and gas heaters must never be left burning in a closed room at night.

## Keeping a good caretaker

Pay on time, give a weekly day off with relief cover, provide proper meals and a clean place to rest, and treat the caretaker with respect. A short call of thanks from abroad is noticed and valued.
