## Elderly caretaker Faisalabad: what a caretaker does

An elderly caretaker in Faisalabad usually helps with walking and moving safely around the house, getting in and out of bed, chairs and the car, bathing, dressing and grooming, toileting and continence care, preparing simple meals and helping the person eat, giving tablets on time from a medicine box prepared by the family or doctor, light exercises suggested by the doctor or physiotherapist, accompanying the person to doctor visits, the mosque or family gatherings, keeping the room clean and comfortable, and providing company and conversation.

A caretaker does not give injections, manage IV lines or care for feeding tubes and catheters. Those tasks need a qualified [home nurse in Faisalabad](/services/nurses/faisalabad).

## Diabetes and its complications

Diabetes is very common among older people in Faisalabad, and it brings complications: poor eyesight, numb feet, slow healing wounds, kidney problems and sudden drops in sugar. A good caretaker follows the diabetic diet the doctor has set, makes sure meals are not skipped, checks feet daily for cuts or sores, keeps sweets for an emergency low and tells the family at once about any wound that is not healing. For sugar checks with a glucometer, the family or a nurse can train the caretaker.

## Male or female caretaker?

Most families choose a female caretaker for a mother and a male caretaker for a father, especially because bathing and toileting are involved. Male caretakers are often preferred for heavier patients who need lifting. Tell us your preference.

## Caring for parents with dementia

A parent with dementia may forget names, repeat questions, become anxious in the evening, refuse food or try to leave the house. A good dementia caretaker stays calm, follows a steady routine, gently redirects rather than arguing, keeps doors and gates secure and keeps the family informed.

## Joint families and visitors

In Faisalabad's joint families, many relatives help with an elderly parent, and many visitors come to sit with them. That is a blessing, but it can confuse a caretaker if everyone gives different instructions. Agree one family member who the caretaker reports to, and agree visiting times so the parent gets enough rest.

## Preventing falls

A caretaker walks beside the person, makes sure they use their walker or stick, keeps floors dry and clear, uses a night light and helps them get up slowly. Grab bars, a raised toilet seat, a non slip mat and a ground floor room make a big difference.

## Heat, fog and elderly health

Faisalabad's long summer and power cuts put elderly people at risk of dehydration and heatstroke, so the caretaker offers water often and keeps the room cool. In winter, fog and cold bring chest infections, so the parent needs warm clothing and a warm, ventilated room.

## Caretaker, nurse or helper?

A **helper** suits parents who are still largely independent. A **caretaker** suits parents who need hands on help with walking, bathing, toileting and eating. A **nurse** is needed for medical procedures. Many families move from a helper to a caretaker as a parent grows older.

## Food, fluids and medicines

A caretaker offers small, soft meals and drinks through the day and keeps a simple note of what was eaten. Tablets should be given from a weekly pill box filled by a family member, with each dose ticked on a chart.

## Companionship and dignity

A caretaker who sits and talks, reads the newspaper aloud, takes the parent to the mosque or a family gathering and helps them call relatives on video does as much good as any medicine. Ask the caretaker to treat your parent with respect and protect their privacy.

## Signs the caretaker is working well

After the first month, look for simple signs: your parent seems calmer and cleaner, eats better, takes medicines on time, has fewer falls or near misses and talks about the caretaker warmly. The room is tidy, the medicine chart is filled in and the caretaker tells you about small changes without being asked. If you see the opposite, such as unexplained bruises, sudden fear, weight loss or a parent who goes quiet when the caretaker is near, act at once and tell us.

## Keeping a good caretaker

Pay on time, give a weekly day off with relief cover, provide proper meals and a clean place to rest, and treat the caretaker with respect in front of the wider family. A caretaker who feels valued stays longer and gives better care.
