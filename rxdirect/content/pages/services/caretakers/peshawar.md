## Elderly caretaker Peshawar: what a caretaker does

An elderly caretaker in Peshawar usually helps with walking and moving safely around the house, getting in and out of bed, chairs and the car, bathing, dressing and grooming, toileting and continence care, preparing simple meals and helping the person eat, giving tablets on time from a medicine box prepared by the family or doctor, light exercises suggested by the doctor or physiotherapist, accompanying the person to doctor visits, the mosque, the hujra or family gatherings, keeping the room clean and comfortable, and providing company and conversation.

A caretaker does not give injections, manage IV lines or care for feeding tubes and catheters. Those tasks need a qualified [home nurse in Peshawar](/services/nurses/peshawar).

## Caring for parents with dementia

A parent with dementia may forget names, repeat questions, become anxious in the evening, refuse food or try to leave the house. A good dementia caretaker stays calm, follows a steady routine, gently redirects rather than arguing, keeps doors and gates secure and keeps the family informed. In Pashto speaking homes, a caretaker who speaks the parent's language makes an enormous difference, because memory loss often takes away later learned languages first.

## Preventing falls

A caretaker walks beside the person, makes sure they use their walker or stick, keeps floors dry and clear, uses a night light and helps them get up slowly. Grab bars, a raised toilet seat, a non slip mat and a ground floor room make a big difference.

## Winter, summer and elderly health

Peshawar winters are cold. Elderly people need warm rooms and warm clothing, and gas heaters must never be left burning in a closed room while a parent sleeps. In summer, heat and power cuts bring a risk of dehydration, so the caretaker offers water often and keeps the room cool.

## Caretaker, nurse or helper?

A **helper** suits parents who are still largely independent. A **caretaker** suits parents who need hands on help with walking, bathing, toileting and eating. A **nurse** is needed for medical procedures. Many families move from a helper to a caretaker as a parent grows older.

## Food, fluids and medicines

A caretaker offers small, soft meals and drinks through the day, follows any diet the doctor has set and keeps a simple note of what was eaten. Tablets should be given from a weekly pill box filled by a family member, with each dose ticked on a chart.

## Companionship and dignity

A caretaker who sits and talks in Pashto, helps a father greet his visitors in the hujra, takes a mother to see relatives and helps parents call their sons abroad on video does as much good as any medicine. Ask the caretaker to treat your parent with respect, let them do what they can for themselves and protect their privacy.

## Signs the caretaker is working well

After the first month, look for simple signs: your parent seems calmer and cleaner, eats better, takes medicines on time and talks about the caretaker warmly. If you see unexplained bruises, sudden fear or a parent who goes quiet when the caretaker is near, act at once and tell us.

## Visitors and rest

In Peshawar, relatives, neighbours and elders from the community visit an ailing parent often, and turning visitors away would be unthinkable. A caretaker can help by making sure the parent is clean, dressed and comfortable before guests arrive, serving tea, and gently suggesting rest when the parent tires. Visitors with coughs or colds should be kept at a distance from a frail parent, and the caretaker can quietly make sure the parent's medicines and meals are not missed on busy days.

## Keeping a good caretaker

Pay on time, give a weekly day off with relief cover, provide proper meals and a clean place to rest, and treat the caretaker with respect. Peshawar families value loyalty, and a caretaker who is respected often stays for many years. Agree leave for Eid in advance.
