## Elderly caretaker Multan: what a caretaker does

An elderly caretaker in Multan usually helps with walking and moving safely around the house, getting in and out of bed, chairs and the car, bathing, dressing and grooming, toileting and continence care, preparing simple meals and helping the person eat, giving tablets on time from a medicine box prepared by the family or doctor, light exercises suggested by the doctor or physiotherapist, accompanying the person to doctor visits, the mosque or family gatherings, keeping the room clean and comfortable, and providing company and conversation.

A caretaker does not give injections, manage IV lines or care for feeding tubes and catheters. Those tasks need a qualified [home nurse in Multan](/services/nurses/multan).

## Keeping elders safe in Multan's heat

Multan's summer is dangerous for elderly people. Older bodies feel thirst less and cool down more slowly, and many take medicines that make heat illness more likely. From April to October, a good caretaker keeps the parent indoors between late morning and late afternoon, offers water, lassi, lemon water or ORS often, dresses them in light cotton, keeps curtains closed against the sun and runs fans or the air cooler. During power cuts, damp cloths on the forehead and arms and a battery fan help. The caretaker watches for dizziness, unusual tiredness, confusion, very dark urine or a high temperature, and calls the family at once. A UPS for at least one fan in the parent's room is a sensible investment.

## Caring for parents with dementia

A parent with dementia may forget names, repeat questions, become anxious in the evening, refuse food or try to leave the house. A good dementia caretaker stays calm, follows a steady routine, gently redirects rather than arguing, keeps doors and gates secure and keeps the family informed.

## Preventing falls

A caretaker walks beside the person, makes sure they use their walker or stick, keeps floors dry and clear, uses a night light and helps them get up slowly. Grab bars, a raised toilet seat, a non slip mat and a ground floor room make a big difference.

## Visits to the farm or village

Some Multan elders like to spend time at the family's farm or village. A caretaker can travel with them, but plan carefully: the nearest hospital, medicines for the whole stay, a cool room and a safe bathroom. Agree extra pay for travel days in advance.

## Caretaker, nurse or helper?

A **helper** suits parents who are still largely independent. A **caretaker** suits parents who need hands on help with walking, bathing, toileting and eating. A **nurse** is needed for medical procedures. Many families move from a helper to a caretaker as a parent grows older.

## Food, fluids and medicines

A caretaker offers small, soft meals and drinks through the day, follows any diet the doctor has set and keeps a simple note of what was eaten and drunk. Tablets should be given from a weekly pill box filled by a family member, with each dose ticked on a chart.

## Companionship and dignity

A caretaker who sits and talks, takes the parent for a short walk on a cool evening, accompanies them to the mosque or a shrine they love and helps them call relatives on video does as much good as any medicine. Ask the caretaker to treat your parent with respect and protect their privacy.

## Signs the caretaker is working well

After the first month, look for simple signs: your parent seems calmer and cleaner, eats better, takes medicines on time, has fewer falls or near misses and talks about the caretaker warmly. The room is tidy, the medicine chart is filled in and the caretaker tells you about small changes without being asked. If you see the opposite, such as unexplained bruises, sudden fear, weight loss or a parent who goes quiet when the caretaker is near, act at once and tell us.

## Keeping a good caretaker

Pay on time, give a weekly day off with relief cover, provide proper meals and a cool, clean place to rest, and treat the caretaker with respect. A caretaker who feels valued stays longer and gives better care.
