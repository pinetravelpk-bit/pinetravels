## Babysitter Islamabad or nanny: which do you need?

A **babysitter in Islamabad** looks after children occasionally: an evening when parents go to a dinner in F-6, a few hours on a Saturday, or during a wedding or family event. A **nanny** provides regular childcare, usually full time, and becomes part of the child's daily routine. Nannies follow routines for feeding, naps, play and learning, and often stay with a family for years. Most families who contact us need a nanny, and some also want an occasional babysitter. We can arrange both.

## What a nanny does in an Islamabad home

Typical nanny duties include feeding babies and children, preparing bottles and simple meals, sterilising feeding equipment, bathing, dressing and changing nappies, settling children for naps and bedtime, playing and reading, age appropriate activities, getting older children ready for school, helping with homework, keeping the nursery and children's clothes tidy, accompanying children to the park, classes or appointments, and watching for signs of illness.

Nannies are not usually expected to do general housework. Keeping a nanny focused on the children is better for the children and the nanny. If you also need cleaning or cooking, consider a [maid in Islamabad](/services/maids/islamabad).

## Choosing the right nanny for your child's age

**Newborns and infants** need a nanny who understands feeding schedules, safe sleep, sterilisation, nappy care and soothing a crying baby. A calm, gentle temperament matters most. **Toddlers** need energy, patience and a sharp eye for safety, because toddlers climb, run and put everything in their mouths. **School going children** need someone who manages school routines, helps with homework, encourages good habits and keeps screen time in check. Tell us your child's age and personality, and we will shortlist nannies with matching experience.

Islamabad has many foreign and diplomatic families, as well as Pakistani families returning from the UK, the US, Canada or the Gulf. These families often want a nanny who speaks English, follows routines such as fixed bedtimes and limited sugar, understands car seat use and is comfortable with children who speak little Urdu. Mention these preferences and we will shortlist accordingly. If the nanny will travel with the family to other cities or abroad, discuss it early.

## School runs and activities

Islamabad children often have busy schedules: school in the morning, then Quran lessons, tuition, swimming, football or art classes. A nanny may travel with the children in the family car with a driver, wait during classes and keep them fed and organised between activities. Make a weekly schedule and share it with both the nanny and the driver.

## Setting house rules

Agree on feeding, sweets and snacks; screen time; how to handle tantrums, never smacking or shouting; who can visit and who can collect the children; where the nanny may take them; phone use, limited to breaks and never near water; and emergencies, including who to call and the nearest hospital, such as PIMS, Shifa International or Maroof International. Write these down and review them after the first week.

## Safety at home

A nanny's job is easier and safer when the home is child safe: socket covers, cabinet locks for medicines and cleaning products, gates on stairs, secure windows and balconies, and no small objects within reach of babies. Many Islamabad homes have several floors and terraces, so stair gates and balcony locks matter. Make sure the nanny knows basic first aid for choking, burns and falls.

## Seasons and children's health

Islamabad's spring pollen triggers allergies in many children, winter brings coughs and colds, and summer heat and monsoon bring stomach bugs and dengue risk. A good nanny watches for early signs of illness, keeps children hydrated, uses mosquito repellent in the evening during monsoon and tells parents promptly when something seems wrong.

## Building trust over time

Trust is built gradually. In the first weeks, work from home when you can, come back unexpectedly sometimes and talk to your children about their day. Many parents use cameras in living areas; if you do, tell the nanny openly. Over time, a good nanny earns the family's trust, and the relationship often lasts years.

## Keeping a good nanny

Pay on time, give a weekly day off, keep duties focused on the children and treat the nanny with respect in front of the children. Nannies stay with families who value them, and a nanny who stays through your child's early years gives them stability that matters.
