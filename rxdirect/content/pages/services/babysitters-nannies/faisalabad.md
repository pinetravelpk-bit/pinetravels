Nannies follow routines for feeding, naps, play and learning, and often stay with a family for years. Most families who contact us need a nanny, and some also want an occasional babysitter. ## What a nanny does in a Faisalabad home

Typical nanny duties include feeding babies and children, preparing bottles and simple meals, sterilising feeding equipment, bathing, dressing and changing nappies, settling children for naps and bedtime, playing and reading, age appropriate activities, getting older children ready for school, helping with homework, keeping the nursery and children's clothes tidy, accompanying children to the park, classes or appointments, and watching for signs of illness.

**Newborns and infants** need a nanny who understands feeding schedules, safe sleep, sterilisation, nappy care and soothing a crying baby. A calm, gentle temperament matters most. **Toddlers** need energy, patience and a sharp eye for safety, because toddlers climb, run and put everything in their mouths. **School going children** need someone who manages school routines, helps with homework, encourages good habits and keeps screen time in check. ## Joint families and the nanny

Many Faisalabad homes are joint families, with grandparents, uncles, aunts and cousins under one roof. That brings love and support, but also many voices. Before the nanny starts, agree as a family who sets the rules on feeding, sleep and discipline, and who the nanny should ask when unsure. In a joint family, the nanny may also be asked to look after cousins. Agree clearly whether that is part of the job, and adjust pay if so.

## Business families and long days

In business families where both parents are involved in the mill or the office, days are long and sometimes unpredictable. A live-in nanny, or a day nanny with flexible hours, suits these homes best. Share your typical schedule, including late evenings and travel, so we can find a nanny who is comfortable with it.

## Weather and children's health

Faisalabad summers are hot, and children need protection from the midday sun, plenty of water and a watchful eye for heat exhaustion. Dust storms can trigger coughs and allergies. In winter, fog and smog affect children with asthma, so a nanny should keep them indoors on bad days and plan indoor activities. In monsoon, dengue and stomach bugs are common, so mosquito repellent and strict hygiene with food and bottles matter.

## Setting house rules

Agree on feeding, sweets and snacks; screen time; how to handle tantrums, never smacking or shouting; who can visit and who can collect the children; where the nanny may take them; phone use, limited to breaks and never near water; and emergencies, including who to call and the nearest hospital. Write these down and review them after the first week.

## Safety at home

A nanny's job is easier and safer when the home is child safe: socket covers, cabinet locks for medicines and cleaning products, gates on stairs, secure windows, balconies and roofs, and no small objects within reach of babies. Many Faisalabad homes have open roofs used for drying clothes, so make sure children cannot reach them alone. Make sure the nanny knows basic first aid.

## Warning signs during the trial

Positive signs include getting down to the child's level, speaking softly, following your routines and washing hands before feeding. Warning signs include using the phone while the child plays near stairs or water, raising her voice quickly, forcing food or ignoring crying. Trust your instincts and tell us if something feels wrong.

## Building trust over time

In the first weeks, be at home when you can, come back unexpectedly sometimes and talk to your children about their day. If you use cameras, tell the nanny openly. Over time, a good nanny earns the family's trust, and the relationship often lasts years.

## Keeping a good nanny

Pay on time, give a weekly day off, keep duties focused on the children and treat the nanny with respect in front of the children and the wider family. Nannies stay with families who value them.
