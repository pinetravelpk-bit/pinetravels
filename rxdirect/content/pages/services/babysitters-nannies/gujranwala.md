## Babysitter Gujranwala or nanny: which do you need?

A **babysitter in Gujranwala** looks after children occasionally: an evening out, a few hours during a wedding function, or extra help when relatives visit. A **nanny** provides regular childcare, usually full time, and becomes part of the child's daily routine. Nannies follow routines for feeding, naps, play and learning, and often stay with a family for years. Most families who contact us need a nanny, and some also want an occasional babysitter. We can arrange both.

## What a nanny does in a Gujranwala home

Typical nanny duties include feeding babies and children, preparing bottles and simple meals, sterilising feeding equipment, bathing, dressing and changing nappies, settling children for naps and bedtime, playing and reading, age appropriate activities, getting older children ready for school, helping with homework, keeping the nursery and children's clothes tidy, accompanying children to the park, classes or appointments, and watching for signs of illness.

Nannies are not usually expected to do general housework. Keeping a nanny focused on the children is better for everyone. If you also need cleaning or cooking, consider a [maid in Gujranwala](/services/maids/gujranwala).

## Choosing the right nanny for your child's age

**Newborns and infants** need a nanny who understands feeding schedules, safe sleep, sterilisation, nappy care and soothing a crying baby. A calm, gentle temperament matters most. **Toddlers** need energy, patience and a sharp eye for safety. **School going children** need someone who manages school routines, helps with homework, encourages good habits and keeps screen time in check. Tell us your child's age and personality, and we will shortlist nannies with matching experience.

## Children living with grandparents

Some Gujranwala children live with grandparents while their parents work abroad. Grandparents give love and stability, but chasing a toddler or managing school runs can be exhausting at their age. A nanny takes on the physical work: bathing, feeding, playing, school preparation and homework, while grandparents remain in charge. Parents abroad can stay involved through video calls with the nanny, photos of the day and regular updates. Agree clearly who the nanny reports to, and make sure grandparents feel respected rather than replaced.

## Visiting children from abroad

Children visiting from Europe or the UK often have different routines, foods and languages. A nanny who speaks some English, understands that these children may not eat spicy food, keeps them hydrated in the heat and protects them from stomach bugs makes the visit much easier. Mention visiting children in your brief.

## Weather and children's health

Gujranwala summers are hot, so children need water, shade and protection from the midday sun. In winter, fog and smog affect children with asthma. In monsoon, dengue and stomach bugs are common, so mosquito repellent and hygiene with food and bottles matter.

## Setting house rules

Agree on feeding, sweets and snacks; screen time; how to handle tantrums, never smacking or shouting; who can visit and who can collect the children; where the nanny may take them; phone use, limited to breaks and never near water; and emergencies, including who to call and the nearest hospital. Write these down and review them after the first week.

## Safety at home

A nanny's job is easier and safer when the home is child safe: socket covers, cabinet locks for medicines and cleaning products, gates on stairs, secure windows, roofs and balconies, and no small objects within reach of babies. Make sure the nanny knows basic first aid.

## Building trust over time

In the first weeks, be at home when you can, come back unexpectedly sometimes and talk to your children about their day. If you use cameras, tell the nanny openly, and parents abroad can view them too. Over time, a good nanny earns the family's trust.

## Keeping a good nanny

Pay on time, give a weekly day off, keep duties focused on the children and treat the nanny with respect in front of the children and the wider family. Nannies stay with families who value them. Agree leave for Eid in advance and plan cover, especially if relatives are visiting at the same time.

## Travelling with the family

Some Gujranwala families take a nanny along when visiting relatives in other cities, or even abroad. Travel within Pakistan is simple to arrange, but travel abroad depends on visa rules, a valid passport and the nanny's own family situation. Discuss travel early, agree on extra pay and working hours during the trip, and make sure the nanny has her own documents and a way to contact her family.
