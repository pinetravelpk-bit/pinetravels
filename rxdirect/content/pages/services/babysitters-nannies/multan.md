## Babysitter Multan or nanny: which do you need?

A **babysitter in Multan** looks after children occasionally: an evening out, a few hours at the weekend, or during a wedding or family function. A **nanny** provides regular childcare, usually full time, and becomes part of the child's daily routine. Nannies follow routines for feeding, naps, play and learning, and often stay with a family for years. Most families who contact us need a nanny, and some also want an occasional babysitter. ## What a nanny does in a Multan home

Typical nanny duties include feeding babies and children, preparing bottles and simple meals, sterilising feeding equipment, bathing, dressing and changing nappies, settling children for naps and bedtime, playing and reading, age appropriate activities, getting older children ready for school, helping with homework, keeping the nursery and children's clothes tidy, accompanying children to the park, classes or appointments, and watching for signs of illness.

Nannies are not usually expected to do general housework. Keeping a nanny focused on the children is better for everyone. If you also need cleaning or cooking, consider a [maid in Multan](/services/maids/multan).

## Keeping children safe in Multan's heat

Multan's summer can stretch from April to October, with temperatures above 45 degrees for weeks. Babies and small children overheat much faster than adults. A good nanny keeps children indoors between late morning and late afternoon, dresses them in light cotton, offers water or milk often, keeps rooms cool and never leaves a child in a parked car, even for a minute. She watches for signs of heat exhaustion, such as unusual tiredness, flushed skin, headache or vomiting, and tells parents at once. During power cuts, she knows how to keep a baby cool with wet cloths and a battery fan. Dust storms can trigger coughs, so windows should be closed quickly when the wind picks up.

## Farm visits and travel

Many Multan families spend weekends or harvest weeks at the farm or travel to relatives in nearby districts. A nanny who travels with the family needs to know the rules for the farm: keeping children away from canals, ponds, machinery, animals and pesticides. Agree these rules in advance and make sure the nanny has proper accommodation on the farm.

## Joint families and grandparents

Many Multan homes include grandparents and extended family. Before the nanny starts, agree as a family who sets the rules on feeding, sleep and discipline, and who the nanny should ask when unsure. A nanny caught between conflicting instructions is in an unfair position.

## Setting house rules

Agree on feeding, sweets and snacks; screen time; how to handle tantrums, never smacking or shouting; who can visit and who can collect the children; where the nanny may take them; phone use, limited to breaks and never near water; and emergencies, including who to call and the nearest hospital. Write these down and review them after the first week.

## Safety at home

A nanny's job is easier and safer when the home is child safe: socket covers, cabinet locks for medicines and cleaning products, gates on stairs, secure windows, roofs and balconies, and no small objects within reach of babies. Air coolers hold water and can tip over, so keep them stable and out of reach. Make sure the nanny knows basic first aid.

## Building trust over time

In the first weeks, be at home when you can, come back unexpectedly sometimes and talk to your children about their day. If you use cameras, tell the nanny openly. Over time, a good nanny earns the family's trust, and the relationship often lasts years.

## Keeping a good nanny

Pay on time, give a weekly day off, keep duties focused on the children and treat the nanny with respect in front of the children and the wider family. Nannies stay with families who value them. Agree leave for Eid in advance and plan cover, and in summer make sure the nanny's own room is cool enough to rest properly. A nanny who stays through your child's early years gives them stability that matters.
