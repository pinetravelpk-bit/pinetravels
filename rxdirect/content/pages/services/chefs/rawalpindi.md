## Healthy cooking and special diets

Chefs are increasingly hired for health reasons: diabetic meals for a parent, heart healthy food after surgery, or balanced meal prep for busy professionals. Share any medical guidance and we will shortlist chefs experienced with special diets.

## Setting up your kitchen

Check that your kitchen has enough counter space, a good range, an oven if continental cooking is expected, quality knives and pans, adequate storage and a strong exhaust. Agree on a grocery budget and a simple purchase record. A well equipped kitchen gets the best from a skilled chef.

## Sourcing and budgets

Rawalpindi chefs often buy from Commercial Market, Saddar, the Raja Bazaar wholesale shops and specialty stores across the twin cities. A chef who knows these sources can control costs while keeping quality high. Agree on how purchases will be made and recorded, and review grocery spending together after the first month. A good chef will suggest ways to reduce waste, such as planning leftovers into the next day's menu.

## Eid lunches and family gatherings

Eid in Rawalpindi means open houses. Relatives arrive from morning until late, and many families serve lunch and dinner to thirty or forty people across the day. A chef for Eid plans the menu in advance, preps meat and marinades the night before, cooks in large quantities without losing quality, and keeps food warm and fresh as guests come in waves. Biryani, qorma, kebabs, haleem, sheer khurma and kheer are typical, often with a few continental dishes for younger guests. If you want a chef for Eid, book at least two weeks ahead, because the best event chefs are in demand on those days.

## Barbecue and outdoor dinners

Rawalpindi's mild evenings in spring and autumn, and the lawns of Bahria Town and DHA, make outdoor barbecues popular. A chef who can manage charcoal grills, marinate tikka, seekh kebabs, chops and fish properly, and coordinate with indoor cooking for naan and sides is a real asset for these evenings. ## Discretion and conduct

Chefs in senior officers' and business homes see a lot: guest lists, private conversations, family matters. We brief every chef on discretion and ask references about conduct. A chef who talks about one household's guests in the next household will not last in Rawalpindi's close knit circles, and we do not place chefs whose references raise doubts on this point.

## Working with existing household staff

Most homes that hire a chef already have a cook, maid or helper. Make clear who leads the kitchen and how duties are divided. Some families keep the existing cook for daily meals and bring in the chef for events, while others make the chef head of the kitchen with the cook as assistant. Either works if roles are clear from the start.

## Live-in chefs and accommodation

Full-time chefs in Rawalpindi are often live-in, especially in Bahria Town, DHA and the Cantt, where early breakfasts and late dinners are common. A live-in chef needs a clean, private room with a fan or cooler, bathroom access and proper meals. Agree on working hours, rest time between lunch and dinner service, and a weekly day off. Chefs who work long event days should get time off in lieu. A chef who is well rested cooks better and stays longer.

## Questions to ask in the interview

Ask the chef to describe a dinner for twelve from shopping to service, how they would adjust a menu for an elderly diabetic guest, what they would do if a dish failed shortly before guests arrived, and which kitchens they have worked in. Calm, specific answers are a good sign. Chefs who have truly run a kitchen talk about quantities, timings and suppliers without hesitation, while those who have only assisted tend to speak in general terms.
