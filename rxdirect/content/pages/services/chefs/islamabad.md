## Healthy eating and special diets

Many Islamabad households hire chefs for health reasons: a parent with diabetes, a family member recovering from heart surgery, or simply a desire to eat better. A skilled chef can cook low salt, low fat or low carb meals that still taste good. Share any doctor's or dietitian's guidance, and we will shortlist chefs experienced with special diets.

## Sourcing ingredients in Islamabad

Good chefs know where to find good ingredients, and Islamabad has more options than most Pakistani cities. Imported cheeses, herbs, olive oil and specialty items are available in F-6 and F-7 markets and online stores. Fresh fish arrives from Karachi several times a week. Organic vegetables come from farms around the Margalla Hills and Chak Shahzad. A chef familiar with these sources can produce a better menu at a lower cost than one who buys everything from a single supermarket. During interviews we ask chefs where they shop and how they plan purchases, because it tells us a lot about both quality and cost control.

## Working with a house manager or serving staff

Larger Islamabad households and diplomatic residences often have a house manager, butler or serving staff alongside the chef. The chef needs to work smoothly with them: sharing menus in advance, timing courses with the servers, briefing helpers on plating and keeping the kitchen calm during service. We look for chefs who have worked in teams before and can lead junior staff without friction. If you have a house manager, involve them in the trial so they can see how the chef works with others.

## Confidentiality in the kitchen

Chefs in diplomatic, government and corporate homes often overhear conversations, see guest lists and know the household's schedule. Discretion is essential. We brief every chef on confidentiality, and for sensitive households we ask references specifically about it. Families can also include a simple confidentiality clause in the placement agreement.

## Setting up the kitchen for a chef

Before a chef starts, check that the kitchen has enough counter space, a good range and oven, quality knives and pans, adequate fridge and freezer space, and a strong exhaust. Agree on a grocery budget and a simple purchase record. Many chefs suggest small equipment upgrades in their first weeks, which are usually worth it. A good chef will also reorganise storage so ingredients are easy to find, label containers and set up a simple stock list, which saves money by reducing waste and duplicate purchases.

## Wedding season and events

Islamabad's wedding season from November to February, and the weeks after Eid, keep chefs very busy. Book event chefs early for dholkis, mehndis and walima dinners at home. For larger events, the chef will recommend extra [helpers](/services/helpers/islamabad) for prep, serving and washing up, which we can arrange.

## Questions to ask a chef in the interview

A few questions reveal a chef's real level quickly. Ask them to walk you through a dinner for twelve, from shopping to the last plate. Ask how they would adapt a menu for a guest with a nut allergy or a vegetarian diplomat. Ask what they would do if the main course went wrong forty minutes before guests arrived. Ask which kitchens they have worked in and what each taught them. Specific, calm and practical answers are what you want to hear. Vague claims about cooking "every cuisine" usually mean the opposite.
