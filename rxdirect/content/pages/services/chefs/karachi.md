## Seafood, the Karachi speciality

Karachi is Pakistan's seafood city, and many families eat fish and prawns weekly. A Karachi chef needs to know how to choose fresh fish at the harbour or market, clean and store it safely in the heat, and cook it well in many styles: fried with masala, grilled with lemon and herbs, in a karahi or curry, or as a continental dish. Poorly handled seafood is a health risk, so we ask detailed questions about sourcing and storage. If seafood is important to you, the trial can include it, and you can judge both the cooking and how the chef cleans up after handling fish.

## Humidity, heat and food safety

Karachi's climate is hard on food. Humidity spoils flour, spices and bread quickly, and heat makes meat, dairy and seafood dangerous if left out. Good chefs store dry goods in airtight containers, refrigerate promptly, plan around load-shedding to protect the fridge, and never leave cream desserts or seafood on a buffet for long. During interviews we ask how chefs handle these Karachi conditions.

## Water, gas and the Karachi kitchen

Many Karachi homes depend on tanker water and face low gas pressure, especially in winter evenings. Experienced chefs save water carefully, keep LPG as backup and plan cooking around low pressure hours. These practical skills matter as much as recipes.

## Live-in chefs and accommodation

Given Karachi's commutes, most full-time chefs live in. A live-in chef needs a clean, ventilated room with a fan, bathroom access, proper meals and a weekly day off. Humidity makes ventilation especially important. Agree on working hours and how long event days are compensated.

## Community cuisines in Karachi homes

Karachi chefs often need to cook for more than one tradition. Memon families value khausay, kalia and their own style of biryani. Hyderabadi and Urdu speaking families expect qorma, haleem and properly layered biryani. Bohri households have their own thaal traditions and dishes. Sindhi families enjoy sai bhaji and seafood curries, while Punjabi families look for karahi and saag. A chef who respects these differences, asks the family how they like each dish and adjusts rather than imposing their own version, earns trust quickly. During the trial, ask for at least one dish from your own family's tradition.

## Apartment kitchens

Many Karachi families who hire chefs live in large apartments in Clifton, DHA and PECHS rather than bungalows. Apartment kitchens can be compact, with limited storage and ventilation, and buildings may have rules on staff entry and use of service lifts. Chefs used to hotel kitchens sometimes find this an adjustment. When you share your brief, describe the kitchen and building rules so we can shortlist chefs comfortable working in that space and, for live-out chefs, living close enough to keep to building hours.

## Healthy eating and special diets

Karachi families increasingly hire chefs for health reasons: diabetic meals, low salt diets for heart patients, or balanced meal prep. Share any medical guidance and we will shortlist chefs experienced with it.

## Sourcing and budgets

A Karachi chef who knows Empress Market, the fish harbour, Bohri Bazaar spice shops and specialty stores can deliver better food at lower cost. Agree on a grocery budget and a simple purchase record, and review spending together after the first month.

## Working with existing staff

If you already have a cook, decide whether the chef leads the kitchen or handles events. Clear roles keep everyone working together, and an existing cook who feels respected often learns a lot from a skilled chef.

## Questions to ask in the interview

Ask how they would plan an iftar for thirty, how they choose and store fresh fish, how they balance community and international dishes, and what they would do if a dish failed before guests arrived. Specific, practical answers show real experience. A chef who has truly cooked for large Karachi gatherings will talk easily about quantities per guest, what can be prepared the day before, and how to keep food safe on a long buffet in humid weather. One who has not will stay general.
