## Peshawari cuisine: what we test

During trials we test the dishes that define Peshawar's food: namkeen gosht cooked slowly with just salt, fat and patience; chapli kabab with the right crunch and spice; a rich karahi; kabuli pulao with carrots and raisins; and dumpukht for special occasions. A Peshawar chef should also know how to buy good lamb and mutton, which matters as much as the cooking.

## Hujra hospitality and large gatherings

The hujra is the heart of hospitality in many Peshawar homes. Guests arrive throughout the day, and the kitchen must respond with qehwa, snacks and meals. For weddings, jirgas and condolence gatherings, numbers can reach hundreds, cooked in degs over open fires. Event chefs with this experience coordinate helpers, manage quantities and keep food hot and fresh. Book early for wedding season and Eid. We can arrange [helpers in Peshawar](/services/helpers/peshawar).

## Chefs for organisations and guest houses

University Town and Hayatabad host NGOs, international organisations and guest houses that receive visitors from many countries. These kitchens need chefs who can cook varied international menus, respect dietary needs and work professionally with house managers. We check references from previous organisational placements carefully.

## Outdoor BBQ and tikka

Peshawar's love of tikka, chapli kabab and grilled meat makes BBQ skills valuable. Chefs who can manage charcoal, marinate properly and cook large quantities over open fire are in demand for family gatherings and farmhouse events. Good BBQ chefs also know how to keep raw meat cold until it goes on the grill, how to cook it through without drying it out, and how to time the grill so the first guests and the last guests both get hot, fresh tikka.

## Winter cooking and gas

Peshawar winters bring cold kitchens and low gas pressure. Experienced chefs plan around it with early prep, pressure cookers and LPG backup. Winter also brings dishes like paye, saag and halwa, and hot qehwa for every guest.

## Female guests and household privacy

In many Peshawar homes, meals for female guests are served separately inside the house. A chef may need to coordinate with household staff, often female, who serve inside. We brief chefs on these customs and ask families to explain their arrangements during the first week.

## Discretion and security

In Peshawar, households are especially careful about who works inside the home and what they know. A chef sees guest lists, overhears conversations in the hujra, and knows when the family is home or away. Discretion is not optional. We brief every chef on confidentiality, check addresses and references with extra care, and ask previous employers specifically about trustworthiness. For prominent households, families may also want to register the chef with local police and keep copies of all documents, which we provide at placement.

## Eid, Ramadan and the Peshawar kitchen

Eid ul Adha is especially important in Peshawar, with sacrificial meat cooked in many forms over several days: fresh kaleji on the first morning, namkeen gosht and tikka for guests, and meat distributed to relatives and the poor. A chef familiar with handling large amounts of fresh meat safely, and cooking it in traditional ways, is invaluable that week. Ramadan brings sehri, iftar with pakoras, samosas and fruit chaat, and late dinners for guests. Planning ahead and a capable helper make both seasons manageable.

## Tandoor and fresh bread

Many Peshawar homes value fresh naan with every meal, and some have a tandoor at home. A chef who can manage a tandoor, or coordinate with a nearby tandoor for fresh bread at the right moment, keeps meals authentic. Mention a home tandoor in your brief so we can check this skill.

## Healthy eating and special diets

Many elders live with diabetes or heart conditions. A chef can prepare lighter versions of favourite dishes without losing the Peshawari character. Share any medical guidance, and we will shortlist chefs who have cooked diet meals for elderly family members before.

## Sourcing and costs

Good chefs know where to buy quality meat, rice, spices and dry fruit in Peshawar's markets. Agree on a grocery budget and purchase records, especially for large gatherings where costs add up.

## Live-in chefs and accommodation

Full-time chefs in Peshawar are often live-in. Provide a clean room with heating in winter and a fan in summer, bathroom access, proper meals and a weekly day off. Agree on how long event days are compensated, and allow time off after big weddings or Eid.

## Questions to ask in the interview

Ask how they would cook for a hundred at a wedding, how they choose lamb for namkeen gosht, how they would adapt a menu for foreign guests, and what they would do if a dish failed before guests arrived. Listen for practical detail about quantities, deg sizes, timings and the number of helpers needed. Chefs who have truly managed large Peshawar gatherings answer these questions easily and specifically.

## Working with existing household staff

Most Peshawar homes that hire a chef already have a cook and other staff. Decide who leads the kitchen. Some families keep the existing cook for daily meals inside the house while the chef handles the hujra and events. Others make the chef head of the kitchen. Explain the arrangement clearly to both, and make sure the existing cook feels respected. In households where female staff serve inside and male staff serve the hujra, the chef must coordinate smoothly with both.
