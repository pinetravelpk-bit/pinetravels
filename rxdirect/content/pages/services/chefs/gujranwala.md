## When relatives come home from abroad

This is a uniquely Gujranwala need. Families with members in Europe, the UK and the Gulf see the house fill up for a few weeks around Eid, weddings or summer holidays. Daily cooking jumps from four people to twenty, and every evening seems to bring a dawat. A short term chef for these weeks takes the pressure off the household and the regular cook. Tell us the arrival and departure dates early, as these periods are busy for chefs. Many families plan a welcome dinner, a few dawats for relatives, a BBQ night and daily meals for the full house. A chef who knows this rhythm will plan menus for the whole visit, keep the freezer stocked with marinated meat and kebabs, and make sure no two dinners feel the same.

## Gujranwala's grill and BBQ tradition

Gujranwala's reputation rests heavily on its grilled and fried meats: chicken tikka, chargha, seekh kebab, mutton chops and fish. A chef who can marinate properly, manage charcoal and cook large batches evenly is prized. For lawn BBQ evenings, the chef also coordinates naan, salads, chutneys and raita so everything arrives together.

## Weddings at home

Gujranwala weddings often include functions at home: dholki nights, mehndi and lunches for relatives. Event chefs with deg and catering experience manage large quantities with helpers. Book early in wedding season, which overlaps with the months many overseas relatives return. We can arrange [helpers in Gujranwala](/services/helpers/gujranwala).

## Business families and clients

Gujranwala's industrialists host clients and partners at home. A chef for these dinners needs to present a polished meal and adjust for guests who may not be used to Gujranwala's spice levels. Discretion matters too, and we ask references about it. In a city where business circles are close, a chef who keeps the household's affairs private is essential.

## Eid ul Adha and fresh meat

Eid ul Adha is a major event in Gujranwala households, with sacrificial animals, large amounts of fresh meat and guests over several days. A chef who handles fresh meat safely, knows the traditional first day dishes like kaleji and tikka, and can turn the rest into karahi, qorma, kebabs and nihari is invaluable that week. Freezing and portioning meat for later is another useful skill. If you want your chef working over Eid, agree on it well in advance, since many chefs go home for the holiday.

## Ramadan iftars for the extended family

During Ramadan, many Gujranwala families host iftar for relatives and neighbours, sometimes nightly. A chef plans iftar spreads with pakoras, samosas, chicken rolls, fruit chaat and dahi baray, ready on time at Maghrib, followed by dinner. With planning and a helper, a chef makes the month's hospitality manageable without exhausting the family.

## Chefs for parents with visiting children

Some families hire a chef specifically so that ageing parents can host their children and grandchildren properly when they visit from abroad. The chef takes over the kitchen for the visit, plans menus that please both the parents and the younger generation raised abroad, and leaves the regular cook in place for daily meals afterwards. It is a thoughtful way to make reunions special without straining the parents.

## Healthy eating and special diets

Gujranwala's rich food is part of its charm, but many elders live with diabetes and heart disease. A chef can prepare lighter versions of favourite dishes and separate diet meals for those who need them. Share any medical guidance, and we will shortlist chefs who have cooked for elderly family members with similar needs.

## Summer, fog and food safety

Summers are hot and humid, making food safety essential, especially for grilled meat and dairy desserts at gatherings. Winter brings fog that can delay deliveries, so chefs plan purchases ahead. A strong kitchen exhaust is important for grills and frying.

## Sourcing and costs

Good chefs know Gujranwala's butchers and markets well, buy quality meat at fair prices and plan menus to reduce waste. Agree on a grocery budget and purchase records, especially during busy visiting seasons when spending rises.

## Live-in chefs and accommodation

Full-time chefs are often live-in. Provide a clean, ventilated room, bathroom access, proper meals and a weekly day off. Agree on compensation for long event days.

## Working with existing staff

If you have a cook, decide whether the chef leads the kitchen or handles events and visiting seasons. Clear roles prevent friction. In many Gujranwala homes the long serving cook knows the parents' tastes and health needs better than anyone, so a sensible arrangement is for the cook to keep handling daily meals while the chef takes charge of dawats, BBQ evenings and the busy weeks when relatives are home. Introduce them properly, explain the plan to both, and the two often learn from each other.

## Questions to ask in the interview

Ask how they would cook for forty at a welcome dinner, how they marinate chargha and tikka, how they keep grilled food hot for late guests, and what they would do if a dish failed before guests arrived. Chefs with real experience answer with numbers and timings, not general promises.
