## Home painter Pakistan: preparation is everything

A good home painter in Pakistan spends more time preparing than painting. Proper preparation includes:

- Moving or covering furniture with plastic sheets
- Covering floors, switches and fittings
- Scraping off loose and flaking paint
- Filling cracks and holes and sanding smooth
- Applying putty to uneven walls and sanding again
- Treating damp areas and allowing them to dry
- Applying primer on new plaster or repaired areas

Skipping these steps is why so many paint jobs fail early. Ask the painter what preparation is included in the quote.

## Choosing the right paint

There are many paint types in the Pakistani market. A quick guide:

**Emulsion (plastic paint).** Water based, smooth finish, easy to clean. The standard choice for interior walls. Matt finishes hide imperfections; silk and semi-gloss finishes are easier to wipe.

**Distemper.** Cheaper, chalky finish, less durable and not washable. Suitable for budget work or areas that are rarely touched.

**Weather shield.** Designed for exterior walls, resists rain, sun and fungus. Worth the higher cost for outside surfaces.

**Enamel.** Oil based, hard and glossy. Used on doors, windows, grills and metal. Takes longer to dry and has a strong smell.

**Waterproofing coats.** Applied before paint on roofs, exterior walls and areas prone to seepage.

Ask the painter to recommend paint for each area, and buy from authorised dealers to avoid counterfeit products.

## Wall painter Pakistan: damp and seepage

The biggest enemy of paint in Pakistan is damp. A wall painter in Pakistan must understand where damp comes from:

- **Rising damp** from the ground in walls without a proper damp proof course, common in older houses
- **Seepage** from bathrooms, kitchens or leaking pipes inside walls
- **Roof leaks** during monsoon
- **Condensation** in poorly ventilated rooms

Painting over damp only hides it for a few weeks. The source must be fixed first, sometimes with the help of a [plumber](/services/plumbers), then the wall treated and dried before painting.

## Best time to paint

Weather affects paint quality. In Pakistan, the best months for painting are usually October to March, when it is dry and not too hot. Painting during monsoon means walls take longer to dry and damp gets trapped. Painting in peak summer heat can make paint dry too fast and leave marks. Exterior painting especially should avoid the rainy season.

Many families paint before Eid or weddings, which creates a rush. Book early to get your preferred dates.

## Painting with family at home

Painting while living in the house is common. To make it easier, paint one or two rooms at a time, keep windows open for ventilation, use low odour paints in bedrooms, keep children and elderly family members away from fumes, and agree daily working hours with the painter. Enamel paints have a strong smell, so plan those for when rooms can be left empty for a day.

## Choosing colours that work

Colour choice is personal, but a few practical points help. Light colours make small rooms feel bigger and reflect heat, which matters in hot cities like Multan, Lahore and Karachi. Warm whites, soft beige and pale grey hide dust better than pure white. Darker colours work well for feature walls but show scuffs and fade faster in strong sunlight. For children's rooms and corridors, washable finishes are worth the extra cost. Always paint a small test patch and look at it in daylight and at night before buying paint for the whole room, because colours look very different on a wall than on a shade card.

## How much paint do you need?

Families often buy too much or too little paint. As a rough guide, one litre of emulsion covers around 10 to 12 square metres per coat on a smooth, primed wall, less on rough or porous surfaces. Most walls need two coats, and a colour change from dark to light may need three. Your painter will calculate quantities based on the actual walls. Buying all paint from the same batch avoids slight colour differences between tins.

## Exterior painting and safety

Exterior walls on double and triple storey houses need ladders, scaffolding or a cradle. Safety is essential. Scaffolding must be stable and tied in, ladders should be footed by a second person and painters should not work at height in strong wind or rain. Exterior paint also needs a dry surface, so the walls should be checked for moisture after rain. A good painter will plan exterior work for a dry spell and will not rush it.

## Warning signs of a poor paint job

After painting, check for these problems: patchy coverage where the old colour shows through, drips and runs on walls and doors, rough walls where putty was not sanded, paint on switches, sockets and floors, and bubbles or peeling within a few weeks. Most of these come from rushed preparation or skipping coats. If you notice any of them on our work, tell us and we will send the painter back to put it right.
