## Questions worth asking at the interview

Practical questions reveal real experience quickly. Ask how they press a uniform shirt or a starched kameez, how they keep track of several tablets taken at different times, what they would do if a guest arrived while their employer was resting, and how they handled private matters in previous jobs. ## Why attendants leave, and how to keep them

Attendants usually leave when they are on call day and night with no rest, when other staff treat them as a general servant for the whole house, or when they are blamed for things outside their control. Protect the role: the attendant serves one person, has fixed rest hours and a weekly day off, and any complaint is handled privately and calmly. A small yearly increase and recognition at Eid show that loyalty is noticed.

## Uniform and clothing care

For clients who wear uniforms or formal clothes every day, clothing care is the heart of the job. A skilled attendant presses creases correctly, starches collars and kameez where the client likes it, removes stains without damaging fabric, stores woollens with moth protection and keeps medals, badges, cufflinks and watches in order. Show the attendant how you like things done in the first week, and a good one will keep to your standard without reminders.

## Medicines and appointments

Many clients take several medicines at different times. The attendant keeps a weekly pill box filled by the family or doctor, gives each dose on time, ticks it on a chart, reorders before stock runs out and keeps a diary of doctor visits and tests. They notice small changes, such as poor sleep, low appetite or new confusion, and tell the family early.
