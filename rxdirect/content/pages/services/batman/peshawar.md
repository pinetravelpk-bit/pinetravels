## The hujra and guests

In Peshawar, serving guests well is a matter of family honour. The attendant greets visitors respectfully, serves qehwa, tea and dry fruit promptly, brings meals at the right time, makes sure elders are served first and keeps the hujra clean. They never discuss who visited or what was said. We brief attendants on these customs and look for references that mention good conduct with guests.

## Ex-army and ex-FC attendants

Khyber Pakhtunkhwa has a long tradition of service in the army, Frontier Corps and police. Attendants with this background bring punctuality, cleanliness, routine and loyalty. We check service records to confirm backgrounds.

## Discretion and privacy

An attendant who serves an elder involved in jirgas, business or politics hears sensitive matters. Discretion is essential, and in Peshawar it is a question of trust and honour. References are asked specifically about discretion.

## Winter routines

Peshawar winters are cold. The attendant airs woollen clothes and shawls, keeps the client's room warm with safe heating, never leaves a gas heater burning in a closed room at night and keeps the hujra comfortable for guests.

## Attendants for elderly parents

When the person served is an elderly parent, the attendant becomes a blend of companion, organiser and helper, keeping the day structured and the family, including sons abroad, informed.

## Questions worth asking at the interview

Practical questions reveal real experience quickly. Ask how they press a uniform shirt or a starched kameez, how they keep track of several tablets taken at different times, what they would do if a guest arrived while their employer was resting, and how they handled private matters in previous jobs. Watch how the candidate arrives: neatly dressed, on time and with documents in order usually means the same care on the job.

## Why attendants leave, and how to keep them

Attendants usually leave when they are on call day and night with no rest, when other staff treat them as a general servant for the whole house, or when they are blamed for things outside their control. Protect the role: the attendant serves one person, has fixed rest hours and a weekly day off, and any complaint is handled privately and calmly. A small yearly increase and recognition at Eid show that loyalty is noticed.

## Uniform and clothing care

For clients who wear uniforms or formal clothes every day, clothing care is the heart of the job. A skilled attendant presses creases correctly, starches collars and kameez where the client likes it, removes stains without damaging fabric, stores woollens with moth protection and keeps medals, badges, cufflinks and watches in order. Show the attendant how you like things done in the first week, and a good one will keep to your standard without reminders.

## Medicines and appointments

Many clients take several medicines at different times. The attendant keeps a weekly pill box filled by the family or doctor, gives each dose on time, ticks it on a chart, reorders before stock runs out and keeps a diary of doctor visits and tests. They notice small changes, such as poor sleep, low appetite or new confusion, and tell the family early.

## Household standards and presentation

A household attendant represents the family to every guest. Good attendants are neatly dressed, speak politely, serve quietly and know when to step back. Many families provide a simple uniform, such as a clean shalwar kameez in a set colour, or white for formal occasions. Agree on standards for serving guests, answering the door and handling phone calls in the first week.

## Daily routine example

A typical day for a live-in attendant serving an elder in Hayatabad might be: tea and qehwa after Fajr, clothes and waistcoat laid out, medicines with breakfast, the room cleaned while the elder sits in the hujra, guests served through the morning, lunch for visitors, afternoon rest, evening guests and tea, clothes prepared for the next day and the medicine box checked before night.

## Travel

Clients often travel to Islamabad, their villages or abroad for Umrah. The attendant packs with a checklist, carries medicines and documents and looks after the client throughout the journey.

## Respect works both ways

Agree on working hours, rest times and a weekly day off. Make it clear the attendant serves one person, not the whole household. Pay on time and speak respectfully. Peshawar families value loyalty, and an attendant who feels respected often stays for decades.
