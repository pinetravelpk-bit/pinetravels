## Home security guard Peshawar: the job at a residence

A home security guard in Peshawar usually controls the main gate and checks every visitor, keeps a visitor register with names, times and purpose, receives deliveries and checks them before they enter, opens the gate for family cars and stops unfamiliar vehicles, patrols the boundary at night, switches outdoor lights on and off, checks locks and calls the family or police if anything is suspicious. In homes with a hujra, the guard welcomes genuine guests respectfully, shows them in and informs the family.

Guards in Peshawar also watch the street: unfamiliar vehicles parked nearby, people asking questions about the family or someone watching the house. A good guard notices quietly and tells the family at once.

## The hujra and guest arrivals

In many Peshawar homes, guests arrive at the hujra throughout the day, sometimes without notice. The guard must welcome genuine guests respectfully, according to local custom, while making sure strangers are not let in simply because they claim to know an elder of the family. A guard who knows the regular visitors by sight, calls the family before letting in anyone new and keeps the register up to date strikes this balance well. Guards should never discuss who visits the hujra with anyone outside the household.

## Police registration in Peshawar

KP Police ask residents to register domestic staff, including guards, at the local police station or through their online registration system. Registration needs the guard's CNIC, photographs, address and your details. We provide the documents we have collected and guide you through the process.

## Planning cover for your home

One guard on a 12 hour night shift suits some houses, but many Peshawar families prefer round the clock cover, which means at least two guards on alternating shifts, plus relief cover for weekly days off. Large houses, corner plots and homes with a busy hujra may need a third guard. CCTV, good lighting, a working intercom and strong gates make every guard more effective.

## Winter nights and summer heat

Peshawar winters are cold, and night guards need a proper shelter, a safe heater, warm clothing, a strong torch and a charged phone. Guards should never use a gas heater in a closed cabin without ventilation. Summers are hot and dusty, and day guards need shade, a fan, cold water and short breaks.

## Weddings, jirgas and gatherings

Peshawar weddings, jirgas and condolence gatherings can draw large numbers of guests and vehicles. Gate control becomes harder and more important. Extra guards for these days, a clear rule about where vehicles park and a family member at the gate to identify guests keep the event safe.

## Guards and household staff

In homes with a cook, maid, driver and helper, the guard notes who comes and goes, makes sure staff do not bring unknown people inside and watches for anything carried out of the house. Agree in advance who the guard reports concerns to.

## Keeping a good guard

Pay on time, provide a proper shelter, meals or a meal allowance as agreed, a weekly day off with relief cover and respect. Peshawar families value loyalty, and a guard who is respected and well treated often stays for many years.

## Leave, Eid and relief cover

Many guards in Peshawar come from Charsadda, Mardan, Swat, Dir and the tribal districts, and they travel home for Eid, family weddings and funerals. Mountain roads and weather can delay their return. Agree leave dates in advance, arrange a relief guard through us for those days and keep in touch while the guard is away. A house should never be left without cover simply because the regular guard has gone home, especially during Eid when many families are visiting relatives and streets are quieter than usual.
