## Home security guard Karachi: the job at a residence

A home security guard in Karachi usually controls the main gate and checks every visitor, keeps a visitor register with names, times and purpose, receives deliveries and checks them before they enter, opens the gate for family cars and stops unfamiliar vehicles, patrols the boundary at night, switches outdoor lights on and off, checks locks and calls the family or police if anything is suspicious. In homes with several staff, the guard notes when staff arrive and leave.

Guards in Karachi also watch the street outside: motorcycles slowing down near the gate, people loitering near parked cars, or someone photographing the house. A good guard notices and tells the family.

Sindh Police ask residents to register domestic staff, including guards, at the local police station or through their online registration. Registration needs the guard's CNIC, photographs, address and your details. We provide the documents we have collected and guide you through the process.

## Planning cover for your home

One guard on a 12 hour night shift suits many houses. Bungalows in DHA and Clifton, corner plots and homes on main roads often need round the clock cover, which means at least two guards on alternating shifts, plus relief cover for weekly days off. Flats usually rely on building security, but some families hire a guard for the building entrance jointly with neighbours. CCTV, good lighting, a working intercom and secure gates make every guard more effective.

## Heat, humidity and the monsoon

Karachi is hot and humid for most of the year, and summer heatwaves can be dangerous. Day guards need shade, a fan, cold water and short breaks, and the gate shelter should be ventilated. During monsoon, many roads flood and power cuts are common. Guards should watch for water entering the premises, keep the generator area dry, move cars if needed and report damage.

## Driver and cash movement

Many Karachi families worry most when leaving or arriving home, since this is when snatchings and robberies often happen. A guard who opens the gate quickly, watches the street while the car enters and closes it straight away reduces the risk. Agree a routine with your guard and driver, and never discuss cash movements or travel plans in front of strangers.

## Guards and household staff

In homes with a cook, maid, driver and gardener, the guard notes who comes and goes, makes sure staff do not bring unknown people inside and watches for anything carried out of the house. Agree in advance whether bags are checked and who the guard reports concerns to, so this happens politely and without friction.

## Training and conduct at the gate

A good guard is polite to genuine visitors and firm with everyone else. They ask for names, call the family before opening the gate, never let a stranger in because someone claims to be from a utility company or courier, check packages and refuse to leave the gate unattended. We brief guards on these basics, and you should repeat your own rules in the first week.

## Keeping a good guard

Pay on time, provide a proper shelter, meals or a meal allowance as agreed, a weekly day off with relief cover and respect. Help with transport on strike days if you can. Guards who are treated with dignity stay longer and stay alert.

## Building and street watch arrangements

In some Karachi neighbourhoods, several houses on the same lane share the cost of a street guard, or a block of townhouses hires guards for a shared gate. These arrangements work well when one person is responsible for paying the guard, giving instructions and arranging relief cover. Agree in writing which houses the guard watches, what they check and whom they call in an emergency. We can place guards for shared arrangements as long as there is a clear point of contact for the placement.
