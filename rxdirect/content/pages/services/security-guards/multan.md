## Home security guard Multan: the job at a residence

A home security guard in Multan usually controls the main gate and checks every visitor, keeps a visitor register with names, times and purpose, receives deliveries and checks them before they enter, opens the gate for family cars and stops unfamiliar vehicles, patrols the boundary at night, switches outdoor lights on and off, checks locks and calls the family or police if anything is suspicious. In homes with several staff, the guard notes when staff arrive and leave.

Some families ask guards to water plants or wash the car in the early morning. Small extra tasks are fine if agreed, but the guard's first job is security, and they should never leave the gate unattended.

## Farmhouses and orchards

Many Multan families own farmhouses and mango orchards outside the city. These properties are large, often far from neighbours and sometimes visited only at weekends. A live-in guard watches the boundary, controls who enters, keeps an eye on equipment and stored produce, and reports problems to the family. In mango season, when fruit is valuable and labour comes and goes daily, an alert guard matters most. Farmhouse guards often work alongside a caretaker or gardener, and the arrangement should be clear about who is in charge when the family is away.

Punjab Police ask residents to register domestic staff, including guards, at the local police station or through the Punjab Police tenant and servant registration system. Registration needs the guard's CNIC, photographs, address and your details. We provide the documents we have collected and guide you through the process.

## Planning cover for your home

One guard on a 12 hour night shift suits most houses. Larger houses, corner plots and homes left empty while the family is at the farm or abroad may need round the clock cover, which means at least two guards on alternating shifts, plus relief cover for weekly days off. CCTV, good lighting and a working intercom make every guard more effective.

## Heat, dust storms and night shifts

Multan's summer is among the hottest in Pakistan, with temperatures above 45 degrees for weeks. Day guards need proper shade, a fan or air cooler, cold water and short breaks, or they simply cannot stay alert. Many families switch to a night-heavy arrangement in summer. Dust storms can cut power and visibility suddenly, so guards should have a strong torch, a charged phone and know where the generator or UPS is. In winter, fog reduces visibility at night, and guards need warm clothing and a safe heater.

## Weddings and big gatherings

Multan weddings bring caterers, decorators, guests and their drivers to the house over several days. An extra guard for event days, a guest list at the gate and a clear rule about where drivers wait help keep valuables safe.

## Guards and household staff

In homes with a cook, maid, driver and gardener, the guard notes who comes and goes, makes sure staff do not bring unknown people inside and watches for anything carried out of the house. Agree in advance whether bags are checked and who the guard reports concerns to.

## Training and conduct at the gate

A good guard is polite to genuine visitors and firm with everyone else. They ask for names, call the family before opening the gate, never let a stranger in because someone claims to know the family, check packages and refuse to leave the gate unattended. We brief guards on these basics, and you should repeat your own rules in the first week.

## Keeping a good guard

Pay on time, provide a proper shelter, meals or a meal allowance as agreed, a weekly day off with relief cover and respect. Guards who are treated with dignity stay longer and stay alert. Many guards in Multan come from villages in the surrounding districts and travel home for Eid, harvest and family weddings, so agree leave dates in advance and arrange a relief guard for those days. A guard who knows the family will plan around their needs is far less likely to leave suddenly.
