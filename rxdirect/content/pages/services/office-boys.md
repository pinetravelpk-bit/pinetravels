## Office helper Pakistan: typical duties

An office helper in Pakistan usually handles:

- Opening the office, switching on lights and air conditioning, and preparing desks
- Making and serving tea, coffee and water for staff and visitors

## Office support staff Pakistan: setting up the role

Office support staff in Pakistan perform best when the role is clearly defined. A few practical steps:

- **Write down the daily routine:** opening time, tea times, bank runs, courier pickups and closing tasks.
- **Fix an errand schedule:** for example, bank and courier runs once in the morning and once after lunch, rather than constant trips.
- **Keep a petty cash register:** every rupee given and spent, with receipts.
- **Decide who gives instructions:** usually an office manager, so the office boy is not pulled in five directions.
- **Provide a uniform** if you expect one, and a place to keep personal belongings.

## Pantry boy Pakistan: for larger offices

In larger offices, a dedicated pantry boy in Pakistan focuses on refreshments: tea and coffee rounds, water, snacks for meetings, cleaning cups and keeping the pantry hygienic and stocked. Separating pantry duties from errands means meetings are always served on time and nobody waits for tea because the office boy is out at the bank. Many corporate floors have one pantry boy and one office boy for errands.

## Handling confidential documents

Law firms, accountants, clinics and HR departments handle documents that must stay private. Office boys in these settings should understand that files are not to be read, copied for anyone else or discussed outside. Brief them clearly, keep sensitive files locked, and use sealed envelopes for deliveries. During interviews for these roles, we look for candidates whose references mention discretion.

## Banking and cash safety

Sending an office boy to the bank with cash carries risk, both for the business and for the office boy. Reduce it by using bank transfers wherever possible, limiting cash amounts, varying the timing of bank visits, and having the office boy call when the deposit is done. Never ask an office boy to carry large sums alone in busy areas. A careful routine protects everyone.

## Office boy or cleaner: which does your office need?

Small offices of five to ten people often manage with one office boy who also keeps the place clean. As an office grows, combining both jobs stops working. The office boy is out at the bank when the washroom needs attention, or cleaning the floor when a client arrives and nobody serves tea. For offices of more than fifteen people, or any office where clients visit regularly, a separate [cleaner](/services/cleaners) usually pays for itself in a smoother day and a better impression.

Clinics and food businesses need extra hygiene, so a dedicated cleaner is important there regardless of size.

## Presentation and manners

An office boy is often the first person a visitor meets. A clean uniform, a polite greeting and quick service set the tone for the whole meeting. During interviews we look at how candidates present themselves and speak. After placement, a short briefing from the office manager on how to greet visitors, how to serve tea in meetings and when not to interrupt helps the office boy fit in quickly. Many businesses provide two sets of uniform so one can be washed while the other is worn.

## Office hours, overtime and late meetings

Offices sometimes run late, especially at month end, before audits or during project deadlines. Agree in advance how late the office boy may be asked to stay, how overtime will be paid, and how he will get home if public transport has stopped. An office boy who is asked to stay late without notice or compensation will soon start looking elsewhere. Clear rules keep both sides comfortable.

## Growing with your office boy

A good office boy often becomes a long term part of the team. Many learn basic computer skills, data entry or reception work over time. Businesses that encourage this, and reward it with small increases, often find they have a reliable, multi-skilled support person who knows how everything in the office works.

## Common reasons office boys leave

Office boys usually leave when errands require travel at their own cost, when they are on call long after office hours without overtime, when salaries are delayed, or when they are treated with disrespect by staff. A transport allowance, fair hours and basic courtesy go a long way.

## Questions to ask in the interview

A short interview tells you a lot. Ask the candidate to describe a normal day in his last office, how he kept track of money for errands, what he would do if he lost a document on the way to the bank, and how far he lives from your office. Ask whether he can ride a motorbike if your errands need one. Clear, honest answers matter more than polished ones. If you have a current office boy who is leaving, ask him to spend a day showing the new person the routine, the nearby bank branch, the courier office and the shops you use for supplies. That handover saves weeks of small mistakes.
