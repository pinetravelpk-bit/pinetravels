## Babysitter Pakistan vs nanny: which do you need?

A **babysitter in Pakistan** looks after children occasionally: evenings when parents go out, a few hours on weekends, or during a family event. Babysitters keep children safe, fed and entertained for a short time.

A **nanny** provides regular childcare, usually full time, and becomes part of the child's daily life. Nannies follow routines for feeding, naps, play and learning, and often stay with a family for years.

Most families who contact us need a nanny. Some also want an occasional baby sitter for evenings. We can arrange both.

## What a nanny does

Typical nanny duties include:

- Feeding babies and children, including preparing bottles and simple meals
- Sterilising bottles and keeping feeding equipment clean
- Bathing, dressing and changing nappies
- Settling children for naps and bedtime
- Playing, reading, and age appropriate activities
- Getting older children ready for school and helping with homework
- Keeping the nursery and children's clothes tidy
- Accompanying children to the park, classes or appointments
- Watching for signs of illness and telling parents promptly

Nannies are not usually expected to do general housework. Keeping a nanny focused on children is better for the children and the nanny.

## Childcare staff Pakistan: setting house rules

Childcare staff in Pakistan work best when parents set clear rules. Agree on:

- Feeding: what, when and how much; sweets and snacks
- Screen time: how much, which shows or apps
- Discipline: how to handle tantrums; never smacking or shouting
- Visitors: who can visit and who can collect the children
- Outings: where the nanny may take the children
- Phone use: limited to breaks, never while bathing children or near water
- Emergencies: who to call, where the first aid kit is, nearest hospital

Write these down and review them after the first week.

## Safety at home

A nanny's job is easier and safer when the home is child safe: socket covers, cabinet locks for medicines and cleaning chemicals, gates on stairs, secure windows and balconies, and no small objects within reach of babies. Ask the nanny to point out risks she notices. Make sure she knows basic first aid for choking, burns and falls, and keep emergency numbers on the fridge.

## Building trust over time

Trust is built gradually. In the first weeks, work from home when you can, come back unexpectedly sometimes, and talk to your children about their day. Many parents use cameras in living areas; if you do, tell the nanny openly. Over time, a good nanny earns the family's trust, and the relationship often lasts years.

## Warning signs during the trial

The trial day is your best chance to see how a nanny really behaves with children. Positive signs include getting down to the child's level, speaking softly, following your routines without being reminded, washing hands before feeding, and keeping an eye on the child even while doing something else. Warning signs include using the phone while the child plays near stairs or water, raising her voice quickly, forcing food, ignoring crying, or speaking negatively about previous families. Trust your instincts. If something feels wrong, tell us, and we will arrange another candidate without pressure.

## Nannies and grandparents

In many Pakistani homes, grandparents are closely involved in raising children. That is a blessing, but it can create confusion for a nanny when parents and grandparents give different instructions. Before the nanny starts, agree as a family who sets the rules on feeding, sleep and discipline, and who the nanny should ask when unsure. A nanny caught between two sets of instructions is put in an unfair position, and the child gets mixed messages. A short family conversation at the start prevents most of these problems.

## Children with special needs

Some families need a nanny for a child with autism, cerebral palsy, Down syndrome, ADHD or a medical condition. These placements need extra patience and, ideally, prior experience. Share your child's needs, therapy routines and any guidance from doctors or therapists. We will look for nannies who have worked with similar children, and we suggest a longer trial so the child can adjust. For children who need medical care such as tube feeding or medicines by injection, a [home nurse](/services/nurses) may be needed alongside the nanny.

## Respecting your nanny

Nannies stay longer when they are paid on time, given proper rest and meals, allowed their weekly day off, and spoken to respectfully, especially in front of the children. Children learn from how adults treat each other. A nanny who feels valued gives more warmth and attention to your child.

## When the nanny goes on leave

Children get attached to their nanny, and her leave for Eid or a family wedding can unsettle them. Plan leave in advance, tell older children when she will be away and when she will return, and arrange cover from a family member or a temporary babysitter the children have met before. When a nanny leaves for good, give children time to say goodbye. Sudden disappearances are hard for young children to understand.
