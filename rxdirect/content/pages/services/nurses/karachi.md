## Private nurse Karachi: what a home nurse does

A private nurse in Karachi can give injections, including insulin and blood thinners; manage IV drips and cannulas where the doctor has prescribed them; dress wounds and surgical incisions; care for urinary catheters, colostomy bags, NG and PEG feeding tubes and tracheostomies; monitor blood pressure, pulse, temperature, sugar and oxygen levels; give medicines on schedule and keep a medicine chart; turn and position bedbound patients to prevent bedsores; assist with prescribed exercises; recognise warning signs and call the doctor or arrange hospital transfer; and keep a daily record for the family and the doctor.

A home nurse works under the instructions of the patient's treating doctor. They do not change medicines or diagnose new problems on their own, and a good nurse knows when to call for help.

A patient care nurse in Karachi is most often needed for **recovery after surgery**, such as cardiac surgery at NICVD or a private hospital, joint replacement, spinal surgery or major abdominal operations. **Stroke patients** need positioning, feeding support, sometimes NG tube care, and help with exercises. **Elderly patients with several conditions**, such as diabetes, heart disease and kidney problems, need careful medicine management and monitoring. **Patients with cancer** may need care during treatment or palliative care at home. **Bedbound patients** need turning, skin care and prevention of bedsores. **Patients on oxygen, tracheostomy or home ventilation** need nurses with ICU experience.

## Nurse or caretaker?

If the patient needs medical procedures, such as injections, IV lines, dressings, catheter or tube care, you need a nurse. If they mainly need help with walking, bathing, meals and tablets on time, a [caretaker in Karachi](/services/caretakers/karachi) is usually enough and costs less. Some families start with a nurse after discharge and move to a caretaker once the patient is stable.

## Working with the patient's doctor

The nurse should have the doctor's written instructions, the medicine chart and a contact number for the doctor or ward. Agree how often the nurse should update the doctor and family, and which warning signs mean an immediate call or an ambulance. Save the numbers of the nearest emergency department and an ambulance service such as Edhi, Chhipa or Aman, and keep reports and prescriptions in one folder.

## Daily records and family updates

A good home nurse keeps a daily chart: vital signs, medicines given and when, food and fluids, output, wound condition and anything unusual. Families at work or abroad can ask for a short WhatsApp update after each shift.

## Heat, humidity and power cuts

Karachi's heat and humidity are hard on elderly and bedbound patients. Heatwaves can be dangerous, and load shedding can stop fans, air conditioners, oxygen concentrators and air mattresses. A home nurse tracks fluids carefully, keeps the patient cool, watches for signs of heatstroke and dehydration, and knows what to do when the power goes. Plan a UPS or generator backup for essential equipment.

## Patients in flats

Many Karachi patients live in flats, sometimes on upper floors. Lifts stop during load shedding, which can make hospital trips difficult. Plan ahead with the building management, keep a folding wheelchair or carry chair, and make sure the nurse knows the quickest route to the nearest hospital.

## Infection control

A home nurse should wash hands before and after every procedure, use gloves, dispose of needles in a sharps container, keep supplies clean and teach the family simple hygiene steps. Humidity makes wound and skin care especially important.

## Night duty

Many families need a nurse most at night, when patients are restless, confused or at higher risk of falls and breathing problems. A night nurse should stay awake, check the patient regularly and keep a record.

## Keeping a good nurse

Pay on time, provide meals during long shifts, give the nurse a place to rest during breaks and treat them as a professional. Help with transport on strike days or heavy rain if you can. A nurse who feels respected stays longer and gives better care.
