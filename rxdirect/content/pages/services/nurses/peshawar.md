## Private nurse Peshawar: what a home nurse does

A private nurse in Peshawar can give injections, including insulin and blood thinners; manage IV drips and cannulas where the doctor has prescribed them; dress wounds and surgical incisions; care for urinary catheters, colostomy bags, NG and PEG feeding tubes and tracheostomies; monitor blood pressure, pulse, temperature, sugar and oxygen levels; give medicines on schedule and keep a medicine chart; turn and position bedbound patients to prevent bedsores; assist with prescribed exercises; recognise warning signs and call the doctor or arrange hospital transfer; and keep a daily record for the family and the doctor.

A home nurse works under the instructions of the patient's treating doctor. They do not change medicines or diagnose new problems on their own, and a good nurse knows when to call for help.

## Nurse or caretaker?

If the patient needs medical procedures, you need a nurse. If they mainly need help with walking, bathing, meals and tablets on time, a [caretaker in Peshawar](/services/caretakers/peshawar) is usually enough and costs less. Some families start with a nurse after discharge and move to a caretaker once the patient is stable.

## Visitors and rest

In Peshawar, relatives and neighbours visit a sick person often, as a sign of respect. A home nurse can help manage visiting times politely, make sure the patient gets enough rest and keep visitors with coughs and colds away from patients with low immunity.

## Working with the patient's doctor

The nurse should have the doctor's written instructions, the medicine chart and a contact number for the doctor or ward. Agree which warning signs mean an immediate call or a trip to the emergency department, and keep reports and prescriptions in one folder.

## Daily records and family updates

A good home nurse keeps a daily chart: vital signs, medicines given and when, food and fluids, output, wound condition and anything unusual. Sons and daughters abroad can ask for a short WhatsApp update after each shift.

## Winter and summer care

Peshawar winters are cold, and elderly patients are at risk of chest infections. Gas heaters must never be left burning in a closed room with a sleeping patient, and the nurse should keep the room ventilated. In summer, heat and power cuts put bedbound patients at risk of dehydration, so the nurse tracks fluids closely.

## Equipment at home

Depending on the condition, the patient may need a hospital bed, an air mattress, a wheelchair, a commode, a blood pressure monitor, a glucometer, a pulse oximeter, an oxygen concentrator or a suction machine. Medical suppliers near the major hospitals rent equipment. Plan a UPS or generator backup.

## Infection control

A home nurse should wash hands before and after every procedure, use gloves, dispose of needles in a sharps container and keep supplies clean.

## Night duty

Many families need a nurse most at night, when patients are restless or at higher risk of falls and breathing problems. A night nurse should stay awake, check the patient regularly and keep a record.

## Keeping a good nurse

Pay on time, provide meals during long shifts, give the nurse a private place to rest and treat them as a professional. Peshawar families value loyalty, and a nurse who feels respected stays longer and gives better care.
