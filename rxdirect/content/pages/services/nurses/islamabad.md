## Private nurse Islamabad: what a home nurse does

A private nurse in Islamabad can give injections, including insulin and blood thinners; manage IV drips and cannulas where the doctor has prescribed them; dress wounds and surgical incisions; care for urinary catheters, colostomy bags, NG and PEG feeding tubes and tracheostomies; monitor blood pressure, pulse, temperature, sugar and oxygen levels; give medicines on schedule and keep a medicine chart; help prevent bedsores by turning and positioning bedbound patients; assist with physiotherapy exercises prescribed by the doctor; recognise warning signs and call the doctor or arrange hospital transfer; and keep a daily record for the family and the doctor.

A home nurse works under the instructions of the patient's treating doctor. They do not change medicines or diagnose new problems on their own, and a good nurse knows when to call for help.

A patient care nurse in Islamabad is most often needed for **recovery after surgery**, such as joint replacement, cardiac surgery, spinal surgery or major abdominal operations, where wound care, pain management and mobility support matter. **Stroke patients** need positioning, feeding support, sometimes NG tube care, and help with exercises. **Elderly patients with several conditions**, such as diabetes, heart disease and kidney problems, need careful medicine management and monitoring. **Patients with cancer** may need care during chemotherapy or palliative care at the end of life, with a focus on comfort and dignity. **Bedbound patients** need turning, skin care and prevention of bedsores. **Patients on oxygen, tracheostomy or home ventilation** need nurses with ICU experience.

## Nurse or caretaker?

If the patient needs medical procedures, such as injections, IV lines, dressings, catheter or tube care, you need a nurse. If they mainly need help with walking, bathing, meals and taking tablets on time, a [caretaker in Islamabad](/services/caretakers/islamabad) is usually enough and costs less. Some families start with a nurse after discharge and move to a caretaker once the patient is stable.

## Working with the patient's doctor

The nurse should have the doctor's written instructions, the medicine chart and a contact number for the doctor or the hospital ward. Agree how often the nurse should update the doctor and the family, and which warning signs mean an immediate call or a trip to the emergency department. Keep the discharge summary, test reports and prescriptions in one folder the nurse can see.

## Daily records and family updates

A good home nurse keeps a simple daily chart: vital signs, medicines given and when, food and fluids, urine and bowel output, wound condition and anything unusual. Families abroad or at work can ask for a short WhatsApp update after each shift. These records also help the doctor at follow up visits.

## Equipment at home

Depending on the condition, the patient may need a hospital bed, an air mattress to prevent bedsores, a wheelchair, a commode, a blood pressure monitor, a glucometer, a pulse oximeter, an oxygen concentrator or a suction machine. Many medical suppliers in Blue Area and the F and G sector markets rent equipment. The nurse can advise on what is needed and show the family how to use it.

## Infection control

A home nurse should wash hands before and after every procedure, use gloves, dispose of needles in a sharps container, keep dressings and supplies clean and dry and teach the family simple hygiene steps. This matters especially for patients with wounds, catheters, low immunity or recent surgery.

## Night duty

Many families need a nurse most at night, when patients are restless, confused or at higher risk of falls and breathing problems. A night nurse should stay awake, check the patient regularly, give night medicines on time and keep a record. Provide a comfortable chair, a small lamp and tea or coffee.

## Supporting the family

Caring for a sick parent or spouse is exhausting, even with a nurse. A good home nurse teaches family members simple skills, such as checking sugar, helping the patient sit up safely or spotting early signs of a bedsore, so they feel confident between shifts. The nurse also gives the family something just as important: a few hours of proper sleep and the reassurance that someone trained is watching.

## Keeping a good nurse

Pay on time, provide meals during long shifts, give the nurse a place to rest during breaks and treat them as a professional. Home nursing is demanding work, and a nurse who feels respected stays longer and gives better care.
