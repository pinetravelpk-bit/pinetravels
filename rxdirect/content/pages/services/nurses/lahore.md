## Private nurse Lahore: what a home nurse does

A private nurse in Lahore can give injections, including insulin and blood thinners; manage IV drips and cannulas where the doctor has prescribed them; dress wounds and surgical incisions; care for urinary catheters, colostomy bags, NG and PEG feeding tubes and tracheostomies; monitor blood pressure, pulse, temperature, sugar and oxygen levels; give medicines on schedule and keep a medicine chart; turn and position bedbound patients to prevent bedsores; assist with prescribed exercises; recognise warning signs and call the doctor or arrange hospital transfer; and keep a daily record for the family and the doctor.

A home nurse works under the instructions of the patient's treating doctor. They do not change medicines or diagnose new problems on their own, and a good nurse knows when to call for help.

## Nurse or caretaker?

If the patient needs medical procedures, such as injections, IV lines, dressings, catheter or tube care, you need a nurse. If they mainly need help with walking, bathing, meals and tablets on time, a [caretaker in Lahore](/services/caretakers/lahore) is usually enough and costs less. Some families start with a nurse after discharge and move to a caretaker once the patient is stable.

## Working with the patient's doctor

The nurse should have the doctor's written instructions, the medicine chart and a contact number for the doctor or ward. Agree how often the nurse should update the doctor and family, and which warning signs mean an immediate call or a trip to the emergency department. Keep reports and prescriptions in one folder.

## Daily records and family updates

A good home nurse keeps a daily chart: vital signs, medicines given and when, food and fluids, output, wound condition and anything unusual. Families at work or abroad can ask for a short WhatsApp update after each shift. These records also help the doctor at follow up visits.

## Smog, heat and vulnerable patients

Lahore's smog from November to January is dangerous for patients with heart and lung conditions. A home nurse keeps windows closed on bad days, makes sure the air purifier is running where the family has one, watches oxygen levels closely and alerts the doctor at the first sign of breathing trouble. In summer, elderly and bedbound patients are at risk of dehydration and heatstroke, especially during power cuts, so the nurse tracks fluids carefully.

## Equipment at home

Depending on the condition, the patient may need a hospital bed, an air mattress, a wheelchair, a commode, a blood pressure monitor, a glucometer, a pulse oximeter, an oxygen concentrator or a suction machine. Medical suppliers near the major hospitals rent equipment. Plan a UPS or generator backup for oxygen concentrators and air mattresses.

## Infection control

A home nurse should wash hands before and after every procedure, use gloves, dispose of needles in a sharps container, keep supplies clean and teach the family simple hygiene steps.

## Night duty

Many families need a nurse most at night, when patients are restless, confused or at higher risk of falls and breathing problems. A night nurse should stay awake, check the patient regularly and keep a record. Provide a comfortable chair, a small lamp and tea.

## Supporting the family

A good home nurse teaches family members simple skills, such as checking sugar or helping the patient sit up safely, so they feel confident between shifts, and gives the family a few hours of proper sleep.

## Keeping a good nurse

Pay on time, provide meals during long shifts, give the nurse a place to rest during breaks and treat them as a professional. A nurse who feels respected stays longer and gives better care.
