## Home cook Pakistan: what the job includes

A home cook in Pakistan is responsible for the household's daily meals. In most homes the job covers:

- Breakfast: parathas, eggs, omelettes, halwa puri on weekends, or lighter continental options
- Lunch and lunch boxes for children and working family members
- Dinner with roti or rice, a salan, daal or sabzi, and salad or raita
- Evening tea and snacks, especially when guests visit
- Planning the week's menu with the family
- Making grocery lists and sometimes buying vegetables and meat
- Keeping the kitchen, fridge and storage clean and organised

Some families also want the cook to bake, prepare frozen items like kebabs and samosas for Ramadan, or cook separate meals for elderly parents. Tell us what you need at the start, and we will only send cooks who are comfortable with it.

## Cook for home Pakistan: live-in or live-out?

Choosing between a live-in and live-out cook for home in Pakistan depends on your house and routine.

**Live-out cooks** come at fixed hours, often late morning to after dinner, and go home at night. They suit apartments, smaller homes and families who want privacy in the evening. Distance matters, so we try to match cooks who live close to you.

**Live-in cooks** stay in your servant quarter. They are available for early breakfast, late dinners and unexpected guests. They suit large families, homes with elderly parents and households that entertain often. You provide a clean room, meals and a weekly day off.

Many families start with a live-out cook and switch to live-in once trust is built.

## How to run a useful cooking trial

The trial tells you more than any interview. To make it count:

- **Ask for your everyday food,** not a showpiece biryani. Daal, sabzi, salan and roti reveal real skill.
- **Watch the kitchen habits.** Does the cook wash hands, keep raw meat separate and clean as they go?
- **Check timing.** Was dinner ready when you asked for it?
- **Taste together.** Get the whole family's opinion, including elderly parents and children.
- **Give feedback kindly.** A good cook adjusts salt, spice and oil to your family quickly.

## Ramadan, Eid and guests

The hardest month for any cook is Ramadan. Sehri before dawn, iftar at sunset with pakoras, samosas, fruit chaat and dahi baray, then dinner later in the night, all while the cook is fasting too. Families who discuss Ramadan routines early, split the work sensibly and give the cook rest in the afternoon get through the month without trouble. If your cook goes home for Eid, plan a temporary replacement in advance, since many cooks take leave at the same time.

Guests are part of life in Pakistan, and many families want a cook who can handle a sudden dinner for ten. If this happens often in your home, tell us during the brief. We will look for cooks with experience in larger households, and you may want to agree on extra pay for big events.

## Kitchen hygiene and safety

A cook's hygiene affects your family's health every day. During interviews and trials we look for simple habits: washing hands before cooking and after handling raw meat, using separate boards for meat and vegetables, keeping cooked food covered, storing leftovers properly in the fridge and throwing away anything that smells off. In summer, when food spoils quickly in cities like Multan, Lahore and Karachi, these habits matter even more.

Safety matters too. A good cook checks that the gas is turned off after cooking, keeps the pressure cooker valve clean, and keeps children away from hot oil. If your kitchen uses LPG cylinders or old gas fittings, ask the cook to report any smell of gas immediately.
