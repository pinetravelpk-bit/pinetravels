## Home gardener Rawalpindi: through the seasons

A home gardener in Rawalpindi works with sharp seasonal changes. **Spring**, from February to April, brings flowering roses and winter annuals, and the lawn wakes up after winter. The mali feeds the lawn and watches for aphids. **Summer**, from May to June, is hot and dusty, and the job is mostly watering early or late, mulching beds, washing dust off leaves and keeping the lawn alive. **Monsoon**, from July to September, brings rapid growth, weeds, fungal problems and storms, so mowing, weeding, staking and drainage matter. **Autumn**, from late September to November, is the time to plant winter flowers, overseed lawns and prune many shrubs. In **winter**, frost on cold nights can damage tender plants, so the mali covers or moves them and waters less.

## Dust, construction and leaves

Dust is a constant in Rawalpindi. It coats leaves, blocks their pores and weakens plants. A mali who gently washes down hedges, shrubs and potted plants every week or two, especially in the dry months, keeps the garden looking fresh. Houses next to construction sites in Bahria Town and DHA need this even more often. In autumn, the old trees in the Cantt and Westridge drop huge amounts of leaves, which a good mali turns into compost rather than burning.

## Monsoon storms and Nullah Leh

Rawalpindi's monsoon storms can bring down branches and flood gardens in low lying areas. Before monsoon, the mali should prune dead or overhanging branches, stake young trees and clear drains. After heavy rain, waterlogged lawns need time to dry, and fungal problems need early treatment.

## Lawn care basics

A healthy Rawalpindi lawn needs mowing at the right height, never too short in summer, regular edging, feeding in spring and autumn, and aeration once a year where the soil is compacted. Brown patches usually mean too little water, too much heat or a fungal problem in monsoon. Nut grass and other weeds spread fast if they are not pulled out early. A good mali explains what is happening rather than simply adding more water or fertiliser.

## Water, tankers and watering times

Many Rawalpindi homes depend on WASA supply, boreholes or tankers, and water runs short in summer. A good mali waters early in the morning or after sunset, waters deeply but less often so roots grow down, mulches beds with dry leaves or straw and does not leave the pipe running on paved areas. Drip irrigation for beds and pots saves a lot of water and is worth asking about.

## Trees and pruning

The Cantt, Westridge and Satellite Town have many old trees, including neem, shisham, sukhchain and jaman. A mali can prune small branches and keep young trees healthy, but large trees near roofs, walls or power lines need a professional tree cutter. Ask the mali to report dead branches, termite damage or roots lifting paving early.

## Pests and chemicals

Aphids, whitefly, mealybugs, termites and fungal diseases all appear in Rawalpindi gardens. A careful mali tries simple remedies first and uses chemical pesticides only when needed, with gloves and away from children, pets and the kitchen garden. Store chemicals locked away.
