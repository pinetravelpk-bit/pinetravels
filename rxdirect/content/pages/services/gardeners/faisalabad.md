## Home gardener Faisalabad: through the seasons

A home gardener in Faisalabad works with central Punjab's sharp seasons. **Late winter and spring**, from January to March, bring the best colour: winter annuals and roses bloom and the lawn turns green. The mali feeds the lawn, deadheads flowers and watches for aphids. **Summer**, from April to June, is long and harsh, with temperatures above 45 degrees and dust storms. The job becomes watering early and late, mulching beds, washing dust off leaves and keeping the lawn alive. **Monsoon**, from July to September, brings rapid growth, weeds, fungal problems and waterlogging, so mowing, weeding and drainage matter. **Autumn**, from October to November, is planting season for winter flowers and vegetables, overseeding lawns and pruning. In **December**, fog and frost can damage tender plants.

## Dust, fog and smog

Faisalabad's mills, power looms and busy roads add dust to the air for much of the year, and in winter fog and smog settle over the city. Dust and smog coat leaves and weaken plants. A mali who gently washes down hedges, shrubs and potted plants every week or two keeps them healthier. In dust storms, young plants may need staking so they do not snap.

## Lawn care basics

A healthy Faisalabad lawn needs mowing at the right height, never too short in summer, regular edging, feeding in spring and autumn, and aeration once a year where the soil is compacted. Brown patches usually mean too little water, too much heat or a fungal problem in monsoon. Nut grass spreads fast if not pulled out early. A good mali explains what is happening rather than simply adding more water or fertiliser.

## Kitchen gardens and fruit trees

With Faisalabad's fertile soil, kitchen gardens do very well. In winter, a mali can grow spinach, methi, coriander, radish, carrots, peas, cauliflower and turnips. In spring and summer, tomatoes, chillies, okra, gourds and cucumbers follow. Fruit trees such as guava, kinnow, lemon and mango need feeding before flowering, pruning in winter and protection from fruit flies.

## Water and watering

Many Faisalabad homes depend on boreholes, and water can be brackish in some areas. A good mali waters early in the morning or after sunset, waters deeply but less often, mulches beds and does not waste water on paved areas. Drip irrigation for beds and pots saves water.

## Trees and pruning

Faisalabad's older neighbourhoods have neem, shisham, sukhchain, jaman and peepal trees. A mali can prune small branches and keep young trees healthy, but large trees near roofs or power lines need a professional tree cutter. Before monsoon, ask the mali to check for dead or overhanging branches.

## Rooftop and veranda pots

Many Faisalabad homes, especially in older areas, have little ground space but a big roof. A mali can turn the roof into a productive garden with large pots of tomatoes, chillies, mint, roses and bougainvillea. Pots on the roof dry out fast in summer, so they need watering twice a day in peak heat, a light shade net in the afternoon and a proper potting mix rather than plain soil. Check that water drains away properly so it does not damage the roof.

## Pests and chemicals

Aphids, whitefly, mealybugs, termites and fungal diseases all appear in Faisalabad gardens, and mosquitoes breed in standing water during monsoon. A careful mali tries simple remedies first, removes standing water and uses chemical pesticides only when needed, with gloves and away from children, pets and vegetables.

## Mali, helper or landscaper?

A **mali** maintains the garden week after week. A **landscaper** designs and builds a new garden. A **helper** can water plants and sweep leaves but does not usually know plants. Many families hire a landscaper once and then a mali to keep the garden alive.
