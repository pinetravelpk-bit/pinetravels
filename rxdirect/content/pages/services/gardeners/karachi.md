## Home gardener Karachi: working with the climate

A home gardener in Karachi works with a climate that is warm almost all year. **The cooler months**, from November to February, are the best time to plant flowers, vegetables and new lawns, and gardens look their best from December to March. **Spring and early summer**, from March to June, bring rising heat and strong winds, and the job becomes watering, mulching and protecting young plants. **Monsoon**, from July to September, is short but can bring heavy rain, waterlogging, fungal problems and a burst of weeds. Humidity stays high for much of the year, so the mali watches constantly for mould, mildew and root rot.

## Salt, sand and soil

Much of Karachi, especially DHA, Clifton and areas near the coast, has sandy, saline soil and salty air. Many plants that thrive in Lahore or Islamabad struggle here. A good mali improves the soil with sweet earth, compost and well rotted manure, washes salt off leaves after strong sea winds and chooses salt tolerant plants such as bougainvillea, frangipani, hibiscus, ixora, oleander, palms, lantana and succulents. In raised beds and pots, the mali uses a proper potting mix rather than local sand.

## Water, tankers and saving every drop

Water shortages are a fact of life in Karachi, and many homes depend on tankers. A good mali waters early in the morning or after sunset, waters deeply but less often so roots go down, mulches every bed and pot, groups pots so they shade each other, and uses drip irrigation where possible. Some families reuse water from washing machines or air conditioners for the garden, but salty or soapy water should be used carefully.

## Balcony and rooftop gardens

Many Karachi families garden in flats. Balconies and roofs face strong winds, intense sun and salt. A visit based mali can choose tough plants, use heavy pots that do not blow over, water and feed regularly, repot plants as they grow and treat pests early. Roof gardens need waterproofing and drainage so water does not leak into the flat below.

## Lawn care basics

A Karachi lawn needs the right grass, usually a salt and heat tolerant type, mowing at the right height, never too short in summer, regular edging, feeding in the cooler months and aeration where soil is compacted. Brown patches usually mean too little water, salt build up or fungus in humid weather.

## Trees and palms

Karachi's older neighbourhoods have neem, gulmohar, banyan and coconut and date palms. A mali can prune small branches, clean palm fronds and keep young trees healthy, but tall palms and large trees near roofs or power lines need a professional. Before monsoon, ask the mali to check for dead or overhanging branches.

## Kitchen gardens in Karachi

Karachi's mild winter is perfect for growing vegetables. From November to February, a mali can raise spinach, coriander, mint, lettuce, radish, tomatoes and chillies in beds or large pots, and lemons, curry leaves and papaya do well for much of the year. Use good soil rather than local sand, keep pots out of the harshest afternoon sun and water regularly. A small kitchen garden on a roof or balcony gives fresh herbs every day.

## Pests and chemicals

Mealybugs, whitefly, scale insects, termites and fungal diseases thrive in Karachi's humidity. A careful mali tries simple remedies first, removes standing water to stop mosquitoes and uses chemical pesticides only when needed, with gloves and away from children, pets and the kitchen garden.
