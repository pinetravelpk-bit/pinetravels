## Home gardener Multan: working with the heat

A home gardener in Multan plans the whole year around heat. **The cool season**, from November to February, is when gardens shine: winter annuals, roses and vegetables grow well and lawns recover. This is the time to plant, prune and feed. **Spring**, from late February to March, is short, with flowering and new growth. **The long summer**, from April to October, is about survival: watering deeply at dawn or after sunset, keeping the grass a little longer, mulching every bed, shading young plants with green net and never fertilising in peak heat. **Monsoon**, in July and August, brings humidity, weeds and fungal problems in the middle of the heat. A mali who understands this rhythm saves water and keeps the garden alive.

## Dust storms and hot winds

Multan's dust storms and hot loo winds can strip leaves, snap young plants and bury beds in dust. A good mali stakes young trees and tall plants, washes dust off leaves after a storm, waters deeply before an expected heatwave and plants windbreak hedges such as Arabian jasmine, conocarpus or duranta along exposed boundaries.

## Mango trees and orchards

Mango care is a specialist skill. After the harvest in summer, trees need pruning to open the canopy. Feeding and irrigation are timed around flowering in early spring and fruit set afterwards. Mango hoppers, mealybugs and fruit flies must be watched and controlled, and young trees need protection from frost on the rare cold nights. A mali who has worked in Multan's orchards brings knowledge that a general gardener may not have.

## Lawn care basics

A healthy Multan lawn needs a heat tolerant grass, mowing at the right height, never too short in summer, regular edging, feeding in the cool season and aeration where soil is compacted. Brown patches usually mean too little water or too much heat.

## Water and watering

Water is precious in Multan, and many homes depend on boreholes or canal water on farms. A good mali waters early in the morning or after sunset, waters deeply but less often, mulches beds and uses drip irrigation where possible. Air cooler water should not be dumped on plants if it contains salts or chemicals.

## Kitchen gardens

In Multan's cool season, a mali can grow spinach, coriander, methi, radish, carrots, peas, cauliflower and tomatoes. In summer, heat tolerant crops such as okra, gourds and chillies do well with shade in the afternoon.

## Trees that suit Multan

Trees make Multan homes cooler, and choosing the right ones matters. Neem, sukhchain, shisham, amaltas, jaman, date palms and mango all cope with the heat. Fast growing conocarpus is popular for hedges but can damage foundations and pipes if planted too close to the house, and some families now prefer native trees. A mali can prune small branches and keep young trees healthy, but large trees near roofs or power lines need a professional tree cutter. Before monsoon, ask the mali to check for dead or overhanging branches.

## Rooftop and veranda pots

Pots on a Multan roof dry out within hours in summer. They need watering twice a day in peak heat, a shade net in the afternoon, large containers that hold moisture and a proper potting mix. Group pots together so they shade each other, and move delicate plants under the veranda from May to August.

## Pests and chemicals

Aphids, whitefly, mealybugs, termites and fungal diseases all appear in Multan gardens, and mosquitoes breed in standing water. A careful mali tries simple remedies first and uses chemical pesticides only when needed, with gloves and away from children, pets and vegetables.

## Mali, helper or landscaper?

A **mali** maintains the garden week after week. A **landscaper** designs and builds a new garden. A **helper** can water plants and sweep leaves but does not usually know plants. Many families hire a landscaper once and then a mali to keep the garden alive.

## Keeping a good gardener

Pay on time, buy the tools, seeds and fertiliser the garden needs, and listen to the mali's advice. In summer, let the mali work early in the morning and evening, provide cold water and shade and never expect heavy outdoor work in the afternoon heat. Gardeners take pride in their work, and a mali who is trusted to plan the garden often stays with a family for many years. Agree a weekly day off, a clear list of tasks for each visit and a small budget for seeds and fertiliser, with receipts kept for every purchase.
