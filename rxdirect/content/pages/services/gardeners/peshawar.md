## Home gardener Peshawar: through the seasons

A home gardener in Peshawar works with four clear seasons. **Spring**, from February to April, is the city's most beautiful time: winter annuals and roses bloom, fruit trees blossom and lawns turn green. The mali feeds the lawn, deadheads flowers and watches for aphids on new growth. **Summer**, from May to August, is hot and dusty, and the job becomes watering early and late, mulching beds, washing dust off leaves and keeping the lawn alive. Monsoon rain in July and August brings weeds and fungus. **Autumn**, from September to November, is planting season for winter flowers and vegetables, overseeding lawns and preparing for the cold. **Winter**, from December to January, is cold, with frost on some nights. It is the time to prune fruit trees and roses, protect tender plants from frost and water less.

## Dust, wind and frost

Dry winds and traffic dust coat Peshawar's gardens for much of the year, especially along the Ring Road and in areas with ongoing construction. A mali who washes down hedges and shrubs regularly keeps them healthier. Winter frost can burn bougainvillea, young citrus and potted plants, so they need covering or moving under the veranda on cold nights.

## Fruit trees and orchards

The Peshawar valley is known for its fruit. Peach, plum, pear, apricot, citrus, loquat, pomegranate and fig all grow here. Fruit trees need pruning in winter, feeding before flowering, watering during fruit set and protection from pests such as aphids, fruit flies and borers. A mali who has worked in orchards brings knowledge a general gardener may not have. On farmhouses along Warsak Road and beyond, a full-time or live-in mali is usually needed.

## Lawn care basics

A healthy Peshawar lawn needs mowing at the right height, never too short in summer, regular edging, feeding in spring and autumn, and aeration once a year where the soil is compacted. Brown patches usually mean too little water, too much heat or fungus in monsoon. Weeds spread fast if not pulled out early.

## Kitchen gardens

Many Peshawar families grow their own vegetables. In winter, a mali can raise spinach, coriander, radish, turnips, carrots, peas and lettuce. In spring and summer, tomatoes, chillies, okra, gourds and cucumbers follow. Mint and coriander are grown all year for the kitchen.

## Water and watering

Peshawar homes depend on municipal supply, boreholes or tankers, and water can be short in summer. A good mali waters early in the morning or after sunset, waters deeply but less often, mulches beds and does not waste water on paved areas.

## Trees and pruning

Peshawar's older neighbourhoods have chinar, shisham, peepal, neem and eucalyptus. A mali can prune small branches and keep young trees healthy, but large trees near roofs, walls or power lines need a professional tree cutter.

## Rooftop and veranda pots

Many Peshawar homes have a roof or veranda full of pots. Pots dry out quickly in summer, so they need watering once or twice a day in peak heat, a light shade net in the afternoon and a proper potting mix rather than plain soil. Repot plants every year or two as they grow, check drainage holes so water does not collect, and make sure water draining off the roof does not damage walls or the ceiling below. In winter, move tender plants against a warm wall or under the veranda on frosty nights.

## The hujra garden

In many Peshawar homes, guests first see the lawn and path outside the hujra. Keeping this area neat matters: a freshly mowed lawn, trimmed hedges, clean paths and a few pots of flowers by the entrance. Ask the mali to tidy this area first on days when guests are expected.

## Pests and chemicals

Aphids, whitefly, mealybugs, termites and fungal diseases all appear in Peshawar gardens. A careful mali tries simple remedies first and uses chemical pesticides only when needed, with gloves and away from children, pets and vegetables.

## Mali, helper or landscaper?

A **mali** maintains the garden week after week. A **landscaper** designs and builds a new garden. A **helper** can water plants and sweep leaves but does not usually know plants. Many families hire a landscaper once and then a mali to keep the garden alive.

## Keeping a good gardener

Pay on time, buy the tools, seeds and fertiliser the garden needs, and listen to the mali's advice. Provide water and shade in summer and warm clothing for winter mornings. Gardeners take pride in their work, and Peshawar families value loyalty. A mali who is trusted to plan the garden often stays for many years. Agree leave for Eid and harvest time in advance.
