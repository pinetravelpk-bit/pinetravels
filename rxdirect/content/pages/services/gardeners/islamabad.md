## Home gardener Islamabad: through the seasons

A home gardener in Islamabad works with four distinct seasons. **Spring**, from February to April, is the city's glory: winter annuals bloom, roses flower and the lawn turns green again. The mali feeds the lawn, deadheads flowers and watches for aphids. **Summer**, from May to June, is hot and dry, and the job is mostly watering early in the morning or late in the evening, mulching beds and keeping the lawn alive without overwatering. **Monsoon**, from July to September, brings rapid growth, weeds and fungal problems, so mowing, weeding and drainage matter. **Autumn**, from late September to November, is planting season for winter flowers, the right time to overseed or lay new grass, and the time to prune many shrubs. In **winter**, frost can damage tender plants on cold nights, so the mali covers or moves them and waters less.

## Margalla soil, frost and pollen

Islamabad's soil varies from sector to sector. Some gardens sit on rocky, alkaline ground near the hills, others on clay that cracks in summer and waterlogs in monsoon. A good mali improves the soil with compost and well rotted manure, and knows which plants suit which corner. Winter frost in parts of the city, especially E-11, the D sectors and the Expressway societies, can burn bougainvillea and tender plants, so they need covering on cold nights. Paper mulberry pollen is a serious allergy problem in spring, and some families ask the mali to remove young paper mulberry trees from their plot.

## Water and watering

Water is precious in Islamabad, and many homes depend on tankers or boreholes in summer. A good mali waters early in the morning or in the evening, uses mulch to keep moisture in the soil, waters deeply rather than often and does not flood the lawn. Ask about drip irrigation for beds and pots if water is short.

## Trees, hedges and the CDA

Many Islamabad plots have large trees, some planted by the CDA along the boundary and some by previous owners. A mali can prune small branches, shape hedges and keep young trees healthy, but big trees near power lines or roofs need a professional tree cutter, and some trees on public land need permission before cutting. Ask the mali to report dead branches, termite damage or roots lifting paving early, especially before the monsoon storms.

## Lawn care basics

A healthy Islamabad lawn needs mowing at the right height, never too short in summer, regular edging, feeding in spring and autumn, and aeration once a year where the soil is compacted. Brown patches usually mean too little water, too much heat or a fungal problem in monsoon. Weeds such as nut grass spread fast if they are not pulled out early. A good mali explains what is happening rather than simply adding more water or fertiliser.

## Pests and chemicals

Aphids, whitefly, mealybugs, termites and fungal diseases all appear in Islamabad gardens. A careful mali tries simple remedies first, such as neem oil or soap sprays, and uses chemical pesticides only when needed, with gloves and away from children, pets and the kitchen garden. Store chemicals locked away.
