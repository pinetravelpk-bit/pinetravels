## Home gardener Gujranwala: through the seasons

A home gardener in Gujranwala works with sharp seasonal changes. **Late winter and spring**, from January to March, bring the best colour: winter annuals and roses bloom and lawns turn green. The mali feeds the lawn, deadheads flowers and watches for aphids. **Summer**, from April to June, is hot, and the job becomes watering early and late, mulching beds, washing dust off leaves and keeping the lawn alive. **Monsoon**, from July to September, brings heavy rain, weeds, fungus and waterlogging, so mowing, weeding and drainage matter. **Autumn**, from October to November, is planting season for winter flowers and vegetables and the time to overseed lawns. In **December**, thick fog and occasional frost can damage tender plants.

## GT Road dust and winter fog

Houses near the GT Road and Gujranwala's industrial areas collect dust quickly, and it settles on leaves and weakens plants. A mali who washes down hedges and shrubs every week or two keeps them looking fresh. In winter, dense fog can sit over the city for days, slowing growth and encouraging fungus, so the mali waters less and watches for mildew.

## Lawn care basics

A healthy Gujranwala lawn needs mowing at the right height, never too short in summer, regular edging, feeding in spring and autumn, and aeration once a year where the soil is compacted. Brown patches usually mean too little water, too much heat or fungus in monsoon. Nut grass spreads fast if not pulled out early.

## Kitchen gardens and fruit trees

Gujranwala's fertile soil suits vegetables well. In winter, a mali can grow spinach, methi, coriander, radish, carrots, peas and cauliflower. In spring and summer, tomatoes, chillies, okra and gourds follow. Fruit trees such as guava, kinnow and lemon need feeding before flowering and pruning in winter.

## Water and watering

A good mali waters early in the morning or after sunset, waters deeply but less often, mulches beds and does not waste water on paved areas. In closed houses, a simple timer on a drip system can keep pots alive between visits.

## Trees and pruning

A mali can prune small branches and keep young trees healthy, but large trees near roofs, walls or power lines need a professional tree cutter. Before monsoon, ask the mali to check for dead or overhanging branches.

## Rooftop and veranda pots

Many Gujranwala homes have a roof or veranda full of pots. Pots dry out quickly in summer, so they need watering once or twice a day in peak heat, a light shade net in the afternoon and a proper potting mix rather than plain soil. Repot plants every year or two as they grow, check drainage holes so water does not collect, and make sure water draining off the roof does not damage walls or the ceiling below. In winter, move tender plants against a warm wall or under the veranda on frosty nights.

## Mali, helper or landscaper?

A **mali** maintains the garden week after week. A **landscaper** designs and builds a new garden. A **helper** can water plants and sweep leaves but does not usually know plants. Many families hire a landscaper once and then a mali to keep the garden alive.

## Pests and chemicals

Aphids, whitefly, mealybugs, termites and fungal diseases all appear in Gujranwala gardens. A careful mali tries simple remedies first and uses chemical pesticides only when needed, with gloves and away from children, pets and vegetables.

## Keeping a good gardener

Pay on time, buy the tools, seeds and fertiliser the garden needs, and listen to the mali's advice. Provide drinking water and shade in summer. If you are abroad, a short call of thanks after a good photo update is noticed and valued. A mali who is trusted to plan the garden often stays for many years.
