## Home gardener Lahore: through the seasons

A home gardener in Lahore works with sharp seasonal changes. **Late winter and spring**, from January to March, is Lahore's best garden season: winter annuals bloom, roses flower and the city holds its famous flower shows. The mali deadheads flowers, feeds the lawn and watches for aphids. **Summer**, from April to June, is brutal, with temperatures above 45 degrees, and the job becomes watering early and late, shading delicate plants, mulching beds and keeping the lawn alive. **Monsoon**, from July to September, brings rapid growth, weeds, fungal problems and waterlogging, so mowing, weeding, drainage and staking matter. **Autumn**, from October to November, is planting time for winter flowers and vegetables, overseeding lawns and pruning. In **December**, fog and frost can damage tender plants on cold nights.

## Smog and Lahore gardens

From November to January, smog settles over Lahore and coats leaves with a grey film that blocks sunlight and weakens plants. A mali who gently washes down hedges, shrubs and potted plants every week during smog season keeps them healthier. Smog also makes outdoor work unpleasant, so provide the mali with a mask on bad days and let them start later in the morning when the air is a little clearer.

## Lawn care basics

A healthy Lahore lawn needs mowing at the right height, never too short in summer, regular edging, feeding in spring and autumn, and aeration once a year where the soil is compacted. Lahore's clay soil cracks in summer and waterlogs in monsoon, so adding compost and sand over time improves drainage. Brown patches usually mean too little water, too much heat or fungus. Nut grass spreads fast if not pulled out early.

## Farmhouses and orchards

## Water and watering

Lahore's groundwater is dropping and many homes face shortages in summer. A good mali waters early in the morning or after sunset, waters deeply but less often, mulches beds and does not leave the pipe running on paved areas. Drip irrigation for beds and pots saves a lot of water.

## Trees and pruning

Lahore's older neighbourhoods have magnificent trees, including neem, shisham, peepal, sukhchain, amaltas and jaman. A mali can prune small branches and keep young trees healthy, but large trees near roofs, walls or power lines need a professional tree cutter. Before monsoon, ask the mali to check for dead or overhanging branches.

## Kitchen gardens and fruit trees

Many Lahore families grow their own vegetables. In winter, a mali can raise spinach, methi, coriander, radish, carrots, peas, cauliflower and lettuce in raised beds or even large pots on the roof. In spring and early summer, tomatoes, chillies, okra, gourds and cucumbers follow. Fruit trees such as guava, lemon, kinnow and mango need feeding before flowering, pruning in winter and protection from fruit flies. A mali who keeps simple records of what was sown and when helps the kitchen garden improve year after year.

## Mali, helper or landscaper?

A **mali** maintains the garden week after week. A **landscaper** designs and builds a new garden, with levelling, soil, grass, paths and lighting. A **helper** can water plants and sweep leaves but does not usually know plants. Many families hire a landscaper once and then a mali to keep the garden alive. Tell us what stage your garden is at and we will suggest the right person.

## Pests and chemicals

Aphids, whitefly, mealybugs, termites and fungal diseases all appear in Lahore gardens, and mosquitoes breed in standing water during monsoon. A careful mali tries simple remedies first, removes standing water from pots and trays, and uses chemical pesticides only when needed, with gloves and away from children, pets and the kitchen garden.

## Keeping a good gardener

Pay on time, buy the tools, seeds and fertiliser the garden needs, and listen to the mali's advice. Provide drinking water and shade in summer. Gardeners take pride in their work, and a mali who is trusted to plan the garden often stays with a family for many years.
