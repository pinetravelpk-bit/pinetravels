## Winter and frozen pipes

In Islamabad's coldest weeks, especially in the higher sectors, Bani Gala and the Margalla foothills, exposed pipes on roofs and outer walls can freeze overnight and burst. Insulating exposed pipes, keeping a tap slightly open on the coldest nights and draining lines in houses left empty prevent costly damage.

## Hard water and filters

Water in parts of Islamabad, especially from boreholes, leaves white scale on taps, showers and geyser elements. Scale reduces flow and shortens the life of geysers. A sediment filter on the main line and descaling showerheads and geysers once a year help.

## Monsoon and roof drainage

Islamabad's monsoon brings heavy rain. Blocked roof drains cause water to pool on the roof and seep into ceilings. Before July, ask a plumber to clear roof drains, check rainwater pipes and make sure courtyard drains flow freely. In houses with basements, a working sump pump is essential.

## Plumbing in renovations

When you renovate a bathroom or kitchen in Islamabad, the pipework hidden behind the tiles matters more than the fittings you can see. Replace old GI pipes with good quality PPR while the walls are open, place valves where they can be reached, slope floor drains properly and pressure test every line before tiling. Fixing a leak after new tiles are laid means breaking them again. Our plumbers can work alongside your tile fixer and contractor so the job is done in the right order.

## Saving water at home

Water is precious across Pakistan, and Islamabad is no exception. Fix dripping taps and running toilets quickly, because a single leaking flush can waste hundreds of litres a day. Fit aerators on taps, use a float switch so the overhead tank never overflows, wash cars with a bucket rather than a pipe and water gardens in the early morning. A plumber can check your whole system for silent leaks in one visit.

## Geyser safety

Gas geysers are common in Islamabad homes and need care. A geyser should have a working thermostat, a pressure relief valve that is not blocked, a proper flue that takes fumes outside and a gas connection without leaks. Never install a gas geyser inside a closed bathroom. If you smell gas, notice soot around the burner or the pilot flame is yellow instead of blue, switch it off and call a plumber. Electric geysers need correct earthing and a breaker of the right size, so an electrician may need to check the connection too.

## Water tanks and hygiene

Overhead and underground tanks collect mud, rust and sometimes dead insects or birds. They should be cleaned and disinfected at least twice a year, and lids should close tightly. A plumber can clean the tank, fix float valves that let it overflow, replace leaking connections and fit a simple filter on the main line. Clean tanks mean fewer stomach problems for the family, especially children.

## Warning signs of a bad plumbing job

Be careful if a plumber wants to cut walls before finding the leak, uses lots of tape and sealant instead of proper fittings, cannot explain what went wrong, insists on replacing parts that look fine, or leaves without testing. Good work is neat, tested under pressure and explained clearly. Our plumbers show you what they found and what they fixed.

## What to do while you wait

For a burst pipe or major leak, close the main valve or switch off the pump, turn off the geyser, move valuables away from the water and mop up to prevent damage to floors and walls. For a blocked toilet, stop flushing. For a gas smell from a geyser, close the gas valve, open windows and do not switch on lights or appliances until a plumber has checked it.
