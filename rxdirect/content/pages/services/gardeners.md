## Home gardener Pakistan: planning through the seasons

A home gardener in Pakistan works with the climate, not against it. A typical year:

**Spring (February to April):** feed the lawn, plant summer flowers and vegetables, prune roses, prepare for heat.

**Summer (May to July):** water early morning or evening, mow higher to protect grass, mulch beds, watch for pests and heat stress. In Multan and Lahore, shade sensitive plants.

**Monsoon (July to September):** clear drains, prevent waterlogging, watch for fungus, reduce watering, stake tall plants.

**Autumn (October to November):** plant winter flowers such as petunias, pansies and sweet peas, sow winter vegetables, reseed bare lawn patches.

**Winter (December to January):** reduce watering, protect delicate plants from frost in Islamabad and Peshawar, trim hedges, plan for spring.

## Garden maintenance staff Pakistan: water and sustainability

Water is precious in most Pakistani cities. Good garden maintenance staff in Pakistan use it wisely: watering at dawn or dusk to reduce evaporation, using pipes with spray nozzles rather than open hoses, mulching beds to keep moisture in, and choosing hardy local plants that need less water. In homes that depend on tankers or have limited supply, a gardener who understands water saving is especially valuable.

Composting kitchen and garden waste is another simple habit that improves soil and reduces rubbish. Many gardeners are happy to set up a compost corner if asked.

## Common lawn problems and how a good mali fixes them

**Yellow or brown patches in summer.** Usually caused by heat stress, watering at midday, or mowing too short. A good gardener raises the mower height, waters deeply in the early morning and feeds the lawn before the hottest months.

**Waterlogged soil in monsoon.** Lawns in low lying parts of Rawalpindi, Islamabad and Lahore can stay soggy for days, which rots roots and invites fungus. The fix is improving drainage, aerating the soil with a fork and reducing watering during the rains.

**Weeds taking over.** Weeds spread fast when a lawn is thin. Regular hand weeding, reseeding bare patches in autumn and keeping the grass healthy are better long term solutions than repeated chemical sprays.

**Ants, termites and white grubs.** These pests damage roots and wooden furniture. A gardener should notice early signs, such as wilting patches or mud tunnels, and treat them before they spread.

**Plants dying after being planted.** Often caused by planting in the wrong season, poor soil preparation or overwatering. Buying plants from a reliable nursery and planting at the right time of year saves money.

## Choosing the right grass

The grass you choose decides how much work and water your lawn needs. Many Pakistani homes use local doob grass, which is tough and handles heat well. Mexican grass is popular for its soft texture and low height. Korean grass gives a thick carpet look but grows slowly and needs care. Your gardener can advise which suits your city, soil, sunlight and how much the lawn is used by children or for events.

## Trees, fruit plants and hedges

Trees and fruit plants need a different kind of care from lawns. Mango, guava, citrus, pomegranate and jamun trees need pruning at the right time, regular feeding and protection from pests. Hedges along boundary walls look best when trimmed regularly in the growing season rather than cut back hard once a year. Large trees close to the house should be checked before monsoon for weak branches that could fall in a storm. For tall trees, a gardener may suggest a specialist with proper equipment.

## Kitchen gardens and rooftops

More families are growing their own vegetables and herbs: tomatoes, chillies, mint, coriander, spinach, okra and gourds. A gardener can set up raised beds or pots on a rooftop or in a corner of the lawn, plan crops by season and keep pests away without heavy chemicals. Rooftop gardens need extra care with waterproofing and drainage, so check your roof before adding heavy planters.

## Safety and tools

Gardening involves sharp tools, ladders and chemicals. Provide good quality tools, keep pesticides and fertilisers locked away from children and pets, and ask the gardener to use gloves and a mask when spraying. Electric lawnmowers and hedge trimmers should be checked for damaged cables. In summer, make sure the gardener works in the cooler hours, drinks plenty of water and rests in the shade to avoid heatstroke.

## Making visit based arrangements work

Visit based gardeners serve several homes, so a clear routine matters. Fix the days and approximate times of visits, list the tasks for each visit and decide who will water the plants on the days the gardener does not come. In peak summer, a lawn may need watering every day even if the gardener visits only twice a week, so another household member or a helper should take that on, or an automatic timer can be fitted to the tap. Keep tools in one place so the gardener does not lose time searching for them. A short monthly walk around the garden together helps you agree on priorities for the coming weeks.
