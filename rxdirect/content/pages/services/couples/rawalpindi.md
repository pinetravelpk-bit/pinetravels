A live in domestic couple in Rawalpindi needs a proper home on the property: a private quarter with its own bathroom, a fan for the hot summer and a safe heater for winter, basic furniture, a place to cook or meals provided, and reliable electricity and water. ## Dividing duties clearly

Write down each partner's duties, hours and day off. Decide who gives them instructions. Avoid treating the couple as one worker who does everything. When duties are clear, couples work well together.

## Children on site

Some couples come with young children. Decide in advance whether you can accommodate them, where they will play and whether they will attend a nearby school.

## Leave and relief cover

Couples usually take leave together, which means the house loses both people at once. Plan Eid and annual leave in advance, and arrange relief staff for those days so the house is never left empty.

## Couple or two separate staff?

A couple suits homes where both partners will live on site, where the work splits naturally between inside and outside, and where the family wants stability. Two separate staff may suit better when you only need one person to live in, when you need very specialised skills such as a trained chef and an ex-army guard, or when you do not have a private quarter. In Rawalpindi, many families with large houses or farmhouses find that a couple costs a little less than two individuals and stays longer.

## The trial period

During the first week, watch how the couple settles in. Does each partner do their own work without being chased? Do they speak to each other and to your family respectfully? Is the quarter kept clean? Do they ask sensible questions? Give clear feedback to each partner separately, and do not judge one partner only by the other's performance. If one partner is strong and the other weak, tell us early, because it usually does not improve on its own.

## When problems arise

Couples sometimes argue, fall ill or face family emergencies in their village. Be understanding, but keep the house covered. If a dispute between the partners starts affecting their work, speak to them calmly together. If one partner wants to leave, the other usually goes too, so address concerns early rather than waiting.

## Keeping a good couple

Pay both partners on time, respect their privacy and family life, keep their quarter in good condition and treat them as trusted staff. Couples who feel settled often stay with a family for many years. Small gestures, such as help with their children's school fees, a new set of clothes at Eid or time off for a family wedding, build loyalty that lasts. In a close knit city like Rawalpindi, families known for treating couples well find good staff more easily.
