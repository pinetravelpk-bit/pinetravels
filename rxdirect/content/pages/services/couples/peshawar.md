## Hire domestic couple in Peshawar: two trusted people for both sides of the house

Peshawar homes are often divided in two. The hujra and outer areas belong to the men and their guests, while the family side of the house belongs to the women and children. This makes hiring staff more delicate than in many other cities. A male servant cannot easily work in the family quarters, and a female worker cannot serve guests in the hujra. A domestic couple solves this neatly: the wife works inside with the women of the house, and the husband handles the hujra, gate, driving and outdoor work. Living together on the property, they bring stability, trust and a natural fit with Peshawar's way of life.

RX Direct helps Peshawar families hire a domestic couple with careful checks on both partners, a clear division of duties and our 6 month replacement guarantee. This page explains how couples work in Peshawar homes, typical role combinations, costs and how to set up a successful arrangement.

## The hujra and guests

In homes with a busy hujra, the husband's role is central: greeting guests respectfully, serving qehwa, tea and meals prepared by his wife or the cook, keeping the hujra clean and never discussing who visits. The wife, meanwhile, keeps the family side running smoothly. A couple who understand these customs make hospitality effortless.

## Couples with elderly parents at home

When elderly parents live at home, a couple is especially valuable in Peshawar: the wife cares for the mother, including bathing and dressing, and the husband helps the father to the hujra, the mosque and appointments. For heavier care needs, a [caretaker](/services/caretakers/peshawar) may be added.

## Families with men abroad

In many Peshawar homes, the men of the family work in the Gulf. A trusted couple gives the women at home support inside and a reliable man for errands, driving and the gate. Families abroad can join the interview by video.

## Couple or two separate staff?

In Peshawar, a couple often makes more sense than separate staff because it respects the division of the house. Two separate staff may suit better only when you need very specialised skills or have no private quarter.

## Money, keys and household rules

Set simple rules from the start: a fixed weekly amount for shopping with receipts, a clear list of rooms that stay locked, and an agreement about visitors to the quarter.

## Dividing duties clearly

Write down each partner's duties, hours and day off. Decide who gives instructions to the wife and who to the husband. Avoid treating the couple as one worker who does everything.

## Leave and relief cover

Couples usually take leave together, especially for Eid and family weddings. Plan leave in advance and arrange relief staff so the house is covered.

## The trial period

During the first week, watch how the couple settles in. Does each partner do their own work without being chased? Do they speak to each other and to your family respectfully? Is the quarter kept clean? Do they ask sensible questions? Give clear feedback to each partner separately, and do not judge one partner only by the other's performance. If one partner is strong and the other weak, tell us early, because it usually does not improve on its own.

## When problems arise

Couples sometimes argue, fall ill or face family emergencies in their village. Be understanding, but keep the house covered. If a dispute between the partners starts affecting their work, speak to them calmly together. If one partner wants to leave, the other usually goes too, so address concerns early rather than waiting until a resignation arrives.

## Health and wellbeing

A couple living on your property is a small family. If one partner falls ill, help them see a doctor quickly, both for their sake and because the other partner will struggle to do two jobs alone. Many good employers cover basic medical costs or give an advance when needed.

## Children on site

Some couples come with young children. Decide in advance whether you can accommodate them, where they will play and whether they will attend a nearby school. Many families are happy with one or two children, while others prefer couples without children living on site.

## Winter care for the household

In Peshawar's cold months, the couple's work changes. The wife airs quilts on sunny days and keeps the family rooms warm, while the husband carries gas cylinders, checks heaters and keeps the hujra comfortable for guests. Make sure the couple's own quarter is warm too.

## Keeping a good couple

Pay both partners on time, respect their privacy and family life, keep their quarter warm and in good condition and treat them as trusted members of the household. Peshawar families value loyalty, and a couple who feel respected often stay for many years.
