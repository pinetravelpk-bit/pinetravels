## Looking after closed houses

For a closed house, agree a clear routine: open and air every room weekly, dust and sweep, run taps and flush toilets so pipes stay healthy, check roofs and drains after rain, look after the garden and keep the gate secure. Ask for photos or a short video each week. A trusted relative should visit monthly and hold a spare key. When the family plans to return, the couple deep cleans, makes up the rooms and stocks the kitchen.

## Couples with elderly parents at home

When elderly parents live alone, a couple can be the ideal support: the wife cooks, cleans and helps the mother, while the husband drives the father to appointments, does the shopping and keeps the house secure. Daily WhatsApp updates to children abroad give everyone peace of mind. For heavier care needs, a [caretaker](/services/caretakers/gujranwala) may be added.

## Weddings and family visits

When relatives return for a wedding or Eid, the house fills up. A couple who know the house well lead the preparations, and temporary [helpers](/services/helpers/gujranwala) can be added under their supervision.

## Managing a couple from abroad

Join the interview by video, ask a trusted relative to attend the trial and visit regularly, agree a weekly photo update and a monthly video call, and send salaries on a fixed date. CCTV that you can view from abroad, with the couple told openly, gives extra reassurance.

## Money, keys and household rules

Set simple rules from the start: a fixed monthly household budget with receipts kept, a clear list of rooms that stay locked, and an agreement about visitors to the quarter.

## Dividing duties clearly

Write down each partner's duties, hours and day off. Decide who gives them instructions locally. Avoid treating the couple as one worker who does everything.

## Leave and relief cover

Couples usually take leave together. Plan Eid leave in advance and arrange relief staff or a relative to stay so the house is never left empty.

## Couple or two separate staff?

A couple suits homes where both partners will live on site, where the work splits naturally between inside and outside, and where the family wants stability. Two separate staff may suit better when you only need one person to live in or need very specialised skills. For closed houses and parents living alone, a couple usually costs a little less than two individuals and stays longer.

## The trial period

During the first week, watch how the couple settles in. Does each partner do their own work without being chased? Do they speak to each other and to your family respectfully? Is the quarter kept clean? Do they ask sensible questions? Give clear feedback to each partner separately, and do not judge one partner only by the other's performance. If one partner is strong and the other weak, tell us early, because it usually does not improve on its own.

## When problems arise

Couples sometimes argue, fall ill or face family emergencies in their village. Be understanding, but keep the house covered. If a dispute between the partners starts affecting their work, speak to them calmly together. If one partner wants to leave, the other usually goes too, so address concerns early rather than waiting until a resignation arrives.

## Health and wellbeing

A couple living on your property is a small family. If one partner falls ill, help them see a doctor quickly, both for their sake and because the other partner will struggle to do two jobs alone. Many good employers cover basic medical costs or give an advance when needed.

## Children on site

Some couples come with young children. Decide in advance whether you can accommodate them, where they will play and whether they will attend a nearby school. Many families are happy with one or two children, while others prefer couples without children living on site.

## Keeping a good couple

Pay both partners on time, respect their privacy and family life, keep their quarter in good condition and treat them as trusted staff. A short call of thanks from abroad is noticed and valued. Couples who feel settled often stay with a family for many years.
