## Farmhouses and weekend homes

Lahore's farmhouses are busy at weekends and for events, and quiet during the week. A couple keeps them ready: the husband looks after the gate, lawns, orchard, generator and water supply, and the wife cleans, prepares guest rooms and cooks when the family visits. Agree how the couple's time is used on quiet days, and who they report to when the family is away. For large farmhouses, a guard alongside the couple is common.

## Entertaining and wedding season

Lahore families host dawats, family lunches and wedding functions at home. A couple who have worked in busy households know how to prepare: the wife leads the kitchen and guest rooms, the husband manages parking, setup and outdoor cleaning. For big events, add temporary [helpers](/services/helpers/lahore) under the couple's supervision.

## Couples with elderly parents at home

When elderly parents live at home, a couple can be ideal: the wife helps with cooking, cleaning and caring for the mother, while the husband drives the father to appointments and helps him walk. For heavier care needs, a [caretaker](/services/caretakers/lahore) may be added.

## Homes of families abroad

Many Lahore families spend summers abroad, and some live abroad full time. A couple keeps the house lived in, aired and maintained, and has it ready when the family returns. Agree a checklist, regular photos and a trusted local contact.

## Dividing duties clearly

Write down each partner's duties, hours and day off. Decide who gives them instructions. Avoid treating the couple as one worker who does everything.

## Leave and relief cover

Couples usually take leave together. Plan Eid and annual leave in advance, and arrange relief staff so the house is never left empty.

## Couple or two separate staff?

A couple suits homes where both partners will live on site, where the work splits naturally between inside and outside, and where the family wants stability. Two separate staff may suit better when you only need one person to live in, when you need very specialised skills such as a trained chef and an ex-army guard, or when you do not have a private quarter. In Lahore, many families with large houses or farmhouses find that a couple costs a little less than two individuals and stays longer.

## The trial period

During the first week, watch how the couple settles in. Does each partner do their own work without being chased? Do they speak to each other and to your family respectfully? Is the quarter kept clean? Do they ask sensible questions? Give clear feedback to each partner separately, and do not judge one partner only by the other's performance. If one partner is strong and the other weak, tell us early, because it usually does not improve on its own.

## When problems arise

Couples sometimes argue, fall ill or face family emergencies in their village. Be understanding, but keep the house covered. If a dispute between the partners starts affecting their work, speak to them calmly together. If one partner wants to leave, the other usually goes too, so address concerns early rather than waiting.

## Money, keys and household rules

A live-in couple often handles grocery money, keys and access to every room. Set simple rules from the start: a fixed weekly amount for shopping with receipts kept in a notebook, a clear list of rooms that stay locked, and an agreement about visitors to the quarter. Rules that apply to both partners equally, explained politely on the first day, prevent most misunderstandings later.

## Health and wellbeing

A couple living on your property is a small family. If one partner falls ill, help them see a doctor quickly, both for their sake and because the other partner will struggle to do two jobs. Many good employers cover basic medical costs or give an advance when needed, and register the couple with PESSI or EOBI where it applies.

## Keeping a good couple

Pay both partners on time, respect their privacy and family life, keep their quarter in good condition and treat them as trusted staff. Couples who feel settled often stay with a family for many years.
