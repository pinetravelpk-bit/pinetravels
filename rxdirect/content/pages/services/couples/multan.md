## Farmhouses and mango orchards

Couples are ideal for Multan's farmhouses and orchards. The husband looks after the gate, lawns, orchard, livestock, tubewell and generator, and the wife keeps the house clean and cooks when the family visits. In mango season, the farm is busy with pickers, traders and trucks, and a couple who know the family's routine keep the house running smoothly. Agree who the couple reports to when the family is in town, and how their time is used in quieter months.

## Couples with elderly parents at home

When elderly parents live at home, a couple can be ideal: the wife helps with cooking, cleaning and caring for the mother, while the husband drives the father to Nishtar or other appointments and helps him walk. For heavier care needs, a [caretaker](/services/caretakers/multan) may be added.

## Couple or two separate staff?

A couple suits homes where both partners will live on site and the work splits naturally between inside and outside. Two separate staff may suit better when you only need one person to live in or need very specialised skills. Many Multan families with farmhouses find that a couple costs a little less than two individuals and stays longer.

## The trial period

During the first week, watch how the couple settles in. Does each partner do their own work without being chased? Do they speak respectfully? Is the quarter kept clean? Give feedback to each partner separately. If one partner is strong and the other weak, tell us early.

## Money, keys and household rules

Set simple rules from the start: a fixed weekly amount for shopping with receipts kept in a notebook, a clear list of rooms that stay locked, and an agreement about visitors to the quarter.

## Health and wellbeing

If one partner falls ill, help them see a doctor quickly. In summer, make sure both partners rest in the hottest hours and drink enough water, especially the partner who works outdoors.

## Dividing duties clearly

Write down each partner's duties, hours and day off. Decide who gives them instructions. Avoid treating the couple as one worker who does everything.

## Leave and relief cover

Couples usually take leave together, often at Eid and harvest. Plan leave in advance and arrange relief staff so the house or farm is never left empty.

## When problems arise

Couples sometimes argue, fall ill or face family emergencies in their village. Be understanding, but keep the house covered. If a dispute between the partners starts affecting their work, speak to them calmly together. If one partner wants to leave, the other usually goes too, so address concerns early rather than waiting until a resignation arrives.

## Children on site

Some couples come with young children. Decide in advance whether you can accommodate them, where they will play and whether they will attend a nearby school. Many families are happy with one or two children, while others prefer couples without children living on site.

## Livestock and seasonal farm work

Many Multan farmhouses keep a few buffaloes, cows or goats, and some families keep animals at the town house too for milk or before Eid ul Adha. If the husband will care for animals, say so in the brief and agree extra pay, because feeding, milking and cleaning sheds take hours every day. During wheat harvest and mango season, farm work increases sharply. Agree in advance whether the couple helps with this, how many extra hours are involved and whether temporary labour will be hired, so the house itself is not neglected.

## Keeping a good couple

Pay both partners on time, respect their privacy and family life, keep their quarter cool and in good condition and treat them as trusted staff. Couples who feel settled often stay with a family for many years. Small gestures, such as a share of mangoes from the orchard, new clothes at Eid and help with their children's school fees, build loyalty that lasts.
