## Home security guard Pakistan: the job at a residence

A home security guard in Pakistan usually handles:

- Controlling the main gate and checking every visitor
- Keeping a visitor log with names, times and purpose
- Receiving deliveries and checking them before they enter
- Watching the perimeter and doing regular rounds, especially at night
- Opening the gate for family cars and checking unfamiliar vehicles
- Switching outdoor lights on and off and checking locks
- Calling the family or the police if anything is suspicious
- Keeping an eye on household staff entry and exit

Some families also ask guards to water plants or wash the car during the day. Minor extra tasks are fine if agreed, but the guard's first job is security.

## Private security guard Pakistan: choosing the right cover

A private security guard in Pakistan can be arranged in several ways:

**Night guard only.** The most common setup for homes, covering the hours when the house is most vulnerable.

**Day and night guards.** Two guards on 12 hour shifts give round the clock cover. Suitable for larger homes, homes of prominent people and houses that are often empty.

**Guard plus household staff.** Some homes have a day guard who also opens the gate for the family, with a night guard for full security.

**Team with supervisor.** For larger properties, offices or housing society gates with several entry points.

One person cannot guard properly for 24 hours. If you need full cover, plan for two guards plus relief for days off.

## Keeping night guards alert

The biggest complaint about guards is sleeping on night duty. It is usually a sign of a badly designed arrangement rather than a bad guard. To keep night guards alert:

- Keep shifts to 12 hours, never 24
- Provide a chair, shelter and light, but not a bed at the gate
- Ask for a visitor and rounds register signed every hour or two
- Make occasional random calls during the night
- Give a proper weekly day off with a relief guard
- Pay on time; tired, worried guards are less alert

## Working with CCTV and alarms

Guards and technology work best together. A camera covering the gate and perimeter lets the family check on things remotely and gives the guard backup if there is a dispute. An alarm button that rings inside the house helps the guard warn the family quickly. If you have CCTV, show the guard how to check it, and agree who reviews recordings if something happens.

## What a guard should never do

Clear boundaries protect everyone. A guard should never let unknown visitors in without confirming with the family, leave the gate unattended, share the family's schedule or travel plans with anyone, accept packages for people they do not know, or bring friends to the post. We include these points in every guard's briefing, and families should repeat them in the first week.

Most gated housing societies, including DHA, Bahria Town and the Askari schemes, have their own security arrangements and rules for private guards. Many require residents to register their guards with the society's security office, provide a copy of the CNIC and sometimes a police verification form. Some societies restrict private guards to the inside of the property boundary. Check your society's rules before your guard starts, and register them in the first week. ## Treating guards well

Guards spend long, often boring hours outdoors in heat, cold, dust and rain. Small comforts make a big difference to how alert and loyal they are: a shaded post with a fan in summer, a heater or warm jacket in winter, clean drinking water, access to a bathroom, tea on cold nights and proper meals for live-in guards. Respect matters too. A guard who is spoken to politely by the family will speak politely to your visitors.

## Ex-army guards: what to expect

Families often ask for retired army or FC personnel, and for good reason. Ex-forces guards usually bring discipline, punctuality, a habit of following procedures and confidence in handling difficult situations. They tend to take the visitor log and rounds seriously. On the other hand, some are older and may struggle with very long shifts, so the arrangement should be realistic. We check the discharge book or service record to confirm the background, rather than relying on what the guard says.

## Emergencies

Every guard should know exactly what to do in an emergency: who to call first, where the main power switch is, how to reach the nearest police station, and how to help in a fire or medical emergency. Write these numbers on a card at the guard's post. Run through the plan once in the first week.
