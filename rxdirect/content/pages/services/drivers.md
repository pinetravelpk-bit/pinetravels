## Family driver Pakistan: what the job includes

A family driver in Pakistan usually handles:

- School drop-offs and pick-ups, often for several children at different schools
- Office drops and pick-ups for working family members
- Taking elderly parents to doctors, hospitals and family visits
- Grocery runs, bill payments and other errands
- Weekend outings, weddings and family events
- Basic car care: checking oil, water, tyre pressure and keeping the car clean
- Keeping a fuel and mileage log

Some families also need drivers for airport runs at odd hours or regular out of city trips. Tell us your routine and we will shortlist drivers who are comfortable with it.

## Personal driver Pakistan: for executives and senior family members

A personal driver in Pakistan often serves one person: a business owner, a senior officer, a doctor with hospital rounds or an elderly parent. These roles call for extra qualities: discretion about where the employer goes and who they meet, polished manners with guests, flexibility for changing schedules and a calm driving style. When you need a personal driver, tell us who they will drive and what that person values most.

## Private driver Pakistan: rules worth agreeing

A private driver in Pakistan works best with clear rules from day one. Agree in writing:

- Daily hours, weekly day off and how overtime is paid
- Whether the car can be taken home at night
- How fuel is bought and recorded
- Phone use while driving (it should be none)
- Seat belts and child seats for children
- Who the driver should take instructions from when several family members need the car

Clear rules protect the driver as much as the family. Disputes almost always come from things that were never agreed.

## Safety first: what good drivers do

Safe driving habits matter more than speed or shortcuts. Good drivers keep to speed limits, especially near schools; never use the phone while driving; make sure children are belted in; keep a safe distance on the motorway; avoid driving when tired; and report any problem with the car straight away. During our road tests we watch for these habits as closely as for skill.

Families can help by not pressuring drivers to rush, planning school and office timings realistically, and making sure the car is properly maintained.

## Car care and maintenance

A good driver looks after the car as if it were their own. That means checking engine oil, coolant and tyre pressure regularly, keeping the car clean inside and out, noticing unusual sounds early and reminding the family when servicing, registration renewal or the token tax is due. Some families ask drivers to take the car to the workshop for routine service. If you want this, agree how payments and receipts will be handled.

## School runs: what parents should know

School runs are the most common reason families hire a driver, and they deserve extra care. Share the exact pick-up and drop-off timings, the school gate procedures and who is allowed to collect the children. Give the driver the school's contact number and tell the school who the driver is. Ask the driver never to leave children alone in the car and never to stop for personal errands with children inside. A driver who takes these points seriously is worth keeping.

## Licence types and why they matter

In Pakistan, driving licences are issued by category. A licence for a motorcycle does not allow driving a car, and a car licence does not cover heavy vehicles. Some drivers carry licences from another province, which are valid nationwide, and some carry international or old manual licences that may have expired. We check that the licence matches the vehicle you own, that it is valid, and that the photo and details match the driver's CNIC. For drivers in Islamabad and Punjab, licence details can usually be checked online through the official licensing authority, which adds another layer of confidence.

## Common reasons drivers leave

The most frequent reasons drivers leave a job are unpaid overtime, very long days without breaks, being on call every day with no proper day off, and being blamed for traffic delays. A fair schedule and agreed overtime rate solve most of these. When a driver does leave under our guarantee, we send replacements quickly so school runs are not disrupted for long.

## Using tracking and dash cameras

Some families install a tracker or dash camera in the car. This is reasonable, especially when children travel without an adult, but tell the driver openly at the start. Honest drivers rarely mind, and many welcome a camera because it protects them if there is an accident or a dispute about a route. Hidden tracking discovered later damages trust far more than it helps.
