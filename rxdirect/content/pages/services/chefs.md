## Private chef Pakistan: what the role covers

A private chef in Pakistan is responsible for the entire food experience in your home, not just cooking. Typical duties include:

- Planning weekly menus with the family, balancing taste, health and variety
- Cooking daily meals at a high standard across several cuisines
- Preparing multi-course dinners for guests and formal events
- Managing kitchen stock, ordering ingredients and controlling costs
- Supervising kitchen helpers or junior cooks
- Keeping the kitchen to professional hygiene standards
- Plating and presentation for guests
- Baking breads, cakes and desserts

Some chefs also handle special diets, children's menus, meal prep for the week or catering for family businesses.

## Chef service Pakistan: full-time or event based

Our chef service in Pakistan works in two ways.

**Full-time private chefs** work in your home every day, usually six days a week. They suit families who entertain often, large households and anyone who wants consistent high quality food. Live-in arrangements are common for chefs serving early breakfasts and late dinners.

**Event chefs** are booked for specific occasions: a dinner party for twenty, a mehndi, a Eid lunch for the whole family, or a week of events around a wedding. Event chefs often bring or coordinate helpers, and we agree the menu, numbers and cost in advance.

## Personal chef Pakistan: choosing the right person

A personal chef in Pakistan becomes part of your daily life, so fit matters as much as skill. When you interview, consider:

- **Cuisine range.** Can they cook what your family actually eats, as well as what impresses guests?
- **Flexibility.** Will they adjust recipes to your family's taste, or insist on doing things their way?
- **Organisation.** Do they plan ahead, or rush at the last minute?
- **Cost awareness.** A chef who orders imported ingredients for every meal can double your grocery bill.
- **Temperament.** Chefs who have worked in busy restaurants can be intense. Make sure they will be comfortable in a home.

## Setting up your kitchen for a chef

A chef can only work as well as the kitchen allows. Before a chef starts, check:

- Enough counter space for preparation and plating
- A good range or cooktop, and an oven if baking or continental cooking is expected
- Sharp knives, quality pans and basic professional tools
- Proper storage: fridge and freezer space, dry storage, spice racks
- Good ventilation, since a weak exhaust makes summer cooking miserable
- A clear budget for groceries and how purchases will be recorded

Many chefs will suggest small equipment upgrades in their first weeks. These are usually worth it.

## Working with your chef day to day

The best results come from a weekly menu meeting. Spend fifteen minutes with your chef on Sunday to plan meals, guests and any special requests for the week. Agree a grocery budget and a simple record of spending. Give feedback after dinners, especially early on, so the chef learns your family's preferences quickly. When you host, brief the chef on the number of guests, any dietary needs and the timing of courses well in advance.

## Healthy eating and special diets

More families now hire a chef for health reasons rather than for entertaining. A parent with diabetes needs controlled carbohydrates without bland food. A family member recovering from heart surgery needs low salt, low fat meals that still taste like home. Someone training for fitness wants high protein meal prep for the week. Children with allergies need careful ingredient control.

A skilled chef can handle all of this, often within the same household, without anyone feeling they are eating "diet food". If health is the main reason for hiring, share the doctor's or dietitian's guidance with us. We will look for chefs who have cooked for special diets before, and the trial can focus on those meals.

## Questions to ask a chef in the interview

A few questions reveal a lot about a chef's real level:

- Walk me through a dinner menu for twelve guests, including timings.
- How would you adjust your cooking for an elderly parent with diabetes?
- How do you keep grocery costs under control in a home kitchen?
- What would you do if a dish went wrong thirty minutes before guests arrive?
- Which kitchens have you worked in, and what did you learn in each?

Listen for specific, practical answers. Vague answers usually mean limited experience.
