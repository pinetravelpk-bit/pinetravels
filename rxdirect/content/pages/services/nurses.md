## Private nurse Pakistan: what the role covers

A private nurse in Pakistan provides skilled medical care at home, including:

- Giving injections: intramuscular, subcutaneous and insulin
- Managing IV drips, cannulas and infusions as prescribed
- Dressing surgical wounds, ulcers and bed sores
- Catheter care and changes
- NG tube feeding and care
- Colostomy and stoma care
- Monitoring blood pressure, pulse, temperature, oxygen and blood sugar
- Managing complex medicine schedules accurately
- Turning bedridden patients and preventing pressure sores
- Nebulisation and oxygen therapy support
- Recording observations for the doctor
- Recognising warning signs and arranging hospital transfer when needed

Nurses also help with hygiene and comfort, though for patients who mainly need daily living support, a [caretaker](/services/caretakers) may be more suitable.

## Working with the patient's doctor

A home nurse works under the guidance of the patient's doctor, not instead of them. Share the doctor's instructions, prescriptions and follow up dates with the nurse. Ask the nurse to keep a simple daily record of vital signs, medicines given, food and fluid intake and any concerns. Take this record to doctor appointments. Doctors find it very useful, and it helps catch problems early.

## Preparing your home for a nurse

A few preparations make nursing care safer and easier:

- A bed that allows the nurse to work comfortably, ideally a hospital bed for bedridden patients
- An air mattress for patients at risk of bed sores
- Good lighting near the bed
- A clean area for medicines and dressing supplies
- A blood pressure monitor, glucometer and thermometer
- A list of emergency numbers and the nearest hospital
- For live-in nurses, a private room to rest

## Night care and 24 hour cover

Patients who need care through the night, such as those on oxygen, with dementia or at risk of falls, may need a night nurse. One nurse cannot safely provide 24 hour care for long. For round the clock care, two nurses on 12 hour shifts, or a nurse plus a caretaker, is the safe arrangement.

## Infection control at home

Patients recovering from surgery or with weak immunity are vulnerable to infection. A good nurse washes hands before and after every procedure, uses gloves, keeps dressing materials sterile, disposes of needles in a sharps container and keeps the patient's area clean. Family members and visitors should follow basic hygiene too, especially during winter flu season.

## Warning signs a nurse should report immediately

One of the most valuable things a qualified nurse brings is knowing when something is wrong. Families should agree with the nurse that these signs are reported straight away, day or night:

- Sudden breathlessness, chest pain or a drop in oxygen levels
- High fever, especially after surgery or with a catheter in place
- Redness, swelling, pus or a bad smell from a wound
- Confusion, unusual drowsiness or difficulty waking the patient
- Very high or very low blood sugar readings
- Blood in urine or stool, or vomiting blood
- Signs of a new stroke: facial drooping, slurred speech or sudden weakness on one side

The nurse should know the patient's doctor's number, the nearest emergency department and how to call an ambulance. Rescue 1122 operates in Punjab and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, and Edhi and Chhipa ambulances are widely used in Karachi.
