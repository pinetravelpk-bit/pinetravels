## Domestic cleaner Karachi: supplies and safety

Most domestic cleaners in Karachi use supplies provided by the family. Keep a basic kit: mop and bucket, brooms, microfibre cloths, glass cleaner, floor cleaner, bathroom cleaner, a fan brush, an anti-fungal spray and gloves. Marble needs pH neutral cleaners. ## Salt air and humidity

Homes in DHA, Clifton and Defence View sit close to the sea, and salt air does damage over time. Glass becomes hazy within days, metal window grills and railings rust, and wooden furniture can swell or grow mould. A cleaner who wipes glass and metal regularly, keeps bathrooms and kitchens dry, leaves cupboards open for airing on dry days and reports spots of rust or damp early helps protect the house itself. In very humid months, check behind wardrobes and under sinks for mould, and ask the cleaner to wipe these spots with an anti-fungal product.

## Monsoon and flooding

Karachi's monsoon is short but can be intense. Roads in many areas flood quickly, power cuts are common and mud is carried into homes on shoes, tyres and pets. After heavy rain, porches, garages and stairs need washing, and ground floor flats may need water cleared and floors dried properly. Make sure electricity is safe before cleaning begins, and photograph any damage if you plan to raise it with a landlord or building management.

## Pre-Eid and wedding deep cleaning

Before Eid and weddings, Karachi families want the house spotless: walls washed, fans and lights cleaned, curtains taken down, cupboards emptied and wiped, floors and grout scrubbed. A deep clean team can do in a day what one person would take a week to finish. Book early, because the last two weeks before Eid are the busiest time of the year. A lighter clean after Eid, once guests have left, deals with the extra stains and dishes.

## Flats and high rise buildings

Many Karachi families live in flats, from older buildings in Gulshan and North Nazimabad to new towers in Clifton and along Shahrah-e-Faisal. Flats bring their own issues: building rules on entry and exit, limited parking, lifts that stop during load shedding and balconies exposed to road dust. Tell us the building's entry rules, whether the cleaner needs to be registered with security and how many floors they may have to climb when the lift is off. Part-time cleaners coming two or three days a week usually suit flats well.

## When nobody is home

If the cleaner will work while you are out, decide on keys or building access, lock private rooms and keep valuables away. A WhatsApp message on arrival and departure builds confidence. Be home for part of the first few sessions so you can give feedback and see how the cleaner works. If you have a camera at the entrance, tell the cleaner openly.

## Homes with children, elderly parents or pets

Use mild products where babies crawl and lock cleaning chemicals away. Mop when elderly parents are resting so they do not slip, and leave bathrooms dry. Mention pets in your brief, because some cleaners are nervous around dogs and pet hair adds to the work.
