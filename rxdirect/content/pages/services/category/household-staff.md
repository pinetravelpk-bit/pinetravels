## Full time household staff Pakistan: what the job looks like

Full-time household staff usually work eight to ten hours a day, six days a week, with one day off. For live-out staff the hours are fixed, for example 9 am to 6 pm or 11 am to 9 pm for a cook who covers lunch and dinner. For live-in staff the hours are more spread out, with a long break in the afternoon.

What a fair full-time arrangement includes:

- Salary paid by a fixed date each month
- One full day off each week
- Annual leave, usually around two weeks, often taken at Eid
- An Eid bonus, commonly half or one month's salary
- A yearly increase
- Meals during work hours

Families who offer these basics keep staff much longer. Workers talk to each other, and households with a good reputation find it easier to hire.

## Live in household staff Pakistan: what to prepare

Live-in staff are a big help, especially in larger homes and families with children or elderly parents. Before a live-in worker arrives, make sure you have:

1. A clean, private room with a fan, light and a lock on the inside
2. Access to a bathroom
3. Bedding, a cupboard or shelf for belongings
4. A clear daily schedule with break times
5. An agreed day off and how they will travel home for leave

Female live-in workers often prefer homes where another woman is present. Many male workers prefer servant quarters with a separate entrance. We discuss these details openly so both sides are comfortable.

## Home staff agency Pakistan: common mistakes to avoid

We see the same problems again and again when families hire without a clear plan:

- **Hiring one person for three jobs.** A maid who also cooks, watches the baby and washes the car will burn out and leave. Split the work or hire a helper.
- **No trial period.** Everyone looks good in an interview. A trial day in your real routine shows you much more.
- **Unclear salary terms.** Agree in writing what the salary includes, when it is paid and how leave works.
- **Ignoring early warning signs.** Late arrivals, phone use during work and small lies tend to grow. Raise them early and kindly.
- **Paying an advance to a stranger.** Never pay large advances to workers you have not verified.

A home staff agency like ours helps you avoid most of these by setting expectations on both sides before day one.

## Signs your home needs more help

Families often wait too long before hiring. By the time they call us, someone in the house is exhausted and the existing worker is ready to quit. These are the signs we hear most often:

- Dinner is regularly late or replaced with takeaway because nobody has time to cook.
- Your maid is also cooking, babysitting and running errands, and her work quality has dropped.
- A parent is staying home from work to look after a newborn or an elderly relative.
- The house is clean on weekdays but falls apart on the worker's day off.
- Guests visit often and every visit means a day of extra work for the family.
- The lawn, roof tanks and outdoor areas have been ignored for months.

If two or more of these sound familiar, adding one helper or a part-time cleaner usually solves more than you expect. It also protects the worker you already have, who is likely doing more than one person should.

## Managing household staff well

Good staff stay with good employers. Over the years we have noticed a few habits shared by families who keep the same workers for years.

**They give clear instructions.** Instead of "clean the house", they say which rooms, how often and to what standard. A written list on the fridge for the first month helps a lot.

**They correct privately.** Pointing out a mistake in front of guests or other staff embarrasses the worker and rarely fixes the problem. A quiet word works better.

**They notice good work.** A simple "the food was very good today" costs nothing and makes a real difference to how a worker feels about the job.

**They keep promises.** If you agreed to a day off on Sunday, do not cancel it at the last minute. If you promised an increase after six months, give it.

**They handle money carefully.** Grocery money is counted and recorded. Salaries are paid in full. Advances are small, agreed in advance and deducted fairly.

## Safety in a home with staff

Hiring verified staff is the most important safety step, but a few simple habits add another layer of protection:

- Keep jewellery, cash and important documents in a locked cupboard or safe.
- Keep a copy of the worker's CNIC and our verification summary in a safe place.
- Register live-in staff with your local police station or society security office if your area requires it. Many housing societies in DHA, Bahria Town and Askari ask for this.
- Give keys only to the people who need them, and take them back when someone leaves.
- Install a camera at the entrance if you want, but tell staff about it openly.
- Save our number. If anything feels wrong, call us early rather than late.
