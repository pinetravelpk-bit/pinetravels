## Home maintenance staff Pakistan: prevention saves money

Most expensive repairs start as small problems that nobody fixed. A drip under the sink becomes a damp wall. A loose wire behind a switchboard becomes a burnt socket. A door that sticks in the monsoon becomes a broken frame. Regular home maintenance staff in Pakistan can catch these early.

A simple seasonal routine looks like this:

- **Before summer:** check air conditioner wiring and breakers, clean water tanks, test pumps and motors, check UPS batteries.
- **Before monsoon:** clear roof drains, check for cracks and seepage, seal windows and doors, check exterior paint.
- **Before winter:** service geysers and gas heaters, check gas pipe connections, fix draughty windows.
- **Once a year:** a full electrical check, especially in houses older than fifteen years.

Offices, housing societies and families with several properties often keep a technician on a monthly arrangement for this. It costs far less than emergency repairs.

## House maintenance workers Pakistan: safety first

A few safety rules apply to every job:

1. For electrical work, the main supply must be switched off for the circuit being worked on.
2. Gas related work, such as gas heater or stove connections, must use proper fittings and be leak tested.
3. Painters should use ventilation and keep paint and thinners away from children.
4. Ladders and scaffolding must be stable, especially for exterior work on double storey houses.
5. Keep children and pets away from the work area.

House maintenance workers in Pakistan often work without basic safety gear. We brief our technicians on these points and expect them to follow them.

## Warning signs of a bad tradesman

Whether you hire through us or someone else, watch for these signs. They usually mean trouble later:

- **Asking for a large advance before seeing the job.** A small amount for materials on a big job can be fair. A large cash advance for labour is not.
- **Refusing to give a price until the end.** A skilled tradesperson can estimate most jobs, even if the final figure changes slightly.
- **Pushing expensive replacements straight away.** Sometimes a part does need replacing. But a technician who wants to replace everything without explaining why is often padding the bill.
- **Leaving halfway to "get a part" and not coming back.** This is one of the most common complaints we hear.
- **No tools of their own.** A real electrician carries a tester and a multimeter. A real plumber carries wrenches and sealing tape.
- **Messy work hidden behind covers.** Ask to see inside switchboards and under sinks before the covers go back on.

When you use RX Direct, we deal with these problems for you. If a technician behaves badly, we hear about it, act on it and stop sending them.

## Problems we see most often in Pakistani homes

Houses in Pakistan face a particular set of problems because of heat, dust, voltage swings and water quality. The calls we get most often are:

**Tripping breakers and burnt sockets in summer.** Air conditioners, water pumps and irons running together overload old wiring. The fix is usually proper load distribution and correctly rated breakers, not just a new socket.

**Low water pressure and dry taps.** Often caused by blocked lines, a weak motor or a poorly placed tank. In some areas it is the supply itself, and a pressure pump helps.

**Damp and peeling paint.** Rising damp from the ground or seepage from the roof ruins paint within months. Painting over it does not work. The source must be treated first.

**Swollen doors in monsoon.** Wooden doors absorb moisture and stick. A carpenter can plane and reseal them, and the right polish prevents it next year.

**Geyser problems in winter.** Gas geysers need regular servicing, and many accidents happen because nobody checks the venting. Electric geysers often fail because of hard water scale.
