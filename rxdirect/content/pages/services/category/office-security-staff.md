## Hire security staff Pakistan: choosing the right cover

The type of security you need depends on your premises and your risk. Here is how we usually advise clients:

**A single guard on day shift** suits most small offices, clinics and shops where the main need is visitor control during working hours.

**Day and night guards on 12 hour shifts** suit homes, warehouses and offices with valuable equipment, where the premises must never be left without someone at the gate.

**A team with a supervisor** suits housing society gates, larger factories and sites with several entry points.

**A guard who can also drive** suits homes where the family needs occasional driving and a presence at the gate. We place these combined roles carefully, since one person cannot guard the gate while driving.

When you hire security staff in Pakistan, think about relief cover too. Guards need a weekly day off and annual leave, and the gate still needs to be covered on those days. We can arrange relief guards.

## Rules every business should follow with support staff

A few practices protect both the business and the worker:

1. Pay salaries on time, through bank transfer where possible, so there is a record.
2. Keep a copy of each worker's CNIC and our verification summary.
3. Give clear written duties, especially for guards: shift times, visitor rules and emergency contacts.
4. Provide uniforms for guards and office boys if you expect them to wear one.
5. Respect working hours and pay overtime for late nights.
6. Register guards with your building or society security office where required.

## Common problems we solve for businesses

Most companies that come to us have already tried hiring support staff on their own. The same problems come up again and again:

**Guards who sleep on night duty.** Usually this happens when one guard is made to cover 24 hours, or when nobody checks on the night shift. We place guards on realistic 12 hour shifts and suggest simple checks such as a register signed every two hours or a call from the supervisor at random times.

**Office boys who disappear on long errands.** Often the office boy has no clear list and is sent out for every small task. A fixed errand schedule, for example bank and courier runs once in the morning and once after lunch, saves hours each week.

**Drivers who treat the company car as their own.** Clear rules on fuel logs, personal use and parking at night prevent this. We brief corporate drivers on these rules before placement.

**High turnover.** Support staff leave when salaries are paid late, overtime is ignored or they are spoken to rudely. We share honest feedback with clients when we see these patterns, because replacing staff every two months costs the business more than fixing the cause.

**Petty cash going missing.** Keep a simple register for every rupee given for errands, with receipts where possible. Honest staff welcome this, because it protects them too.

## Questions to ask office and security candidates

Even after our checks, you should meet the candidate yourself. Useful questions include:

- For guards: What would you do if someone refused to show ID at the gate? How do you stay alert on night duty?
- For office boys: How do you keep track of money given for errands? Have you handled confidential files before?
- For drivers: Which routes would you take from here to the airport at rush hour? How do you keep a fuel log?
- For all: Why did you leave your last job? What time can you reach here in the morning?

The answers tell you a lot about experience and attitude. We are happy to sit in on interviews if you want our view.
