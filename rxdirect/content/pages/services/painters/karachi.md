## Salt air, mould and the right products

Near the coast, ordinary paint chalks, fades and peels quickly, and metal grills and railings rust through enamel within a year or two. Use exterior paints designed for coastal conditions, an anti fungal paint in bathrooms and kitchens, and a rust converter and good primer before enamel on metal. Proper surface washing and drying before painting matter more here than anywhere else.

## Painting flats

In Karachi flats, the painter works in a small space with furniture that cannot always be moved out. Work room by room, cover everything with drop sheets, keep windows open for ventilation and check the building's rules on working hours, lift use and disposal of empty paint tins. Repainting between tenants is quicker with a standard neutral colour.

## Grills, railings and gates

Karachi homes have a lot of metal: window grills, balcony railings, gates and staircases. In the salty air, these rust quickly. Scraping off rust, applying a rust converter and a good metal primer, then two coats of quality enamel, protects them far longer than a quick coat of paint over rust.

## Exterior painting and safety

Exterior walls, boundary walls and stairwells often need ladders or scaffolding. Painters should use stable ladders or proper scaffolding, never stand on chairs or drums, and work in pairs at height. Keep children and pets away from the work area, and move cars from under walls being painted. For tall houses in Karachi, ask how the painter plans to reach the top floor safely before agreeing the quote.

## Best time to paint

Paint dries and cures best in dry, mild weather. In Karachi, the months after the monsoon and before the coldest weeks, and again in early spring, usually give the best results. Avoid painting exteriors in heavy rain, dust storms or the hottest midday sun, and give interior paint extra drying time in damp or cold weather.

## Ceilings, cracks and soot

Ceilings collect cooking smoke, heater soot and water stains from roof leaks above. Before repainting, a painter washes or sands stained areas, seals water marks with a stain blocking primer so they do not bleed through, and fills hairline cracks. Settlement cracks that keep coming back may point to a structural or plaster problem, and the painter should tell you honestly rather than simply covering them. In Karachi, ceilings in kitchens and rooms with gas heaters usually need repainting more often than the walls.

## Preparation is everything

Most paint failures in Karachi come from poor preparation, not poor paint. A good painter scrapes off loose and flaking paint, fills cracks, sands the surface smooth, applies putty where walls are uneven, sands again, wipes away dust and applies a primer before the top coats. Skipping primer saves a day but leads to patchy colour and paint that peels. Ask what preparation is included in the quote.

## Choosing the right paint

Use **emulsion** for interior walls and ceilings, with a washable finish in kitchens, children's rooms and passages. Use **weather resistant exterior paint** outside. Use **enamel** on wood and metal, over the right primer. Use **anti fungal paint** in bathrooms and damp prone rooms. Matt finishes hide wall imperfections, while silk and satin finishes are easier to wipe clean. A reliable brand costs more per tin but covers better and lasts longer, so the total cost is often similar.

## How much paint do you need?

The painter will estimate based on wall area, surface condition and the number of coats. As a rough guide, new or very porous walls drink more paint, and strong colours over light ones may need an extra coat. Buy all the paint for a room from the same batch so the shade matches, and keep a little left over for touch ups.

## Choosing colours that work

Light, warm whites and soft neutrals make rooms feel bigger and cooler, which suits Karachi's hot months. Use stronger colours on one feature wall rather than all four. Test a small patch on the wall and look at it in daylight and at night before deciding, because colours look very different on a chart.

## Painting with the family at home

Most families stay at home while painting is done. Work room by room, move furniture to the middle and cover it, keep windows open, choose low odour paints where children, elderly parents or anyone with asthma will be in the house, and keep everyone out of a room until the paint has dried.

## Warning signs of a poor paint job

Be careful if a painter skips scraping and primer, thins the paint too much to stretch it, paints over damp or mould, leaves drips and brush marks, splashes floors and switches, or asks for most of the money upfront. Good painters cover your furniture, work cleanly and walk through the finished job with you.
