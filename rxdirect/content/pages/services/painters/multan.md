## Heat and the right time to paint

In Multan's summer, paint dries too fast in direct sun, leaving brush marks and poor adhesion. Exterior painting is best done from October to March. If it must be done in summer, painters should work on shaded walls, early in the morning and late in the afternoon. Heat reflective exterior paints in lighter colours keep walls and rooms cooler.

## Dust storms and boundary walls

Dust storms leave Multan's walls coated and dull. Washable exterior paint, a thorough wash before repainting and regular cleaning of the boundary wall keep the house looking fresh between paint jobs.

## Older houses and damp

Older houses in Gulgasht and the inner city often suffer from rising damp at the base of walls after the monsoon. Painting over damp only hides it for a few weeks. A good painter identifies the cause, lets the wall dry, scrapes off loose paint and uses a damp resistant primer before the top coats.

## Exterior painting and safety

Exterior walls, boundary walls and stairwells often need ladders or scaffolding. Painters should use stable ladders or proper scaffolding, never stand on chairs or drums, and work in pairs at height. Keep children and pets away from the work area, and move cars from under walls being painted. For tall houses in Multan, ask how the painter plans to reach the top floor safely before agreeing the quote.

## Best time to paint

Paint dries and cures best in dry, mild weather. In Multan, the months after the monsoon and before the coldest weeks, and again in early spring, usually give the best results. Avoid painting exteriors in heavy rain, dust storms or the hottest midday sun, and give interior paint extra drying time in damp or cold weather.

## Ceilings, cracks and soot

Ceilings collect cooking smoke, heater soot and water stains from roof leaks above. Before repainting, a painter washes or sands stained areas, seals water marks with a stain blocking primer so they do not bleed through, and fills hairline cracks. Settlement cracks that keep coming back may point to a structural or plaster problem, and the painter should tell you honestly rather than simply covering them. In Multan, ceilings in kitchens and rooms with gas heaters usually need repainting more often than the walls.

## Preparation is everything

Most paint failures in Multan come from poor preparation, not poor paint. A good painter scrapes off loose and flaking paint, fills cracks, sands the surface smooth, applies putty where walls are uneven, sands again, wipes away dust and applies a primer before the top coats. Skipping primer saves a day but leads to patchy colour and paint that peels. Ask what preparation is included in the quote.

## Choosing the right paint

Use **emulsion** for interior walls and ceilings, with a washable finish in kitchens, children's rooms and passages. Use **weather resistant exterior paint** outside. Use **enamel** on wood and metal, over the right primer. Use **anti fungal paint** in bathrooms and damp prone rooms. Matt finishes hide wall imperfections, while silk and satin finishes are easier to wipe clean. A reliable brand costs more per tin but covers better and lasts longer, so the total cost is often similar.

## How much paint do you need?

The painter will estimate based on wall area, surface condition and the number of coats. As a rough guide, new or very porous walls drink more paint, and strong colours over light ones may need an extra coat. Buy all the paint for a room from the same batch so the shade matches, and keep a little left over for touch ups.

## Choosing colours that work

Light, warm whites and soft neutrals make rooms feel bigger and cooler, which suits Multan's hot months. Use stronger colours on one feature wall rather than all four. Test a small patch on the wall and look at it in daylight and at night before deciding, because colours look very different on a chart.

## Painting with the family at home

Most families stay at home while painting is done. Work room by room, move furniture to the middle and cover it, keep windows open, choose low odour paints where children, elderly parents or anyone with asthma will be in the house, and keep everyone out of a room until the paint has dried.

Good painters cover your furniture, work cleanly and walk through the finished job with you.
