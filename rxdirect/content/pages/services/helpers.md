## Domestic helper Pakistan: typical duties

A domestic helper in Pakistan usually takes on a mix of:

- Washing dishes and keeping the kitchen tidy
- Chopping vegetables, cleaning meat, kneading dough and other kitchen prep
- Sweeping, dusting and mopping smaller areas
- Taking out rubbish and keeping outdoor areas clean
- Carrying groceries and running small errands
- Serving tea and food when guests visit
- Helping the maid with laundry and folding
- Washing the car and cleaning the porch

Helpers are flexible by nature. The key is to agree which tasks are theirs, so they do not end up doing the cook's and the maid's work as well.

## Home helper Pakistan: working alongside other staff

A home helper in Pakistan usually works with someone else, so team fit matters. Before placing a helper, we ask who they will work with and what that person expects. Some tips for smooth teamwork:

- Make it clear who gives the helper instructions: you, the cook or the maid.
- Split duties in writing so nobody feels overloaded.
- Do not let senior staff treat the helper as their personal servant.
- Pay the helper directly, not through another staff member.

When roles are clear, a helper lightens everyone's load and the whole team works better.

Kitchen helpers deserve special mention. In large households and homes with frequent guests, the cook spends hours on prep and cleaning instead of cooking. If you have a good cook who seems overwhelmed, a kitchen helper is usually the answer.

## Training a new helper

Helpers often learn on the job. The first two weeks are the most important. Show the helper how you want things done, from how dishes are stacked to where cleaning supplies are kept. Correct mistakes calmly and early. Many helpers grow into cooks or maids over time, and families who invest in training often end up with loyal, skilled staff.

## Fair treatment for helpers

Because helpers are often the youngest and least experienced members of household staff, they can be the easiest to overlook. Make sure the helper gets proper meals, rest breaks, a weekly day off and their full salary on time. Do not let other staff take advantage of them. A helper who feels respected works harder and stays longer.

## Helper, maid or cleaner: which do you need?

These three roles overlap, and families often ask which one fits. A simple way to decide:

**Choose a maid** if you need one person to take full responsibility for cleaning, laundry and keeping the house in order every day. A maid works more independently and is accountable for the standard of the whole house.

**Choose a cleaner** if you mainly need floors, bathrooms and kitchens cleaned on a schedule, daily or a few times a week, and do not need laundry or other household support. Cleaners suit apartments and offices.

**Choose a helper** if you already have a cook or maid who needs support, or if you want someone flexible for lighter chores and errands at a lower cost. Helpers take instructions and fill gaps.

Many larger homes have all three in some form. Smaller homes often start with one helper and add a maid later. If you are unsure, describe your day to us and we will suggest the right mix.

## Errands and handling money

Helpers are often sent to the market, the pharmacy or the bank. That means handling cash. To keep things clear and fair, give a fixed amount for each errand, ask for the change and receipts, and keep a simple written record. Honest helpers appreciate this, because it protects them from any suspicion. If the helper will make regular purchases, agree a weekly limit and check the record together at the end of each week.

For safety, avoid sending a new helper with large amounts of cash in the first weeks, and never share bank cards or PIN codes.

## Why helpers leave

Helpers leave most often when they are treated as the lowest person in the house: given leftover food, spoken to rudely by other staff, denied a day off or paid late. They also leave when the job quietly grows into two or three jobs without any change in pay. Simple fairness keeps them, and helpers who are treated well often grow into trusted cooks and maids with the same family over the years.
