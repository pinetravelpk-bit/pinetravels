## Home cook Peshawar: what the job covers

A home cook in Peshawar usually handles:

- Breakfast: parathas, anday, chai, and sometimes paye or halwa on weekends
- Lunch and dinner for the family: salan, daal, sabzi, rice and roti or naan
- Qehwa, green tea and snacks for visitors throughout the day
- Guest meals at short notice: karahi, namkeen gosht, pulao, kebabs
- Larger cooking for Eid, weddings and family gatherings
- Menus planned with the family and grocery lists for local markets
- Kitchen cleaning and organisation

Many households also want simpler, less oily food for elderly parents and lighter dishes for children. Tell us how your family eats.

## Peshawari food: what we test

During interviews and trials, we focus on the dishes that define a Peshawar kitchen: namkeen gosht cooked slowly with just salt and fat, chapli kabab with the right crunch, karahi, kabuli pulao with carrots and raisins, and soft naan or roti. We also test everyday daal and sabzi, because that is what the family eats most days. For families who want them, we include continental dishes for children.

## Winter cooking and gas

Peshawar winters are cold, and gas pressure often drops in the mornings and evenings. Experienced cooks plan ahead, preparing salan early, using pressure cookers and switching to LPG where available. Winter also brings dishes like saag, paye and halwa, and plenty of hot qehwa for guests. Ask cooks how they handle low gas days during the interview, and make sure the kitchen has proper ventilation if an LPG cylinder is used indoors.

## Cooking for elderly parents

Many Peshawar families have parents at home while children work in the Gulf or other cities. Elderly parents often need food with less salt, less fat and softer textures. A cook who understands this, while still cooking proper food for guests, is valuable. Share any medical advice with us and we will shortlist cooks who have cooked for older people.

## Cooking for the hujra and large gatherings

In many Peshawar homes, the hujra or guest room receives visitors throughout the day: elders from the village, business contacts, neighbours, relatives passing through. The kitchen supports it with a steady flow of qehwa, chai, dry fruit, biscuits and, when the time is right, a full meal. Cooking for the hujra requires a cook who can judge quantities quickly, keep food warm and fresh, and serve neatly. For jirgas, condolence gatherings and weddings, the numbers can reach fifty or more, and the cook may need extra hands. We look for cooks whose references mention this kind of experience, and we can arrange temporary helpers for big occasions.

## Tandoor and fresh naan

Some Peshawar households have a tandoor at home, and fresh naan with every meal is a matter of pride. Using a tandoor well takes practice: getting the heat right, slapping the dough at the right moment, and not burning the bread or the cook's hands. If you have a home tandoor and want the cook to use it, mention it in the brief. Many cooks from Khyber Pakhtunkhwa are skilled at it, but not all are, and it is worth checking in the trial.

## Buying meat and groceries

Peshawar families are particular about meat. Many prefer buying from a trusted butcher themselves, while others send the cook with clear instructions on the cut and quantity. Agree how meat and groceries will be bought, set a weekly budget for daily items like vegetables, milk and bread, and keep a simple record of spending. It keeps things clear for everyone and protects an honest cook from doubt.

## Kitchen hygiene

During the trial, notice whether the cook washes hands, keeps raw meat separate, cleans properly and covers food. In a kitchen that serves both family and guests, good hygiene protects everyone.

## Ramadan, Eid and weddings

Ramadan brings sehri and big iftar gatherings. Eid means guests from morning to night, and wedding season brings large family events. Many cooks go home for Eid, so plan cover early. For big events, a [helper](/services/helpers/peshawar) alongside the cook makes the work manageable.

## Keeping a good cook in Peshawar

Pay on time, respect the day off, allow travel home at Eid, and recognise good work, especially after a big gathering. Cooks who are treated with respect often stay with Peshawar families for many years. In a culture that values loyalty as much as Peshawar's does, a long serving cook often becomes almost part of the family, trusted with recipes, guests and the household's good name.
