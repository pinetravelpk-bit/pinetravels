## Karachi biryani, seafood and iftar

Three things come up in almost every Karachi cook brief. First, biryani: layered properly, rice not mushy, meat tender, spice to the family's taste. Second, seafood: many Karachi families eat fish and prawns weekly, and handling seafood safely in the city's heat matters. Third, Ramadan: big iftar spreads with pakoras, samosas, dahi baray, fruit chaat and rooh afza, followed by dinner. We test cooks on the dishes that matter most to you.

## Humidity, heat and food safety

Karachi's humidity spoils food quickly and makes flour, spices and rice attract insects. Good cooks store dry goods in airtight containers, keep leftovers refrigerated, and never leave seafood or meat out. In load-shedding hours, they plan cooking so the fridge stays closed as much as possible. During interviews we ask how cooks handle these Karachi problems.

## Water and gas in the Karachi kitchen

Many Karachi homes depend on tanker water and face gas pressure problems, especially in winter evenings. Experienced Karachi cooks know how to save water while washing vegetables and dishes, and they plan cooking around low gas hours, sometimes switching to an LPG cylinder. These small skills make a cook far easier to live with.

## Cooking for guests in Karachi homes

Hospitality in Karachi rarely comes with notice. A cousin calls from the gate, an uncle arrives with his family after Maghrib, friends drop in after a wedding. In many homes the cook is expected to put together chai, snacks and sometimes a full dinner within an hour. Cooks who have worked in busy Karachi households keep a few things ready for this: frozen kebabs and samosas, extra dough, boiled chickpeas, yoghurt for raita. If your home has frequent guests, mention it in your brief, and we will look for cooks whose references describe this kind of pace. You may also want to agree in advance on extra pay for large gatherings, so the cook does not feel taken for granted.

## Cooks for elderly parents and special diets

Many Karachi families have parents with diabetes, high blood pressure, heart conditions or kidney problems. Their meals need careful control of sugar, salt, oil and sometimes protein, without becoming tasteless. A cook who understands this can make a real difference to a parent's health. Share the doctor's or dietitian's guidance with us, and we will shortlist cooks who have cooked for similar needs. During the trial, ask the cook to prepare one regular meal and one diet meal, so you can see both.

When you share your brief, mention the kitchen size and any building rules, such as restrictions on staff entry times or use of the service lift. ## Kitchen hygiene

During the trial, notice whether the cook washes hands, keeps raw and cooked food apart, cleans seafood properly, covers food from flies and leaves the kitchen clean. In Karachi's climate these habits protect your family's health.

## Grocery runs and delivery

Many Karachi families now order groceries, meat and fish through delivery apps or by phone from a nearby shop.
