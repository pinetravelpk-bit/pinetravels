## Winter gas, summer heat and the Rawalpindi kitchen

Rawalpindi kitchens change with the seasons. In winter, gas pressure drops in many areas, especially in the mornings and evenings when everyone is cooking. Experienced cooks plan around it: kneading dough and preparing salan early, using a pressure cooker to save time, and keeping an LPG cylinder as backup where the family has one. In June, kitchens become very hot, and a cook who has to stand over a stove for hours without a working exhaust fan will not stay long. Monsoon brings humidity that spoils flour, spices and leftovers quickly, so good storage habits matter.

During interviews we ask cooks how they handle these situations. A cook who already knows Rawalpindi's gas and weather problems adapts much faster than one who is new to the city.

## Kitchen hygiene we look for

Hygiene matters as much as taste. In trials, watch whether the cook washes hands before starting and after handling raw chicken, keeps separate boards for meat and vegetables, covers cooked food, puts leftovers in the fridge promptly and cleans the stove at the end. In a joint family home where children and elderly parents eat from the same kitchen, these small habits protect everyone's health.

## Keeping a good cook in Rawalpindi

Pay on the same date every month, keep the weekly day off, give feedback on taste early and kindly, and make sure the kitchen has a working exhaust fan for summer. A cook who feels respected stays for years.
