## Cooking for health: diabetes, blood pressure and heart care

Gujranwala's rich food culture comes with a cost that many older residents know well: diabetes, high blood pressure and heart disease are common. When parents live alone or with a small household, their cook has a direct effect on their health. A good cook for an elderly parent uses less ghee and salt, offers whole wheat roti, cooks more vegetables and lentils, avoids frying every day, and keeps sweets for special occasions. These changes do not have to make food boring. A skilled cook knows how to keep flavour with spices, lemon and fresh herbs while cutting back on the things the doctor has warned about.

Share the doctor's advice with us, and we will look for cooks who have cooked for patients with similar conditions. During the trial, ask for one meal cooked to the doctor's guidance and watch how your parents respond to it.

## Kitchen hygiene and food safety

During the trial, notice whether the cook washes hands, keeps raw meat separate, covers food and cleans up properly. Gujranwala summers are hot and humid, and good kitchen habits prevent stomach illnesses.

## Seasons in the Gujranwala kitchen

Summers bring heat and humidity, so a working exhaust fan and good storage matter. Winters bring fog, saag and makki ki roti, gajar ka halwa and rich meat dishes. Gas pressure can drop in winter, and experienced cooks plan around it.

## Ramadan and Eid
