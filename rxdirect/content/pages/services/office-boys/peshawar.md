## Qehwa, tea and Pashtun hospitality

In Peshawar, how a guest is received says a lot about the office. A good office boy greets visitors respectfully, offers water at once, serves qehwa or tea promptly with dry fruit or biscuits where that is the custom, and refills cups without being asked. Elders and senior visitors are served first. The office boy stays out of meetings unless called and never repeats what they overhear. We brief office boys on these customs and look for references that mention good conduct with guests.

## Banking and errands

An office boy in Peshawar deposits cheques at bank branches in Saddar, University Town and the Cantt, pays bills, collects documents and drops parcels at courier offices. Good practice protects both the office and the office boy: a register of every errand, receipts for every payment, deposit slips for every bank visit and no large cash amounts carried without a reason. Vary the timing of bank runs where possible, and start new staff with small errands.

## Office boy, cleaner or receptionist?

A cleaner focuses on floors, washrooms and dusting, usually before or after working hours. A receptionist answers calls and manages visitors. An office boy covers the pantry, errands, photocopies, courier and general support, with light cleaning in smaller offices. Small offices often manage with one capable office boy, while larger offices and projects do better with a separate cleaner.

## Pantry hygiene

Ask the office boy to wash cups with hot water and soap, wipe counters daily, keep milk and food in the fridge, throw out anything stale and empty bins every evening. Make sure the kettle, gas stove and heaters are switched off before locking up.

## Winter and summer

Peshawar winters are cold, and offices rely on gas heaters. The office boy should switch heaters on before staff arrive, keep rooms ventilated and switch everything off at night. Summers are hot and dusty, so provide water and avoid sending the office boy on long errands in the afternoon heat.
