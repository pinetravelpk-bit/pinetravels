## Hire domestic couple in Pakistan: two trusted people, one household

For large homes, farmhouses and houses that stay empty part of the year, hiring two separate workers who do not know each other can create problems. They may not get along, one may leave and the other follows, or one lives in while the other commutes from far away. A domestic couple solves many of these issues. A husband and wife who work together, live together on the property and share responsibility often bring stability that two individual hires cannot.

## Live in domestic couple Pakistan: accommodation

A live in domestic couple in Pakistan needs proper accommodation. At minimum:

- A private room or small quarter with a door that locks
- A bathroom for their use
- Basic furniture: bed, cupboard, fan and light
- Kitchen access or a small cooking area
- Privacy from the main household

For couples with children, more space is needed. Good accommodation is one of the strongest reasons couples stay. Poor accommodation is one of the main reasons they leave.

## Setting clear duties for each partner

The most common problem with couples is unclear division of work. Write down each partner's duties, hours and who they report to. For example: wife cooks lunch and dinner and keeps the main house clean; husband drives the family, maintains the garden and secures the gate at night. Review after the first month. Many families keep a simple weekly checklist for each partner on the kitchen wall for the first few weeks, which helps new couples settle into the routine and gives the family an easy way to see what has been done.

Also agree on what happens if one partner falls ill or needs leave. Usually the other covers essential duties for short periods, but this should not become permanent without discussion.

## Couples with children

Many couples have young children. Some families are happy for children to live on the property; others are not. Be honest about your preference early. If children will stay, agree on space, noise, play areas and schooling. Children of staff should be treated kindly; many families help with school fees, which builds strong loyalty.

## Why families choose couples over two separate hires

Families who have tried both often prefer couples for a few practical reasons. A couple living on the property gives the house a steady presence day and night, which matters for security. Leave is easier to plan, because the couple usually travels home together at fixed times, rather than two workers taking leave at different moments. Couples tend to be more settled, since their family life is on the property rather than split between the job and a home elsewhere. And there is less friction, because two strangers do not have to learn to share a quarter and a kitchen.

There are trade-offs. If one partner is excellent and the other weak, you may have to accept both or lose both. Accommodation must be larger and more private. And if the couple has a serious disagreement or a family emergency, both may need time off at once. Knowing these trade-offs at the start helps you decide.

## Managing leave and Eid

Couples usually want to visit their families together, most often at Eid and for weddings or funerals in the village. Agree on annual leave at the start, typically around two weeks a year plus emergency leave. Plan for cover during these periods: a temporary helper, a guard for the gate or a relative staying in the house. For farmhouses and empty homes, it is especially important not to leave the property unattended while the couple is away. We can arrange temporary cover if you give us notice.

## Fair pay for both partners

Some families pay the husband a full salary and treat the wife's work as an extra. This is unfair and usually leads to resentment. If both partners are working, both should be paid, either separately or as a clearly agreed combined salary that reflects both jobs. Paying each partner directly into their own account or wallet is a good practice. It respects both people and avoids disputes within the couple about money.

For farmhouses outside city limits, registering with the local police station is a sensible step even if it is not demanded.
