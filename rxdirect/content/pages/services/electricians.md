## Residential electrician Pakistan: common problems we fix

A residential electrician in Pakistan sees the same problems again and again:

**Tripping breakers in summer.** Usually too many heavy appliances on one circuit. The fix is redistributing load and using correctly rated breakers, not just replacing the breaker with a bigger one, which is dangerous.

**Burnt sockets.** Caused by loose connections, cheap sockets or overloading. Replace with good quality sockets and check the wiring behind.

**Flickering lights.** Often a loose neutral or failing connection. Needs checking before it causes damage.

**UPS not charging.** Can be battery age, wiring or the UPS itself. A good electrician diagnoses before replacing.

**Shocks from appliances.** Usually missing or faulty earthing. This is dangerous and should be fixed immediately.

**Voltage problems.** Low or high voltage damages appliances. Stabilisers protect sensitive equipment.

## Electrical technician Pakistan: safety rules we follow

An electrical technician in Pakistan working through RX Direct follows basic safety rules:

- The supply to the circuit is switched off before work starts
- Wire gauge matches the load: thicker wire for AC and geyser circuits
- Breakers are rated correctly for the wire and load
- Joints are made properly inside boxes, never left taped in walls
- Earthing is provided for appliances that need it
- Everything is tested before the electrician leaves

If an electrician suggests skipping any of these to save money, it is a warning sign.

## Before summer: an electrical check

Summer puts the heaviest load on home wiring. Before May, it is worth having an electrician check:

- Air conditioner circuits and breakers
- Main DB box connections, which loosen over time
- UPS batteries and connections for load-shedding season
- Water pump and motor connections
- Any socket that feels warm when in use

A pre-summer check costs far less than repairing fire damage or replacing burnt appliances.

## Electrical work in older houses

Houses built twenty or thirty years ago in areas like Satellite Town, Model Town, PECHS, Gulberg and the older sectors of Islamabad were wired for far fewer appliances than families use today. Rubber insulated wires harden and crack with age, aluminium wiring causes loose connections, and DB boxes may have no proper breakers at all. Rewiring is a bigger job, but in older homes it is often the safest long term choice. An electrician can assess the condition and suggest whether partial or full rewiring is needed.

## Cutting electricity bills safely

With electricity tariffs rising every year, many families ask electricians how to reduce their bills. Practical steps an electrician can help with include replacing old tube lights and bulbs with LED fittings, fitting energy efficient fans with modern regulators, checking that air conditioners have proper wiring so they run efficiently, separating heavy loads onto their own circuits, and finding hidden faults such as earth leakage that waste power and trip breakers. Inverter air conditioners and efficient motors also help, though they are a larger investment.

A useful habit is checking your meter at night when most appliances are off. If it is still moving quickly, something is drawing power that should not be, and an electrician can trace it.

## Electrical safety for families with children

Children are naturally curious about sockets and wires. Simple steps reduce the risk: socket covers or shuttered sockets in children's reach, no loose extension cords across floors, switchboards fixed firmly to the wall, and an RCD or earth leakage breaker on the main board that cuts power quickly if someone gets a shock. Ask your electrician to fit an RCD if your DB box does not have one. It is one of the most effective safety devices in any home.

## Signs you should call an electrician now

Some electrical problems can wait for a convenient visit. Others should not. Call an electrician urgently, and switch off the affected circuit or the main supply, if you notice a burning smell from a socket or switchboard, scorch marks or melting on plugs, sparks when plugging something in, a buzzing sound from the DB box, a mild shock from a tap, appliance or metal fixture, or a breaker that trips again immediately after being reset. These are signs of a fault that can lead to fire or serious injury.
