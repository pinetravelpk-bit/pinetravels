## Safe City, challans and good habits

Lahore's Safe City cameras issue e-challans for signal violations, speeding, lane violations and seat belt offences. A good driver keeps to limits on Canal Road and the Ring Road, stops at signals, uses seat belts, keeps the phone away while driving and parks legally. Agree in advance who pays challans caused by careless driving.

## Smog season and visibility

From November to January, Lahore's smog can drop visibility sharply, especially in the early morning and late evening. Accidents rise on the Ring Road and motorway. A careful driver slows down, uses fog lights properly, keeps a safe distance and avoids unnecessary late night trips. Families with children or elderly parents with asthma often keep car windows closed and use the air recirculation setting. We ask drivers how they handle smog during interviews.

## Summer heat and long waits

In Lahore's summer, drivers often wait in parked cars outside schools, offices and hospitals in 45 degree heat. Running the engine for air conditioning wastes fuel; switching it off risks heatstroke. Agree a sensible rule, and where possible, let the driver wait in shade or indoors. A driver who is looked after in June stays loyal.

## School runs

Share exact timings, gate procedures and who can collect the children. Give the school the driver's name, photo and number. Ask the driver never to leave children alone in the car, never to stop for personal errands with children inside, and always to use seat belts. Lahore's school zones are crowded, and a driver who arrives early and parks properly avoids stress for everyone.

## Fuel, logs and honesty

Agree on how fuel is bought and recorded, with receipts and odometer readings in a notebook or shared message. Check weekly. Honest drivers welcome a clear system. If fuel use suddenly rises, ask calmly. Often there is a simple reason, such as a road closure or a new school route, but it is always better to talk early.

## Drivers for elderly parents

Many Lahore drivers mainly drive elderly parents to hospitals, mosques and family visits. These roles need patience, help in and out of the car, waiting at hospitals and gentle driving over the city's speed breakers. Overseas families often hire a driver so a parent can stay independent and keep their routines.

## Weddings and late nights

Lahore's wedding season keeps drivers busy late into the night. Mehndis, baraats and walimas at marquees on Canal Road, Raiwind Road and the Ring Road often end after midnight, and drivers wait for hours in crowded parking lots. Agree on how late-night duty will be paid, make sure the driver has eaten, and let them rest the next morning if possible. A driver who is treated fairly through wedding season is one who will still be with you next year.

## Motorway trips

Many Lahore families travel to Islamabad, Faisalabad or their villages by motorway. Motorway driving needs lane discipline, steady speeds, rest stops on long journeys and care in fog. Agree on daily allowances for out of city trips. For overnight stays, arrange proper accommodation and meals for the driver, and avoid asking them to drive back the same night after a long day. Tired driving on the M2 or M3 is one of the biggest risks on these journeys.

## Monsoon flooding

Lahore's monsoon brings sudden downpours that flood underpasses and low lying roads such as parts of Lakshmi Chowk, Garhi Shahu, Gulberg and the Canal Road underpasses within minutes. A sensible driver knows which routes to avoid after heavy rain, never drives through water deeper than the wheel hubs, and waits rather than risking the car and passengers. Electrical faults and stalled engines are common after flooding, so it is worth checking the car once the rain stops.
