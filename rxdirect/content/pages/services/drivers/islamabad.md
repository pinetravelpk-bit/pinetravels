## Fuel, logs and the company car

Fuel is one of the biggest running costs for any family with a driver, and one of the most common sources of disputes. Keep it simple: fill up at one or two known stations, keep receipts, and ask the driver to note the odometer reading at each fill. A small notebook in the glove box or a shared WhatsApp note is enough. Over a month, you will see a clear pattern of kilometres per litre for your car. If the numbers suddenly change, ask about it calmly. Honest drivers appreciate a clear system because it protects them from suspicion. For corporate cars, the same rules apply, with a monthly fuel report shared with the office.

## Monsoon, fog and winter driving

Islamabad's weather tests drivers in different ways through the year. Monsoon downpours flood underpasses and parts of the Expressway within minutes, and a careful driver knows which routes to avoid. Winter fog on the motorway and around the airport can drop visibility close to zero in December and January. Trips to Murree and the Galyat in snow need chains, slow driving and real experience. During interviews we ask drivers how they handle these conditions, and we prefer drivers whose references mention calm, safe driving in bad weather.

## Car care and maintenance

A good driver checks oil, coolant and tyre pressure, keeps the car clean, notices unusual sounds early, and reminds you when servicing, token tax or registration renewal is due. In Islamabad's cold winters, they check the battery and antifreeze; in summer, the air conditioning and coolant.

## Live-in or live-out?

**Live-out drivers** suit most Islamabad families with fixed schedules. **Live-in drivers** suit homes with servant quarters in DHA, Bahria Town or larger F and E sector houses, where early airport runs and late events are common.

## Common reasons drivers leave

Drivers usually leave because of unpaid overtime, very long days with no break, no proper day off or being blamed for traffic. A fair schedule and agreed overtime solve most of these. Simple courtesies help too: letting the driver eat properly on long days, giving a place to rest while waiting, and not calling at midnight for small errands.
