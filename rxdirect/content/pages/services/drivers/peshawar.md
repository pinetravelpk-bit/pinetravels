## Security awareness

Good Peshawar drivers share some habits. They vary routes when the family asks, avoid stopping unnecessarily, keep doors locked and windows up in crowded areas, never discuss the family's schedule or guests with anyone, stay alert at signals and checkpoints, and report anything suspicious straight away. During interviews we ask drivers how they handle these situations, and we check references specifically on discretion.

## Checkpoints and documents

Peshawar and the roads around it have regular police and security checkpoints. A driver should always carry their CNIC and licence, keep the car's registration documents in order, and handle checks politely and calmly. A driver who becomes argumentative at a checkpoint can create trouble for the whole family.

## The M1 and trips north

Many Peshawar families drive to Islamabad on the M1 for work, flights and family. Motorway driving needs lane discipline, steady speeds and rest stops. Summer trips to Swat, Kalam, Chitral or the Kaghan valley need hill driving experience: careful overtaking, correct gear use on steep descents and patience on narrow roads. In winter, fog on the motorway and snow in the hills need extra care. Agree daily allowances and accommodation for overnight trips.

## School runs

Share exact timings, school gate procedures and who can collect the children. Give the school the driver's name and photo. Ask the driver never to leave children alone in the car, never to stop for personal errands with children inside, and always to use seat belts.

## Fuel, logs and honesty

Agree on how fuel is bought and recorded, with receipts and odometer readings. Check weekly, and ask calmly if something seems unusual. Trips to Islamabad or the north change fuel use a lot from month to month, so look at the overall pattern rather than single days. A clear system protects both the family and an honest driver from misunderstandings.
