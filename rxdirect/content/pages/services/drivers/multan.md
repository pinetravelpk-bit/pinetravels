## Family driver Multan: what the job includes

A family driver in Multan usually handles a mix of city and longer journeys. The city side includes school drop-offs and pick-ups, office and shop drops for family members, hospital and clinic visits for elderly parents, grocery runs and errands, and evening trips to relatives, mosques and weddings.

The longer side includes visits to family farms and villages across South Punjab, motorway trips to Lahore, Faisalabad and Islamabad, airport runs to Multan International Airport, and sometimes trips to Bahawalpur or Dera Ghazi Khan. On top of driving, a good driver checks the car daily, keeps it clean despite the dust, keeps a fuel log and reminds the family about servicing and token tax.

## Driving in Multan's heat

Multan's summer is one of the hottest in Pakistan, and it affects drivers in several ways. Cars overheat in traffic, tyres wear faster on hot roads, and drivers waiting in parked cars face real danger of heatstroke. A sensible family gives the driver a shaded or indoor place to wait, cold water and a clear rule on running the air conditioning. Long trips are best planned for early morning or evening. During interviews we ask drivers how they look after the car and themselves in extreme heat.

## Farm trips and rural roads

Rural roads around Multan can be narrow, broken and shared with tractors, trolleys, animals and motorcycles. Driving them safely needs experience, patience and a car in good condition. If farm trips are part of the job, tell us how often and how far, and we will shortlist drivers who know these roads. Agree on meal allowances and, for overnight stays, proper accommodation for the driver.

## Motorway and fog

The M4 and M5 connect Multan with Faisalabad, Lahore, Islamabad and Sukkur. Motorway driving needs lane discipline, steady speeds and rest stops on long journeys. In winter, dense fog can close sections of the motorway and make rural roads dangerous. A careful driver slows down, uses fog lights and does not insist on travelling when visibility is poor.

## Weddings, Urs and late nights

Multan's social calendar keeps drivers busy after dark. Wedding functions at marquees along Bosan Road and the bypass often end after midnight, and the annual Urs at the shrines of Bahauddin Zakariya and Shah Rukn-e-Alam brings heavy crowds and road closures around the old city. A driver who knows how to avoid congested areas, finds safe parking and gets the family home without fuss is worth a lot during these periods. Agree in advance how late-night duty will be paid, make sure the driver has eaten, and let them rest the next morning when possible. Families who look after drivers through these busy nights usually keep them for years.

## School runs

Share exact timings, school gate procedures and who can collect the children. Give the school the driver's name and photo. Ask the driver never to leave children alone in the car, never to stop for personal errands with children inside, and always to use seat belts.

## Fuel, logs and honesty

Agree on how fuel is bought and recorded, with receipts and odometer readings in a notebook. Check weekly. Farm trips can make fuel use vary, so look at monthly patterns and ask calmly if something seems unusual.

Many Multan drivers mainly drive elderly parents to hospitals, mosques and relatives, often while their children work in other cities or abroad. ## Car care

Multan's dust and heat are hard on cars. A good driver checks oil, coolant, tyres and air filters regularly, keeps the car clean and reminds you about servicing, token tax and registration renewal. Before summer, a coolant and air conditioning check prevents breakdowns on the bypass in 45 degree heat, and before farm season, a tyre and suspension check saves trouble on rough roads.

## Live-in or live-out?

**Live-out drivers** suit families with fixed schedules in central areas. **Live-in drivers** suit larger homes with quarters, families who travel to farms often, and households where early starts and late events are common.

## Common reasons drivers leave
