## Cash, banking and honesty

Many Faisalabad drivers handle cash: bank deposits, payments at the cloth market, fuel and errands. Clear rules protect everyone. Keep cash amounts reasonable, ask for receipts, vary the timing of bank runs, and have the driver call when a deposit is done. Never send a new driver alone with large sums in the first weeks. Where possible, move larger payments to bank transfers or mobile banking. It is safer for the business and removes a heavy responsibility from the driver, who would otherwise be an obvious target on busy market days.

## Motorway driving

The M3 to Lahore and M4 to Multan and Islamabad are part of many Faisalabad drivers' routines. Motorway driving needs lane discipline, steady speeds, rest stops and extra care in winter fog, which is often dense around Faisalabad. Agree on daily allowances for out of city trips, and do not ask drivers to return the same night after a long day.

## Fog season

From December to January, fog around Faisalabad can bring visibility close to zero, especially on the motorway and rural roads. Motorways are sometimes closed. A careful driver slows down, uses fog lights, keeps a safe distance and does not insist on travelling when conditions are dangerous.

## Weddings and late nights

Faisalabad's wedding season fills marquees along Canal Road and Sargodha Road, and functions often end after midnight. ## Summer heat and waiting

Faisalabad summers are long and hot. Drivers often wait outside mills, banks and schools. Agree a sensible rule on air conditioning and let drivers wait in shade or indoors when possible. Running the engine for hours wastes fuel, but sitting in a closed car in 44 degree heat is dangerous. Many mills and offices have a waiting area for drivers, and offering water and a place to sit makes a real difference in June and July.

## School runs

Share timings, gate procedures and who can collect the children. Ask the driver never to leave children alone in the car and always to use seat belts. Give the school the driver's name, photo and phone number, and agree what to do if a child is late or the school closes early. Faisalabad's school roads, especially around Jail Road, Satiana Road and the canal, get crowded at dismissal time, and a driver who arrives a few minutes early and parks properly avoids a lot of stress.

## Fuel, logs and records

Agree on how fuel is bought and recorded. A notebook with odometer readings and receipts, checked weekly, keeps things clear. For company vehicles, a monthly fuel report to the office works well. If fuel use suddenly changes, ask calmly. There is often a simple explanation, such as more motorway trips that month, but talking early prevents doubt from building up.
