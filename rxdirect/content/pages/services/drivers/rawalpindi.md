Most Rawalpindi drivers spend a lot of time in Islamabad. ## Fuel, logs and honesty

Agree on how fuel is bought and recorded. A notebook with odometer readings and receipts, checked weekly, keeps things clear. Honest drivers welcome the system because it protects them. Sudden changes in fuel use are worth a calm conversation. Often there is an innocent reason, such as a new school route or heavy traffic diversions, but it is better to talk about it early than to let doubt build up quietly.

## Monsoon and winter driving

Rawalpindi's monsoon floods the Nullah Leh area, Committee Chowk and low lying roads quickly. A good driver avoids these routes in heavy rain and never drives through deep water. Winter brings fog on the motorway and GT Road, and snow on the road to Murree. We ask drivers about their experience in these conditions and prefer those with references for calm, safe driving.

## School runs

Share exact timings, school procedures and who can collect the children. Ask the driver never to leave children alone in the car and never to stop for personal errands with children inside. Seat belts for every child, every trip. Give the school the driver's name, photo and number, and tell the driver exactly what to do if a child is not at the gate on time.

## Drivers for elderly parents

Many drivers in Rawalpindi mainly drive elderly parents to hospitals, mosques and relatives. These roles need patience, help in and out of the car, waiting at hospitals and gentle driving. Overseas families often hire a driver so a parent can stay independent. ## Old city lanes and parking

Parts of Rawalpindi, especially around Raja Bazaar, Saddar, Bohar Bazaar and the older mohallas, have narrow lanes, heavy foot traffic and almost no parking. A driver who knows these streets can drop family members close to where they need to go, wait without blocking traffic, and find a way out when a lane is suddenly closed by a wedding tent or a delivery truck. This local knowledge saves time and stress, and it is something we test by asking drivers to describe how they would reach specific places in the old city.

## Trips to Murree, Kashmir and the north

Rawalpindi families often travel to Murree, Patriata, Muzaffarabad or further north, and many drivers have family in Kahuta, Kotli Sattian or Azad Kashmir. Mountain roads need experience: careful overtaking, correct use of gears on steep descents, and patience behind slow trucks. In winter, snow and black ice make the Murree Expressway dangerous. If hill trips are part of the job, we shortlist drivers with proven hill driving experience. Agree daily allowances and accommodation for overnight trips in advance.

## Car care

A good driver checks oil, coolant and tyres, keeps the car clean and reminds you about servicing, token tax and registration. Dusty Rawalpindi roads mean air filters need regular attention, and monsoon puddles hide potholes that damage tyres and suspension.

## Live-in or live-out?

**Live-out drivers** suit most families. **Live-in drivers** suit Bahria Town, DHA and Cantt homes with quarters, where early starts and late events are common.

## Common reasons drivers leave

Unpaid overtime, very long days, no day off and being blamed for traffic. A fair schedule and agreed overtime keep good drivers for years. So do small courtesies: proper meals on long days, a place to rest while waiting at hospitals or weddings, and not calling late at night for errands that could wait until morning. Drivers talk to each other, and families known for treating drivers well find it much easier to hire.
