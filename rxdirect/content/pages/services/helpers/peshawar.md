## Domestic helper Peshawar: typical duties

A domestic helper in Peshawar often divides time between the kitchen, the hujra and errands. In the kitchen, that means washing dishes, chopping, cleaning meat, kneading dough and keeping the cook supplied. In the hujra, it means serving qehwa and tea, bringing food, clearing up and keeping the room clean and ready. Around the house, helpers sweep courtyards and roofs, carry gas cylinders, take out rubbish and help with heavier chores.

Errands are a big part of the job: meat from a trusted butcher, vegetables and fruit, fresh naan from the tandoor, dry fruit for guests, medicines and supplies from the old city bazaars or Hayatabad's markets.

## Hujra service and hospitality

Serving guests in the hujra is an important job in a Peshawar home. A good helper greets guests respectfully, serves qehwa and tea promptly, keeps cups and plates moving, brings food at the right time and clears up quietly. They know not to listen in or talk about what they hear. We brief helpers on these customs and look for references that mention good conduct with guests.

## Winter chores

Peshawar winters are cold. Helpers carry LPG cylinders, refill heaters, bring in wood or coal in older homes, help air quilts on sunny days and keep entrances clean during the rainy months. They should also know never to leave gas heaters burning in closed rooms and to report any smell of gas immediately. In the rainy months, they keep courtyards swept and drains clear so water does not collect near the house.

## Kitchen helpers for big families

Large Peshawar families cook in big quantities, especially for guests. A kitchen helper preps, serves and washes up, keeping the cook from working endless hours. During Ramadan and at Eid, especially Eid ul Adha with large amounts of fresh meat, a helper is close to essential.

## Weddings and gatherings

Peshawar weddings, jirgas and condolence gatherings can draw large numbers. Temporary helpers for these occasions serve guests, manage dishes and keep the house and hujra in order. Book early. For large gatherings, a team of two or three temporary helpers working under your regular staff keeps service smooth, ensures guests are never left waiting for tea or food, and means the house and hujra are clean again by the next morning.

## Helpers for elderly parents

Many Peshawar parents live at home while sons work in the Gulf or other cities. A helper can keep the house tidy, make tea and simple meals, fetch medicines and groceries, and provide company. If more care is needed, such as help with bathing, walking or medicines, a [caretaker](/services/caretakers/peshawar) may be better.

## Eid ul Adha and fresh meat

Eid ul Adha is one of the busiest weeks of the year in a Peshawar home. Animals are bought days in advance and need feeding and care, the sacrifice brings large amounts of fresh meat, and that meat must be cleaned, cut, divided and distributed to relatives, neighbours and the poor. Helpers do much of the heavy work: looking after the animal, helping the butcher, washing the courtyard afterwards, carrying meat to the kitchen and delivering portions around the neighbourhood. They also help the cook with the first day's kaleji and tikka for the steady stream of guests. If your helper will be with you over Eid, agree on the extra work and any extra pay in advance.

## Market runs in the old city

Qissa Khwani, Namak Mandi, Andar Shehr and the other bazaars of the old city are where many Peshawar families buy meat, spices, dry fruit and household goods. They are crowded and hard to navigate. A helper who knows these markets, can find trusted shops and bargain sensibly saves the family a great deal of time. During interviews we ask helpers which markets they know well.

## Training a new helper

Helpers who come from villages may need guidance in the first weeks: how you like guests served, how dishes are stacked, where supplies are kept and which shops to use. Correct calmly and praise good work. Many helpers grow into trusted cooks, drivers or chowkidars over time.

## Errands and money

Give a fixed amount for each errand, ask for change and receipts, and keep a simple record. Avoid sending a new helper with large sums early on, and never share bank cards or PIN codes. For regular purchases such as meat, dry fruit and gas cylinders, agree on the shops and keep a simple notebook of every payment. It protects an honest helper as much as it protects you.

## Live-in helpers

Live-in helpers need a clean space, bathroom access, proper meals, heating in winter, a fan in summer and a weekly day off. Many come from Charsadda, Mardan, Swat or Dir and travel home for Eid and family weddings. Plan leave in advance, agree return dates, and keep in touch while they are away, since mountain roads can delay their return.

## Why helpers leave

Helpers leave when treated as the lowest person in the house, when duties keep growing without extra pay, or when they get no day off. Fair treatment keeps them for years. Peshawar families value loyalty, and a helper who is respected, fed well and given time to visit family often stays long enough to become a trusted part of the household.
