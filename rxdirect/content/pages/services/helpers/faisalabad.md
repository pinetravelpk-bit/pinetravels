## Domestic helper Faisalabad: typical duties

A domestic helper in Faisalabad usually supports the cook and maid. In the kitchen, that means washing the mountain of dishes a large family produces, chopping vegetables, cleaning meat, kneading dough, grinding masalas and serving at meals. Around the house, it means sweeping, mopping smaller areas, cleaning the courtyard and roof, taking out rubbish and helping with laundry.

Errands are a big part of the job: vegetables and fruit from the local mandi, meat from the butcher, bread and milk, medicines, and small purchases from the bazaars around the Clock Tower. In many homes the helper also carries lunch tiffins to family members at the factory, office or shop, and serves tea to the steady flow of guests.

## Training a new helper

Helpers often arrive with limited experience, especially if they come straight from a village. The first two weeks matter most. Show the helper how you want dishes washed and stacked, where cleaning supplies are kept, how to serve guests and which shops to use for errands. Correct mistakes calmly and early, and praise good work. Within a month, most helpers settle into the household's rhythm, and many grow into skilled cooks or maids over time.

## Errands and money

Give a fixed amount for each errand, ask for change and receipts, and keep a simple record. Avoid sending a new helper with large sums early on, and never share bank cards or PIN codes. In business households, where cash moves around more than in most homes, a simple notebook of every rupee given and spent protects both the family and an honest helper.

## Live-in helpers

Live-in helpers need a clean space, bathroom access, proper meals, a fan in summer and a weekly day off. If the helper shares a quarter with other staff, make sure there is enough space and that the arrangement is fair. Many live-in helpers come from villages around Jhang, Toba Tek Singh and Chiniot and go home at Eid and harvest, so plan cover for those weeks.

## Why helpers leave

Helpers leave when treated as the lowest person in the house, when duties keep growing without extra pay, or when they get no day off. Fair treatment keeps them for years. In Faisalabad, where families value loyalty, a helper who is treated well often stays long enough to become a trusted cook, driver or maid in the same household.
