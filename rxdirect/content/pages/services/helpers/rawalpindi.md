## Domestic helper Rawalpindi: typical duties

A domestic helper in Rawalpindi usually supports the rest of the household. In the kitchen, that means washing dishes, chopping, cleaning meat, kneading dough and serving. Around the house, it means sweeping, dusting, mopping smaller areas, taking out rubbish, cleaning the courtyard, roof and porch, and helping the maid with laundry.

Errands are a big part of the job in Rawalpindi: groceries from Commercial Market or the local sabzi wala, medicines from the pharmacy, bread from the bakery, bills at the bank and small purchases from Raja Bazaar. When guests arrive, which is often, the helper serves tea and clears up.

## Home helper Rawalpindi: working alongside other staff

A home helper in Rawalpindi usually works with a cook, maid, driver or batman. Decide who gives the helper instructions, split duties in writing, and do not let senior staff treat the helper as their personal servant. Pay the helper directly. In officers' homes, make sure the helper understands the household's routine and the batman's role, so duties do not overlap.

## Kitchen helpers for joint families

In a joint family of ten or more, the cook cannot do everything. A kitchen helper takes over the preparation and cleaning so the cook can focus on cooking, and meals arrive on time. In Ramadan, when sehri and iftar double the work, a kitchen helper is close to essential. If your cook seems overwhelmed, a helper is usually the answer before you lose the cook.

## Helpers for roofs, courtyards and outdoor areas

Many Rawalpindi homes have roofs used for drying clothes and water tanks, courtyards that collect dust, and porches and garages that need regular cleaning. Bahria Town and DHA houses often have lawns too. A helper can keep these areas tidy, wash cars and help the gardener with heavier work. Dust settles quickly on Rawalpindi roofs and porches, so a daily sweep makes a visible difference.

## Helpers in officers' and military homes

Rawalpindi's military households often have a batman or personal attendant who looks after the officer's uniform, room and daily needs, and a cook who runs the kitchen. A helper in these homes fills the remaining gaps: cleaning, errands, serving guests at dinners and helping the cook on busy days. Military homes tend to run on fixed schedules and expect discipline, punctuality and a tidy appearance. We look for helpers whose references come from similar homes, and we brief them on the routine before they start. Clear boundaries with the batman and cook keep everyone working together without friction.

## Monsoon and winter work

Rawalpindi's monsoon brings mud into courtyards and porches, and roof drains need clearing before heavy rain. A helper can keep entrances clean, sweep water off roofs and courtyards after a downpour and help move things away from leaks. In winter, helpers often carry LPG cylinders, bring in firewood or coal in older homes, help air out quilts on sunny days and keep heaters clean. These seasonal tasks are easy to forget until they are urgent, and a helper who handles them without being asked is a real asset.

## Market runs in the old city

Raja Bazaar, Saddar, Moti Bazaar and the wholesale markets of the old city are where many Rawalpindi families buy groceries, cloth, kitchen supplies and wedding shopping. They are crowded, noisy and hard to park in. A helper who knows these markets, can find the right shop quickly, bargain sensibly and bring back the right items saves the family hours. During interviews we ask helpers which markets they know and how they handle money on errands.

## Errands and handling money

Give a fixed amount for each errand, ask for change and receipts, and keep a simple record. Avoid sending a new helper with large sums in the first weeks, and never share bank cards or PIN codes. Honest helpers welcome a clear system for money, because it protects them from doubt when prices change or change is short. A small notebook kept in the kitchen, with purchases written down each day, is all most families need.

## Live-in helpers

Live-in helpers need a clean space in the servant quarter, bathroom access, proper meals, a heater in winter and a fan in summer, and a weekly day off. If they share a quarter with other staff, make sure the arrangement is fair and comfortable.

## Training and growth

Helpers learn on the job. Show them how things are done, correct calmly and praise good work. Many helpers grow into cooks or maids over time, giving your household continuity and giving the helper a better future with the same family.

## Why helpers leave

Helpers leave when they are treated as the lowest person in the house, when duties keep growing without extra pay, or when they get no day off. Fair treatment keeps them for years. Simple things matter: the same food as the rest of the staff, a proper rest in the afternoon, a fixed day off, and being spoken to politely by everyone in the house, including other workers. Families known in their neighbourhood for treating helpers well rarely struggle to find good ones.
