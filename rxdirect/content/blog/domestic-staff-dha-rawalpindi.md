DHA Rawalpindi has expanded across several phases, each with its own gate, security desk and resident-staff registration process. For households here, hiring domestic staff isn't just about finding someone reliable, it's also about finding someone who can clear the entry process smoothly and consistently.

## What DHA Rawalpindi's entry process usually means for staff

Most phases require staff to register with the security office using a CNIC, and many issue a staff ID card or sticker for regular entry. This works best when the staff member's documentation is already in order, which is one more reason we verify CNIC and address details for every candidate before placement, rather than leaving it for move-in day.

## Household types we see across DHA Rawalpindi's phases

DHA Rawalpindi has a notably high share of military and ex-military families given its proximity to Rawalpindi Cantt, alongside a growing number of civilian professional households in the newer phases further out. Military families often already have a clear sense of what documentation and verification they expect from staff, having dealt with cantonment-style entry requirements before, which makes the registration step at the gate feel routine rather than an extra hurdle.

## Documents to have ready before a candidate starts

Beyond the security desk's own registration requirements, it's worth keeping your own file on any staff member: their original CNIC (photocopy kept separately), a recent photo, their current address, and the phone number of at least one previous employer. This isn't just for our verification process, if a phase's security office ever asks for additional confirmation later, having this on hand means you're not scrambling to track the candidate down for details.

## How we check references properly

A written letter from a previous employer is easy to produce and hard to verify, so we don't rely on one alone. Our process involves calling the previous employer directly and asking specific questions: how long the person worked there, why they left, whether there were any concerns about reliability or honesty, and whether the employer would hire them again. Genuine references tend to answer with specific detail; vague or overly rehearsed answers are a signal to check a second reference before moving forward.

## Red flags we watch for

A few things make us slow down and verify more carefully, regardless of how well a candidate interviews: an address that isn't consistent when asked about twice, reluctance to provide a previous employer's contact number, or a work history with unexplained gaps. In DHA Rawalpindi specifically, where the security desk will eventually cross-check CNIC details anyway, catching inconsistencies early avoids both a bad placement and a delay at the gate.
