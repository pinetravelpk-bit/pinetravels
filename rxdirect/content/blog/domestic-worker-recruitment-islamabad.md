Families usually see only the end of the hiring process: a shortlist, an interview, a trial. Behind that shortlist is a longer story. Someone found the worker, checked who they were, called their previous employers, tested their skills and talked to them about the job. That is domestic worker recruitment, and how it is done in Islamabad shapes the quality, honesty and staying power of the people who end up in your home.

This article takes you behind the scenes. It explains how responsible recruitment works, why ethical practices matter to families as much as to workers, and what job seekers in Islamabad and Rawalpindi should know before they register with any agency.

## Where domestic workers in Islamabad come from

Islamabad's domestic workforce is drawn from across Pakistan. Many workers come from:

- **Northern Punjab:** Rawalpindi district, Attock, Chakwal, Jhelum
- **Southern Punjab:** Bahawalpur, Rahim Yar Khan, Muzaffargarh, Layyah
- **Khyber Pakhtunkhwa:** Mansehra, Abbottabad, Swat, Mardan, Kohat
- **Azad Kashmir and Gilgit-Baltistan**
- **Islamabad's own villages and suburbs:** Bhara Kahu, Tarlai, Golra, Sihala, Tarnol

Many live with relatives in Islamabad or Rawalpindi while looking for work; others travel specifically for a live-in job. Understanding where workers come from helps recruiters confirm addresses, contact families and match workers to households where they will feel comfortable.

## How domestic worker recruitment Islamabad agencies run finds workers

Good domestic worker recruitment in Islamabad does not rely on chance. Responsible agencies build a steady flow of candidates through:

1. **Referrals from placed workers.** Workers who were treated well often recommend relatives and friends. These candidates come with a built-in reference.
2. **Returning workers.** Staff whose employers moved abroad or no longer need help come back for a new placement, with a fresh reference from the last family.
3. **Walk-in registrations.** Workers visit the office in person with their documents, which allows face to face screening from the start.
4. **Online and phone registrations,** followed by an in-person interview before any placement.
5. **Community links** with mosques, local elders and community organisations in areas where many workers live.

## Screening: from registration to shortlist

Not every worker who registers is ready to be placed. A careful screening process filters candidates in stages.

### Stage 1: Documents

Original CNIC examined and matched, details recorded, expired cards flagged. Workers without a valid CNIC cannot be placed until they have one.

### Stage 2: Interview

An experienced recruiter talks with the worker about their background, past jobs, skills, expectations, family situation and reasons for seeking work. The interview also tests communication and attitude.

### Stage 3: References

Previous employers are called. The recruiter asks specific questions about duties, honesty, punctuality and the reason for leaving, and records the answers.

### Stage 4: Address confirmation

Current and permanent addresses are confirmed through visits, landlords or trusted contacts.

### Stage 5: Skills check

Practical tests suited to the role: cooking dishes, cleaning and ironing, a road test for drivers, childcare scenarios for nannies, clinical discussion for nurses.

### Stage 6: Profile and matching

Only after passing these stages does a worker's profile enter the pool from which shortlists are drawn. Matching then considers the family's location, hours, household and preferences.

A significant share of candidates do not pass every stage. That is the point of screening. Candidates who fall short are not always turned away for good. A worker whose CNIC has expired can come back once it is renewed. A helper who cannot yet be trusted with cooking can be placed in a role that fits their current skills. Being honest with candidates about why they were not shortlisted helps them improve, and many return months later ready for a better job.

## Why ethical recruitment matters

Domestic work in Pakistan has a long history of informal and sometimes exploitative recruitment. Middlemen who charge workers fees, hold their documents or push them from job to job to earn repeat commissions still exist. Ethical recruitment is the opposite, and it benefits families directly.

### No fees for workers

When workers pay a middleman to get a job, they start in debt. They may be pressured to leave early so the middleman can place them again and earn another fee. Families then face constant turnover. Ethical agencies charge the family, not the worker. RX Direct never charges workers to register or be placed.

### Full salary to the worker

When an agency takes a cut of the worker's monthly salary, the worker earns less than the family thinks they are paying. Underpaid workers leave. We recommend, and practise, salary paid directly and in full by the family to the worker.

### Honest job descriptions

Workers should know exactly what they are agreeing to: duties, hours, day off, living conditions and salary. Recruiters who promise easy jobs to fill vacancies create disappointment and early resignations.

### Documents stay with the worker

Families and agencies should keep copies, never originals. Holding a worker's CNIC or other documents is unfair and can be illegal.

### No child workers

Children cannot legally be employed as domestic workers. Every worker we recruit is an adult with a valid CNIC.

### Fair treatment after placement

Ethical recruitment continues after placement. Workers should have someone to contact if they are mistreated or if terms are not honoured. An agency that listens to workers as well as families catches problems early.

## What families gain from ethical recruitment

It might seem that ethical recruitment is mainly about protecting workers. It is, but it also gives families:

- **Workers who stay longer,** because they are not in debt or underpaid
- **Honest candidates,** because the process attracts people who want a genuine job
- **Fewer surprises,** because both sides know the terms from the start
- **Peace of mind,** knowing the person in your home was treated fairly to get there

## Domestic worker recruitment Islamabad timelines through the year

Demand for domestic workers in Islamabad rises and falls through the year:

- **Before Ramadan and Eid:** many workers go home, and families need replacements or temporary cover
- **After Eid:** a wave of workers returns looking for new placements, and families who lost staff are hiring
- **Wedding season (November to February):** high demand for cooks, servers and drivers
- **Summer holidays:** families travelling abroad may release staff, while others need extra childcare

Good recruiters plan ahead for these cycles so that shortlists are available when families need them.

## What recruiters look for in an interview

Documents and references show history. The interview shows the person. Experienced recruiters listen for a few things in particular:

- **Specific answers.** A genuine cook describes the dishes she made for a family in F-11 and how many people she cooked for. A weak candidate says only "I can cook everything".
- **Respectful talk about past employers.** Workers who speak angrily about every previous family often bring the same conflict to the next one.
- **Realistic expectations.** A worker who asks about hours and the day off is planning to stay. One who agrees to everything without question may leave when reality arrives.
- **Calm under simple pressure.** A question such as "what would you do if the baby fell from the bed?" shows how the candidate thinks when something goes wrong.
- **Honesty about gaps.** Time spent at home caring for a parent or working on the family's land is normal. Hiding it is a warning sign.

Recruiters also explain the job honestly and watch the reaction. A candidate who hesitates when told about late dinners or a large house is better identified now than in the second week of a placement.

## Preparing the worker before the first day

Good recruitment does not stop at the shortlist. Before a worker starts, we brief them on the family's household: the number of people, the main duties, the hours and day off, the salary and payment date, any house rules the family has shared, and how to travel to the house. For live-in workers, we explain what to bring and what the quarters include. Workers who arrive knowing what to expect settle faster and are far less likely to leave in the first week. We also remind them who to contact at our office if something is unclear or if a problem comes up, so small issues are raised early instead of turning into a sudden resignation.

## Matching beyond skills

Two cooks with equal skills can have very different outcomes in the same house. Matching considers more than the job title: the worker's language and the family's, comfort with pets, experience with large or small households, willingness to travel with the family, and whether the worker prefers a quiet home or a busy one. For elderly care, temperament often matters more than experience. For households with teenage children, maturity matters. Domestic worker recruitment Islamabad families benefit from most is the kind that asks these questions before sending a profile.

## For families: how to support good recruitment

Families play a part in recruitment quality too:

- **Give honest job descriptions** to the agency, including hours, duties and living conditions

- **Give honest feedback** after interviews and trials, so recruiters can improve matches
- **Provide references** for good workers when they leave, which helps them find the next job
- **Report problems** rather than simply dismissing a worker, so the agency can learn

## For workers: how to find domestic work safely in Islamabad

If you are looking for a job as a maid, cook, driver, nanny, caretaker, helper, guard, gardener or nurse in Islamabad or Rawalpindi, these steps will help:

1. **Keep your CNIC valid.** Renew it before it expires. You cannot be placed without it.
2. **Keep contact details of past employers.** Ask for a phone number when you leave a job on good terms.
3. **Never pay a fee for a job.** Genuine agencies charge employers, not workers.
4. **Never hand over your original documents.** Give copies only.
5. **Ask about the job clearly:** duties, hours, day off, salary, payment date and living conditions.
6. **Get the terms in writing** where possible.
7. **Register with a known agency** that has an office you can visit.

## Common recruitment problems and how they are solved

**Candidates who disappear after interview.** Often a sign of unclear terms or a better offer elsewhere. Clear salary and duties from the first call reduce this.

**Workers who exaggerate experience.** Practical skills checks reveal this before a family is affected.

**False references.** Calling references directly and asking specific questions usually exposes them.

**Mismatch of expectations.** A worker expecting a small flat finds a large house with frequent guests. Honest job descriptions fix this.

**Long commutes.** Matching workers to nearby areas avoids early resignations.
