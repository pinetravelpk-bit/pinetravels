## Stage one: newly married or living alone

Young professionals and newly married couples in Islamabad often live in apartments in E-11, G-11, F-11 Markaz, Bahria Enclave or the high rise buildings on the Expressway. Both partners usually work. The home is small, but there is no time for cleaning, laundry and cooking after a long day.

**What usually works:** a part-time maid who comes three to five days a week for three or four hours, and possibly a part-time cook who prepares dinner and lunch boxes for the next day.

**What to watch for:** building rules about visitors and staff entry, keys or access cards, and whether you are comfortable with someone in the flat while you are at work. Many couples arrange for the maid to come in the evening or on weekends when one of them is home.

**How a provider helps:** by sending workers who live close enough to reach your building reliably, because a part-time worker who spends two hours on public transport each way will not last. In Islamabad, that means matching workers from Golra and Sector I-11 for the western sectors, and from Bhara Kahu or Tarlai for Bani Gala and Park Road.

## Stage two: a new baby

The arrival of a baby changes everything about a household. Nights become short, the house fills with visitors, and the mother needs rest and support, especially after a caesarean.

**What usually works:** an experienced nanny with newborn care experience, either live-in or for long day shifts, plus a maid to keep up with the extra laundry and cleaning. Some families add a cook for the first few months so that the mother can eat properly without anyone spending hours in the kitchen.

**What to watch for:** hygiene above all. A newborn nanny must wash hands every time, sterilise bottles properly, know safe sleep positions and never shake or rough handle a baby. Ask how she would handle a high fever at night, a baby who will not stop crying or a feed the baby refuses.

**How a provider helps:** by testing nannies on these exact situations and checking references specifically with families who had infants. Our [nanny service in Islamabad](/services/babysitters-nannies/islamabad) explains the checks in detail.

## Stage three: school years

Once children are at school, the challenge becomes logistics. Morning drops, afternoon pick ups from different schools, tuitions, sports, and a mother or father who may also have a career.

**What usually works:** a reliable [driver](/services/drivers/islamabad) is often the first hire, followed by a full-time maid who manages the house during the day and a cook who handles lunch and dinner. In larger homes, a helper may take care of outdoor cleaning and errands.

**What to watch for:** a driver who carries your children must be calm in traffic, careful at school gates and completely trustworthy. Check the licence, test the driving on Islamabad roads, and agree clear rules: no phone calls while driving, no stops on the way, no passengers other than the children.

**How a provider helps:** by road testing drivers and calling previous families who used them for school runs. We also match drivers who live within reasonable distance of your sector, so early starts are not a problem.

## Stage four: a bigger house and more staff

Many Islamabad families eventually move to a larger home in DHA, Bahria Town, the E sectors or one of the farmhouse areas around Chak Shahzad and Park Road. A bigger house brings more rooms, more bathrooms, a garden, guest quarters and more entertaining.

**What usually works:** a small team. A typical large Islamabad household might include a cook, one or two maids, a driver, a gardener and a security guard. Some families prefer a [domestic couple](/services/couples/islamabad), for example a cook and a driver who are married and live in the staff quarters together.

**What to watch for:** with more staff, roles must be clear. Who answers the gate? Who serves guests? Who cleans the kitchen after dinner? Overlapping or unclear duties are the most common cause of friction between staff.

**How a provider helps:** by thinking about the team, not just the individual. When we place a second or third worker in a house, we ask about the existing staff, their languages and personalities, and the duties already covered, so the new person fits in.

## Stage five: caring for elderly parents

Islamabad has a large population of retired government officers, armed forces families and professionals who settled here for the climate and calm. As parents age, many families face the difficult moment when a parent cannot safely be alone.

**What usually works:** a [caretaker](/services/caretakers/islamabad) for daily living support, such as walking, bathing, meals, medicines on time and company. If there are medical needs, such as wound care, injections, catheter care or post stroke monitoring, a qualified [home nurse](/services/nurses/islamabad) is the right choice.

**What to watch for:** patience and respect. An elderly parent may be frustrated, forgetful or embarrassed to need help. The right caretaker treats them with dignity, speaks to them kindly and reports changes in health honestly.

**How a provider helps:** by matching temperament as carefully as skill, and by checking whether a caretaker has looked after someone with similar needs, such as dementia, Parkinson's or limited mobility after a fall.

## Stage six: owners living abroad

Islamabad has many homes owned by families who live in the Gulf, the UK, North America or Europe. Some homes stand empty for months; others house parents who stayed behind.

**What usually works:** a live-in caretaker or a trusted couple to look after the house and any family members living there, a gardener on a weekly schedule and a security guard. Some overseas owners also want a driver for parents who no longer drive.

**What to watch for:** supervision from a distance is hard. Agree clear daily or weekly check ins, ask for photos of the house, and ask a relative or friend nearby to visit regularly.

**How a provider helps:** by doing the interviews and checks on the ground, sharing video calls with you so you can meet candidates, and staying available if the worker needs to be replaced while you are abroad.

## Signs you have outgrown your current arrangement

Households rarely decide to change their staffing in one moment. The signs build up slowly. Look out for these:

- **You are doing the work yourself again.** If you regularly clean the kitchen at night or iron school uniforms on Sunday, your current staff cannot keep up with the house as it is now.
- **One worker is doing three jobs.** A maid who also cooks, looks after a toddler and answers the gate will eventually burn out or start cutting corners.
- **Routines keep breaking.** Late school drops, missed medicine times for a parent or meals that are never ready on time all point to a gap.
- **Staff are arguing about duties.** When workers argue about who should do what, the problem is usually unclear roles, not bad people.
- **Your own health or work is suffering.** If running the house is affecting your job, sleep or family time, it is time to look again.

When you notice two or more of these, talk to a domestic staff provider Islamabad families trust and describe the whole picture. Often the fix is a change of role or hours rather than an extra salary, and an honest provider will tell you so.

## Keeping good staff for longer

Families often ask how to keep good staff. The answer is simpler than most expect:

1. Pay on time, every month, without having to be asked.
2. Give a weekly day off and stick to it.
3. Provide decent food and, for live-in staff, a clean room with a fan, heater and bathroom.
4. Speak respectfully, especially in front of guests and children.
5. Give a yearly increase in line with inflation and an Eid bonus.
6. Listen when staff raise a problem, and deal with it early.

Families who follow these six habits rarely need to call a provider for replacements.
