Airport Housing Society (AHS) is one of Rawalpindi's older, well-established residential schemes, with a mix of long-time resident families and newer households. Like many established neighborhoods, hiring domestic staff here has traditionally worked through word-of-mouth, a neighbor's recommendation, a relative's driver's cousin. It works, until it doesn't.

## Why more AHS households are switching to verified hiring

The most common reasons families in Airport Housing Society give us for moving away from informal referrals:

- **No real way to verify a referral's claims**, "he's worked for my cousin for years" isn't something you can actually check.
- **No recourse if something goes wrong**, informal hires rarely come with any kind of guarantee.
- **Inconsistent availability**, referral-based hiring often means waiting weeks for the "right" recommendation to come along.

## What we offer instead

Every candidate we place in Airport Housing Society goes through CNIC and address verification, reference checks with prior employers, and a personal interview, with a police character certificate additionally required for security guard roles. If a placement doesn't work out within the guarantee period, we arrange a replacement at no extra fee.

## The households we place staff with in AHS

Airport Housing Society has a fairly even mix of long-settled families who've lived there for decades and newer households who've moved in more recently, often drawn by the area's established infrastructure and proximity to the airport and cantonment areas. Long-settled families tend to be replacing staff who've retired or moved on after years of service, and they usually want a close match to the routine that's already in place. Newer households are more often building a staffing setup from the ground up and want guidance on what roles they actually need first.

## What a typical AHS booking looks like, from message to placement

A resident reaches out on WhatsApp with their street or block and the role they need. We ask a few clarifying questions, live-in or live-out, working hours, any specific skill requirements, and send a shortlist of pre-verified candidates, usually within 48 hours. The household interviews directly, either by phone or in person, and once someone is chosen, a trial period follows before the arrangement is considered permanent. If it doesn't work out during that trial, we go back to the shortlist rather than leaving the household to start over.

## Questions AHS residents ask us most

**How is this different from just asking around the neighborhood?**
The core difference is documentation and accountability. A neighbor's recommendation can be a great starting point, but there's usually no way to verify the claims behind it or any recourse if things don't work out. Our process builds both of those in from the start.

**Can you place someone quickly if our current staff leaves without notice?**
We prioritize urgent requests and aim to get a shortlist to you within 48 hours, though actual start dates depend on candidate availability.

**Do you place staff for security-focused roles specifically, given AHS's location near sensitive infrastructure?**
Yes, security guard placements go through an additional police character certificate check on top of our standard verification, given the nature of the role and the area.

## Documents we ask for before any placement

Every candidate we place in Airport Housing Society is verified against their original CNIC, a current photo, and their stated home address. We also collect the contact details of at least one previous employer as a matter of course, not as an afterthought once a household asks, but as a standard part of our process before a candidate is ever shortlisted. Households are welcome to ask to see this documentation directly before finalizing a hire.

## How we actually verify references

A written reference letter is simple to produce and hard to verify on its own, so our process centers on a direct phone call to the previous employer. We ask specific questions, how long the person worked there, why the employment ended, whether the employer had any concerns about reliability or honesty, and whether they'd hire the person again. Answers that come with specific, natural detail are a good sign; vague or generic praise prompts us to check a second reference before the candidate goes on a shortlist.

## Red flags that make us slow down

A few things prompt extra scrutiny before we'll shortlist a candidate to an AHS household: an address that shifts in detail when asked about a second time, reluctance to provide a previous employer's phone number, or an inconsistent account of recent work history. None of these are automatic disqualifiers on their own, but they're reasons to verify further rather than take a candidate's word at face value, which is exactly the gap informal, word-of-mouth hiring tends to leave open.
