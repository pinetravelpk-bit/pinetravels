Faisalabad's textile-driven economy means a steady base of offices, trading houses and clinics near Jinnah Colony and the commercial core, all relying on dependable office boys for day-to-day support.

## Textile trading floors set the pace here

Faisalabad's office boy requests skew heavily toward textile trading houses and mills, where the role often means more than pantry duty, running fabric samples between offices, handling courier pickups tied to shipment deadlines, and keeping up with a fairly constant flow of visiting buyers and agents. Clinics near Jinnah Colony ask for a calmer, more routine-driven candidate instead. We match based on which pattern the workplace actually runs on.

## What we check before placing a candidate

Every office boy we place goes through CNIC and address verification, reference checks with previous employers, and a personal interview. For trading-house placements specifically, we also confirm the candidate is comfortable with sample handling and time-sensitive courier runs, since that's the daily reality for most Faisalabad businesses in this sector.

## What a typical booking looks like

Most requests start with a WhatsApp message or a quick call describing the office, trading house, mill office, clinic, whatever it is, and roughly what hours are needed. We ask a few follow-up questions: is this a full-time role or part-time coverage during peak buying season, does the office deal with sample handling, and is there a preference for someone who already knows the Jinnah Colony or Madina Town area. From there we shortlist candidates who've already been through verification, usually within a day or two, and arrange a short in-person meeting or trial shift before anything is finalised. Owners running trading floors often want the candidate to sit in for a half-day first, just to see how they handle a real rush of couriers and visiting buyers.

## Range of duties we match for

Not every office boy role in Faisalabad looks the same, so we try to scope the job properly before shortlisting rather than sending a generic candidate. Typical duties include pantry and tea service, courier and document pickup, filing and record-keeping, running errands to banks or government offices, and, specific to the textile trade here, carrying fabric samples between buying houses. Some businesses want one person covering all of it; others, especially larger trading houses, split pantry work from courier and sample-running duties across two placements. We ask which setup fits before proposing candidates.

## Full-time vs. part-time arrangements

A lot of the smaller offices near the commercial core only need coverage for part of the day, particularly clinics with defined morning and evening hours, while mills and trading houses tend to want someone present for the full working day, sometimes with flexibility for early starts when shipments are going out. We place for both arrangements and are upfront about what's realistic, a part-time role naturally narrows the pool of candidates willing to take it, so it's worth knowing which one you need before we start shortlisting.
