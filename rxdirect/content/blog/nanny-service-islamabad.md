Islamabad's dual-income households, especially in F-sectors, Bahria Town and DHA, consistently rank childcare among the most requested and most carefully screened roles we place.

## Infant care is a different hire than after-school support

In our Islamabad requests, the age of the children usually determines everything else about the placement. Households with infants and toddlers are typically looking for a dedicated, experienced full-time nanny, often the family's only childcare arrangement, which makes the vetting stricter and the fit more critical. Families with school-age children in F-sectors and DHA, by contrast, more often need after-school coverage: homework supervision, activity drop-offs and pickups, and evening hours until a parent gets home from work. We treat these as genuinely different roles rather than one generic "nanny" listing, because the skills, and the level of independent judgment required, aren't the same.

## What we check before placing a nanny

Given the sensitivity of the role, we go beyond our standard process: CNIC and address verification, reference checks focused specifically on childcare experience, an in-depth in-person interview, and a health screening. We match candidates to the specific age group they have real experience with, since infant care and after-school supervision draw on different skills entirely.

## What a typical booking looks like

Islamabad inquiries usually come in with a fair amount of detail already, the ages of the children, whether it's a first nanny hire or a replacement, and specific concerns like allergies or a particular routine that needs to be maintained. Because infant-care placements in F-sectors, Bahria Town and DHA tend to be the family's sole childcare arrangement, we spend more time on the front end here: a phone screening, then an in-person interview with the family, often followed by a supervised trial visit where the candidate spends time with the child while a parent is present. For after-school roles, the process moves faster since the stakes around independent judgment are somewhat lower and school schedules give everyone a clear structure to plan around.

## Trial period and replacement policy

Because an infant-care mismatch is far more disruptive than a school-pickup one, we encourage Islamabad families to treat the first two to three weeks as an adjustment period rather than assuming day one will be seamless. If the fit isn't right, even for reasons that have nothing to do with the candidate's competence, we'll find a replacement rather than leaving a family to make it work with someone who isn't the right match for their child's age and temperament.

## Questions we get asked most in Islamabad

**How do you match infant-care nannies specifically, rather than just general childcare staff?** We ask candidates directly about their experience with the age range in question, feeding schedules, sleep routines, developmental stages, and prioritize recent, hands-on experience over general years of service.

**Can the same nanny handle both an infant and an older sibling who needs after-school supervision?** Sometimes, but we're upfront when a household's needs really call for two different skill sets rather than one generalist hire.

**Do you place live-in nannies as well as daily ones?** Yes, both are common in Islamabad depending on the household's space and preference, tell us which you're looking for when you reach out.

## Language preference and household routine fit

Islamabad households are more linguistically mixed than most cities we cover, F-sector families often want a nanny who's comfortable in English as well as Urdu, especially if the children attend an international curriculum school, while some Bahria Town and DHA households prioritize Urdu or Punjabi fluency for day-to-day comfort with grandparents or other relatives in the home. We ask about this upfront rather than assuming, since a nanny who's an excellent match on childcare experience but can't communicate naturally with the rest of the household tends to create friction that has nothing to do with her competence.

## Documents and verification you should expect to see

Before we place anyone, Islamabad families can expect a copy of the candidate's CNIC, at least one contactable reference specific to prior childcare work, not a general character reference, and confirmation that the health screening has been completed. For infant-care placements especially, we encourage families to actually call the reference themselves rather than relying solely on our notes, since hearing directly how a previous family describes the candidate's handling of feeding schedules or nighttime routines often surfaces details that matter for your specific situation.
