## Our screening process

Multan Cantt households tend to expect the same disciplined standard found across cantonment areas nationwide, a guard who keeps documentation on hand, follows a consistent gate routine, and doesn't need reminders about visitor logging. For those placements, we lean harder on verifying a candidate's prior experience with structured entry procedures during reference checks, on top of the standard CNIC verification, police character certificate and physical fitness check. DHA Multan clients, by contrast, often want a guard who can work well alongside the community's own security desk rather than duplicate it, so we screen for that coordination skill separately.

## Shift options

Cantt-area clients most often request a single disciplined day or night guard who mirrors the area's existing security rhythm, while DHA households more frequently ask for full 24-hour coverage split between two guards on 12-hour shifts. We'd rather adjust early than leave a mismatched placement in place.

## Documents and verification you can expect

Every guard we place has completed CNIC verification, holds a police character certificate, has passed a physical fitness check, and has had at least one previous employer contacted about reliability and conduct. For Multan Cantt placements, we specifically ask past employers about the candidate's experience with structured, checkpoint-style entry procedures; for DHA placements, we ask instead about their ability to work alongside an existing community security desk rather than duplicate it. We share this background summary with you before you meet the guard so there are no surprises later.

## Uniform, equipment and reporting

Guards arrive uniformed from day one regardless of which part of Multan they're placed in, this isn't something you need to source separately. Standard equipment includes a torch for night shifts and a visitor/gate log. For Cantt-area placements, we encourage keeping this log even if the guard's routine already mirrors the area's existing security culture, since it's a useful record independent of any informal checkpoint habits. On reporting, guards are expected to flag anything unusual directly to you, an unfamiliar vehicle, a visitor who doesn't check out, anything that breaks the property's normal pattern, rather than deciding on their own that it isn't worth mentioning.
