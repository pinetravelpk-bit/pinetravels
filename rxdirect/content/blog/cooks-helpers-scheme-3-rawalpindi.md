Scheme 3 is one of Rawalpindi's longer-established residential areas, home to a mix of joint-family households and smaller nuclear families along Allama Iqbal Road and the surrounding sectors. It's also an area where many households still hire staff the old-fashioned way, and increasingly, where families are looking for a more reliable alternative.

## The two most common requests from Scheme 3 households

**Cooks.** Most requests are for a daily home cook focused on Pakistani meals, breakfast and dinner being the most common combination for working households. Some larger joint-family homes need a live-in cook who can also help manage the kitchen day-to-day.

**Helpers.** General household help, cleaning, laundry, and daily upkeep, is the second most common request, often paired with a cook for households that want full kitchen-and-house coverage from a small, trusted team.

## Our process

Every cook and helper we place in Scheme 3 goes through CNIC and address verification, reference checks with previous employers, a personal interview, and a health screening. For cooks specifically, we also confirm hands-on experience with the type of cuisine you need before shortlisting.

## Why Scheme 3 households are moving away from informal hiring

For years, the standard way to find a cook or helper in Scheme 3 was through neighbors, local shopkeepers, or word of mouth along Allama Iqbal Road. It still happens, but it comes with real limitations: there's no consistent way to check someone's actual work history, no fallback if the arrangement doesn't work out, and often a long wait before the "right" recommendation comes along. Families who've been through a bad experience this way are usually the ones who reach out to us looking for a process with actual verification and a documented trial period behind it.

## What a typical Scheme 3 placement looks like, start to finish

A household gets in touch with their address, the role they need, and any specifics, cuisine, live-in versus live-out, language preference. We shortlist two or three verified candidates whose background matches, the family interviews them directly, and a trial period follows before anything becomes a long-term arrangement. If a joint family is hiring both a cook and a helper, we run both searches in parallel so the household isn't left managing two separate timelines.

## Questions we're asked most in Scheme 3

**Can you find someone who lives close by, so commuting isn't an issue?**
Yes, Scheme 3 spans several sectors, and we account for where a candidate already lives or has previously worked when building your shortlist, since a shorter commute usually means better long-term reliability.

**Do live-in cooks get a separate room, or is that something we need to arrange?**
That's a household-specific arrangement we discuss during the interview stage, we make sure both sides are clear on living arrangements before anyone commits to a live-in role.

**What if we're replacing a long-time cook or helper who's leaving after years of service?**
We treat these placements carefully, since the household usually has a very specific routine already in place. We ask detailed questions about that routine upfront so the shortlist reflects candidates who can realistically step into it.

## What documents we ask for before placement

Before shortlisting anyone in Scheme 3, we collect a copy of the candidate's original CNIC, confirm their current address matches what's on file, and speak directly by phone with at least two previous employers rather than accepting a written reference letter alone. A phone call lets us ask specific questions, how long they actually worked there, whether they'd be rehired, how the household handled any disagreements, that a letter typically glosses over. For cooks, we also ask for specifics on the households they cooked for, since general claims of experience without detail are usually a sign to look closer.

## Red flags worth knowing about

A candidate who's reluctant to share a previous employer's contact details, gives inconsistent dates for past work, or can't describe specific dishes or duties from a role they claim to have held are all reasons we slow down. None of these automatically disqualify someone, but they're exactly the kind of thing a rushed, informal hire along Allama Iqbal Road tends to miss.
