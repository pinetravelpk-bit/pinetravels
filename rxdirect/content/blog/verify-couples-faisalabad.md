A domestic couple is a different kind of hire. You are not bringing in one person for one role, you are bringing two people into your home at the same time, usually to live in, usually to manage the entire household between them. In Faisalabad, where many larger homes in areas like People's Colony, Madina Town, and the Wapda City surroundings still run on live-in domestic staff, a couple is often hired to cover cooking, cleaning, laundry, and basic household upkeep as a unit. That means the trust you are extending is broader and deeper than for a single staff member. Verifying a couple is therefore not just verifying two individuals separately, it is verifying them as a unit, because the way they work together and the way they present themselves jointly tells you things that two separate interviews never will.

## Why verification matters more when you are hiring two people at once

When you hire one person and it goes wrong, you replace one person. When you hire a couple and it goes wrong, the disruption is larger. They leave together, which means you lose two roles at the same time, often with no notice. If the issue is something serious, dishonesty, a conflict with the household, or a mismatch that was visible at the start but overlooked, the damage is also doubled. Faisalabad families who have had a couple placement go bad usually tell us the warning signs were there in the first meeting but were brushed aside because the household was under pressure to fill the roles quickly. Verification exists precisely to slow that decision down at the point where slowing down is cheapest, before the couple moves in.

## RX Direct's verification steps for domestic couple placements in Faisalabad

Every couple we place goes through four verification steps, and both individuals are checked, not just the one who does most of the talking during the interview.

**1. CNIC and address verification for both individuals.** We check each person's CNIC separately and confirm the address and family details on each document. This matters because couples sometimes present themselves as married when they are not, or one partner may be using documents that do not actually belong to them. We verify both CNICs against each other as well, confirming the relationship they claim is consistent with what the documents show.

**2. Reference checks with previous employers.** We speak with previous households where the couple has worked as a unit, not just separate references for each person. Working as a couple is a skill of its own, and a previous employer who has seen them do it together can tell you things a single-person reference cannot. We ask about how they divided responsibilities, whether they had any conflict that affected the household, and why they left.

**3. Joint interview.** We interview the couple together, in person, in Faisalabad. A joint interview reveals dynamics that separate interviews hide. Who answers first, who defers, whether they contradict each other on basic facts like previous employers or working hours, and how they handle a difficult question when they have to answer in front of each other. These are small observations, but they are the ones that predict how the couple will behave inside your household.

**4. Health screening.** Because a live-in couple is in constant proximity to the family and the kitchen, we require a recent health screening for both individuals, including basic bloodwork and a chest X-ray. This is basic protection for the household and a reasonable expectation for any live-in placement.

## Shortcuts families take that backfire

The most common shortcut in Faisalabad is hiring a couple based on a single phone call with a previous employer. A phone call is good, but one call is not enough. Couples who have been let go from a previous household for a serious reason will often give you the contact details of an earlier, more favorable reference and skip the most recent one. Calling only the reference they volunteer means you hear the best version, not the most relevant one. Always ask specifically for the most recent employer, and ask what the one before that was as well.

The second shortcut is verifying only one half of the couple. This happens often when one partner is clearly the more experienced worker and the other is quieter or seems secondary. But the quieter partner is the one who will be in your home unsupervised at odd hours, handling laundry, cleaning, or kitchen prep. Skipping their verification because they did not speak much in the interview is exactly backwards. The person you check least is often the one you should check most.

The third shortcut is taking the couple's word for their relationship status without checking documents. Some couples presenting together are not actually married, and in some cases one partner is using someone else's CNIC. This is not a moral judgment, it is a verification issue. If the documents do not match the story, that is a signal to stop and ask more questions, not to proceed because the interview went well.

## How to verify a domestic couple independently

If you are hiring without an agency in Faisalabad, or you want to verify alongside our process, here is what we recommend.

**Ask for both CNICs and photograph both sides of each.** Check the names, father's names, and dates of birth against what the couple has told you. If they say they are married, the documents should be consistent with that. Ask to see the originals in person, not just photocopies.

**Verify both addresses.** A utility bill, a letter from a local figure, or any document that ties each person to the address on their CNIC adds a layer beyond the card itself.

**Call at least two previous employers, including the most recent.** Ask open questions about how the couple worked together, not just about each person individually. "How did they divide the work between them?" tells you more than "Were they good workers?"

**Interview them together and watch the dynamic.** Notice who speaks, who interrupts, and whether their stories match on basics like how long they worked somewhere and why they left. Contradictions on simple facts are a warning sign.

## What documents to ask for

At minimum, ask for original CNICs for both individuals, contact details for at least two previous employers with working phone numbers, recent health screening reports for both, and any training certificates if either partner has formal cooking, first-aid, or related training. Keep copies of the CNICs and health reports once hired. If the couple is reluctant to provide any of these, treat that reluctance as information.

## Why a phone call beats a written reference

A written reference letter for a couple is even less useful than one for a single hire, because a letter cannot capture how the two people functioned together day to day. When we call a previous employer, we ask them to describe the couple's routine, who handled what, and how they handled disagreements. The way an employer answers those questions on a phone call, the pauses, the things they choose not to say, tells us far more than any letter. A letter is a curated document. A phone call is a conversation, and conversations are where the real information comes out. This is why we rely on calls rather than letters, and why we suggest families do the same.
