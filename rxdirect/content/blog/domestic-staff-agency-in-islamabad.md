They start searching when the cook has left without notice two days before guests arrive, when a new baby is due next month, or when an elderly parent can no longer be left alone while everyone is at work. ## Why families in Islamabad use a domestic staff agency

Islamabad is a city of busy households. Government officers, doctors, bankers, diplomats, business owners and overseas families keep homes in the F, G, E and I sectors, DHA, Bahria Town and the newer societies along the Expressway and Park Road. Many of these homes are large, with separate quarters, several bathrooms, gardens and more than one car. Running them takes a team, and finding that team on your own is slow.

A domestic staff agency in Islamabad saves you four things:

1. **Time.** You describe the job once, and the agency does the searching, calling and filtering.
2. **Risk.** A serious agency checks identity, address and references before you meet anyone.
3. **Choice.** Instead of one candidate, you see a shortlist of people who already match your hours, budget and household.
4. **Backup.** If the placement does not work out, the agency replaces the worker rather than leaving you to start again.

The last point matters more than most families expect. Even a well matched worker can leave because of a family emergency in the village, a wedding or a move. With an agency, that is a phone call. Without one, it is another month of searching.

## What a good agency does before you meet anyone

The quality of an agency shows in the work it does before the interview. Here is what should happen behind the scenes.

### Sourcing candidates

Good agencies do not rely only on walk ins. They keep a pool of workers who have been placed before and came back with good feedback, accept referrals from existing staff, and register new candidates through an office where they can be met in person. At RX Direct, workers register at our office or through our [staff registration page](/staff/register), and every registration is reviewed by a person, not a form.

### Identity and address checks

Every candidate should show an original CNIC. The agency should match the photo and details to the person in front of them, record the permanent address and the current address in Islamabad or Rawalpindi, and confirm that the current address is real. Many workers live in Bhara Kahu, Tarlai, Golra, Sohan, Khanna or the katchi abadis near the sectors, and an agency that knows the city will know how to confirm these addresses properly.

### Reference calls

A reference only means something if someone actually calls it. The agency should speak to previous employers, ask how long the worker stayed, why they left, how they handled money and keys, and whether the family would hire them again. A short, honest reference from a family in F-10 is worth more than a long certificate from nowhere.

### Skill checks

Saying "I can cook" and cooking well are different things. A cook should be tested on real dishes. A driver should be road tested in Islamabad traffic, on the Expressway and in tight sector streets. A nanny should be asked how she would handle a choking toddler or a fever at night. Our [vetting process](/how-it-works) covers this for every role we place.

## How matching works

Matching is where agencies either earn their fee or waste your time. Sending ten random CVs is not matching. A good match looks at:

- **Hours and living arrangement.** Live-in, full-time live-out or part-time.
- **Household size and routine.** Two working adults with a toddler need something very different from a large family with grandparents and frequent guests.
- **Location and travel.** A live-out worker living in Bhara Kahu can reach Bani Gala and Park Road far more easily than DHA Phase 2 or B-17.
- **Language and background.** Some families prefer Urdu speakers, some want basic English for children, some want Pashto or Punjabi for an elderly parent.
- **Personality and expectations.** A quiet worker may suit a calm home with an elderly parent; an energetic one may suit a house with three children.

When you speak to us, expect questions. We would rather spend ten extra minutes understanding your home than send you people who do not fit.

## The trial period

No interview can show everything. That is why a short paid trial is standard. During the trial, watch for:

- Punctuality and whether the worker keeps to agreed hours
- How they respond to instructions and corrections
- Hygiene in the kitchen and bathrooms
- How they treat children, elderly family members and other staff
- Whether they ask sensible questions or guess

Write down what goes well and what does not. If something small bothers you, mention it early and politely. Most issues in the first week are about expectations, not character, and are easy to fix once spoken about.

## Replacement guarantee

Ask every agency what happens if the worker leaves or does not work out. A serious agency will have a written answer. Ours is a six month replacement guarantee: if the worker leaves or is not suitable within six months, we replace them without charging another placement fee. Some agencies offer one month, some offer nothing. The difference matters when you are relying on someone to look after your children or your parents.

## Questions to ask any agency before you pay

Use this list on the phone. A good agency will answer each question clearly.

1. Where is your office, and can I visit?
2. How do you check identity and address?
3. Do you call previous employers yourselves?
4. How do you test skills for this role?
5. How many candidates will I see, and how soon?
6. What is the fee, and when exactly do I pay it?
7. Do you take any part of the worker's salary?
8. What is your replacement policy, in writing?
9. Will you help with the police registration of the worker?
10. Who do I call if there is a problem after placement?

If the answers are vague, or if the agency avoids giving a fee until after you have committed, keep looking.

## Police registration and paperwork

Islamabad Police ask households to register domestic workers at the local police station or through the ICT police registration process. Registration gives the police a record of who is working in your home and is a sensible step for your own protection. Keep copies of the worker's CNIC, a recent photo, their permanent address and an emergency contact. We provide these documents with every placement and can explain the current registration steps.

It is also worth writing a simple agreement covering salary, hours, weekly day off, leave, notice period and duties. It does not need to be a legal document. It needs to be clear, signed by both sides and kept somewhere safe. Clear terms prevent most disagreements before they start.
