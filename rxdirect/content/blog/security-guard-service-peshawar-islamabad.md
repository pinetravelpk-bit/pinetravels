Peshawar Cantt and Islamabad's Askari sectors share the same disciplined, security-conscious culture, residents in both expect a guard who can produce documentation on request and follow visitor logging procedures without friction.

## Our screening process for both cities

The two cities share a security culture, but not an identical one. Peshawar Cantt placements tend to involve more coordination with existing military-adjacent checkpoints on approach roads, so we look for candidates who are comfortable working near, and communicating clearly with, that layer of security rather than being confused by it. Islamabad's Askari sectors and diplomatic-adjacent areas add a different wrinkle, guards there are more likely to be asked for ID by outside patrols themselves, so we screen for candidates who keep their own documentation in order and know how to represent the household correctly if stopped. Both profiles go through the same non-negotiable baseline: CNIC verification, a police character certificate, reference checks with previous employers on reliability and incidents, and a physical fitness check.

## Shift options

Peshawar Cantt households more often ask for a single guard whose hours mirror the checkpoint patrol schedule nearby, while Islamabad's Askari and diplomatic-adjacent clients more frequently request full 24-hour coverage given the area's stricter round-the-clock expectations. Both options, single-shift or a full rotation split between two guards on 12-hour shifts, are available in either city; tell us your specific sector and we'll match the shift to local norms.

## Beyond the standard checks

For both cities, screening goes further than the baseline CNIC and police character certificate. In Peshawar Cantt, we specifically probe how a candidate has handled coordination with checkpoint or patrol staff in the past, not adversarially, but as a routine part of getting in and out of a secured area smoothly. In Islamabad's Askari and diplomatic-adjacent sectors, we focus more on a candidate's own paperwork discipline, since guards there are themselves more likely to be stopped and asked to identify who they work for. In both cases we're looking for someone who represents the household calmly and correctly under that extra layer of scrutiny, not just someone who can stand at a gate.

## Verifying a guard's employment history properly

A written reference letter alone doesn't tell you much, letters can be generic or outdated. We call previous employers directly and ask specific questions: how long the guard actually worked there, whether there were any security incidents or disputes, how they handled unexpected situations (a late-night disturbance, an unfamiliar visitor refusing to show ID), and whether the employer would rehire them. We also verify the police character certificate independently rather than accepting a photocopy at face value. If you're vetting a guard outside our process, we'd recommend the same, a five-minute phone call to a previous employer surfaces far more than a signed letter ever will.

## Questions we get asked most

**What's a fair trial period for a new guard?**
A week or two of observed performance is usually enough to judge punctuality, alertness, and how they handle the specific entry and logging procedures of your sector. For 24-hour or rotating-shift placements, try to observe at least one full night shift during the trial, since that's when lapses are most likely and hardest to catch after the fact.

**What happens if a guard doesn't work out after placement?**
Tell us as early as possible. We'll shortlist a replacement from the same screened pool rather than leaving you to restart the process, and we factor in what specifically didn't work, punctuality, communication with patrol staff, paperwork discipline, when matching the next candidate.

**Do guards carry their own equipment, or does the household provide it?**
This varies by household and should be clarified before the placement starts, some clients provide a torch, radio or basic uniform, others expect the guard to have their own. Neither is standard, so it's worth a direct conversation rather than an assumption.

**How are shift handovers managed for 24-hour coverage?**
For two-guard rotations, we recommend a short overlap window between shifts so the outgoing guard can brief the incoming one on anything unusual from their shift, an unfamiliar visitor, a maintenance issue, a message left for the household. This is worth setting as an expectation with both guards from day one rather than assuming it'll happen naturally.
