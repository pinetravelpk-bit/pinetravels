If you already care for children in Islamabad, whether for relatives, neighbours, or families you found on your own, you have probably run into the same wall every time you look for a new household: how does a family actually trust you with their kids before they have met you? The honest answer is that most don't, not without a real check behind you. That is exactly the gap verified babysitters and nannies fill through RX Direct, and this guide walks you through what becoming verified actually involves, step by step, in plain words.

## Why Islamabad families ask for verified babysitters

Islamabad households that hire a babysitter or nanny are usually doing it for one of a few reasons: both parents work full time and need someone steady through the day, there is a newborn at home and the mother needs reliable support, or older children need someone present after school until the parents get back. In every one of these situations the family is handing you access to the most important thing in their house, their children, and they want assurance that the person coming in has been checked properly. Verification is what lets a family in F-8, G-10, or Bahria Town say yes quickly instead of spending weeks asking around on their own.

For you, the worker, the benefit is just as real. Once you are verified you stop having to re-explain yourself to every new family, you get placed faster, and you are more likely to land the kind of long term placement that actually pays consistently instead of a string of short trial days that go nowhere.

## The documents you need to bring

Before you come in for verification, gather these so the process isn't delayed:

1. Your original CNIC and one clear photocopy. The name on the CNIC has to match the name you give us, so if you go by a nickname in daily life, tell us that upfront but keep your official name consistent.
2. A current address you can be reached at, even if it is a shared or rented place. We verify address through a combination of the CNIC address and a reachable phone number for someone who knows you.
3. Names and phone numbers of at least two families you have cared for children with before. These do not have to be formal employers, but they do have to be people who will actually pick up the phone and confirm what you did.
4. Any vaccination record or recent medical paper you already have. If you don't have one, don't worry, we arrange the health screening part.

If you have never worked with a family outside your own relatives, that is not a dealbreaker, but you should tell us honestly so we can use character references instead and match you to a first time household accordingly.

## CNIC and address verification

This is the first step and it is non-negotiable. We check your CNIC against the record so the identity we are placing is genuinely yours, and we confirm the address you gave us is one we can actually reach you at. This is not about distrusting you, it is the same check every candidate goes through, drivers, cooks, maids, everyone. For babysitters it matters more, not less, because you are going into a home where children are present and the family needs to know who is walking in.

Bring your original CNIC to the interview. A photo on your phone is not enough for the first visit. If your CNIC is expired or in process, get it renewed first, we cannot complete verification without a valid one.

## Reference checks from prior families

After the CNIC check we contact the families you listed as references. We ask them how long you worked with them, the ages of the children you cared for, what your daily routine looked like, whether you were punctual, and why the arrangement ended. None of this is meant to catch you out, it is meant to give the next family a real picture of how you actually work day to day.

A few things help here. Pick references who remember you clearly, not someone you sat with once for two days. Tell your references in advance that we will call, so they pick up instead of assuming it is a sales call. And if a previous arrangement ended because the family moved away or the children grew up and no longer needed a sitter, say so, that is a perfectly normal reason and we hear it all the time.

## The in-depth personal interview

Once references check out, you sit down with our team for a longer interview. For babysitters and nannies this is more than a quick chat. We go through how you handle specific situations: a toddler who won't stop crying, a child refusing to eat, a fever that comes on suddenly, two siblings fighting, a parent running late and not answering the phone. We are not looking for textbook answers, we want to hear how you actually react, because that is what the family is trusting you to do.

We also talk about ages you are comfortable with. Some candidates are excellent with newborns and nervous with a hyperactive five year old, others are the opposite. Being honest about this means we match you to a household where you will succeed, instead of one where you will be stressed from day one. We cover feeding, sleep routines, hygiene, and basic safety around stairs, kitchens, and electrical points. If you have done a first aid or childcare course, tell us and bring the certificate.

## Health screening

Babysitters and nannies go through a health screening before placement, because you are in close, prolonged contact with children whose immune systems are still developing. This covers a general check and confirmation of relevant vaccinations. We arrange this through our partner clinic and walk you through it, you do not have to figure out where to go on your own. If something shows up in the screening we will talk to you about it honestly before deciding on next steps, we do not simply reject people without an explanation.

## How placement works once you are verified

After you clear all four steps, CNIC and address verification, reference checks from prior families, the in-depth personal interview, and health screening, you go onto our active verified list for Islamabad. When a family reaches out with a requirement that fits your profile, we contact you first with the details: the sector, the number and ages of the children, the hours, whether it is live in or live out, and the pay on offer. You are free to say no to a placement that does not suit you, we do not force anyone into a household they are not comfortable with.

If you say yes, we set up a phone introduction with the family, and if both sides are happy, an in person meeting, usually at the family's home. The trial period starts from the first working day.

## How pay works

Pay is agreed between you and the family before you start, and we make sure it is written down so there is no confusion later. You are paid directly by the household, monthly for long term placements and daily or weekly for shorter ones, depending on what was agreed. We do not take a cut of your monthly salary, our fee comes from the family side, not from your wages. If a family is ever late on payment, tell us and we will follow up with them, you should not have to chase your own money.

## What happens after placement

Once you are placed and the trial is going well, we stay in touch, not to hover, but so that if a routine needs adjusting, the children's schedule changes, or the family asks for extra hours, you have someone to talk to besides handling it all alone with the family. Our replacement guarantee works both ways: if a placement genuinely is not working for you, for example the hours are impossible, the commute is too far, or the household dynamic is difficult, you tell us and we move you to a better matched family rather than leaving you stuck. The same applies if the family asks for a replacement, we re-place you instead of leaving you without work.
