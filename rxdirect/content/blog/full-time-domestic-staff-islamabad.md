Full-time domestic staff are the backbone of most busy Islamabad households. A full-time cook, maid or driver comes in every morning, works through the day and goes home in the evening, giving the family steady help without the arrangements that live-in staff need. Families who hire full-time domestic staff Islamabad wide often say the same thing after a year: "We do not know how we managed before."

Full-time staff are also employees in the real sense. They depend on one household for their whole income. That brings responsibilities for the employer: fair hours, fair pay, proper leave and a clear path for the job to grow. This guide sets out how to employ full-time staff well, from the first agreement to the yearly review.

## What full-time means in practice

In Islamabad households, full-time usually means:

- **Eight to ten working hours a day,** with a meal break
- **Six days a week,** with one fixed day off
- **Live-out,** with the worker returning home each evening, or live-in where agreed
- **One household,** with no other jobs on the side

Some full-time staff work a split shift, for example a cook who works 7 am to 2 pm and returns 6 pm to 9 pm, with the afternoon off. Split shifts suit live-in staff or workers who live very close by. For workers with long commutes, a single continuous shift is fairer.

## Setting hours that last

The most common mistake with full-time domestic staff is letting hours drift. A driver hired for 7 am to 5 pm is gradually asked to stay for dinners, weddings and late flights, until he regularly works fourteen hours. A cook hired for lunch and dinner is asked to make breakfast too. Within a year, the worker is exhausted and starts looking elsewhere.

Prevent this by:

- Writing the hours into the agreement
- Keeping a simple record of extra hours
- Paying overtime or giving time off in return
- Asking rather than assuming when you need extra time
- Reviewing the hours every few months as family routines change

## Overtime

Overtime is normal in Islamabad households, especially for drivers and cooks during the wedding season, Ramadan and when guests visit. Agree how it works at the start. Common approaches:

- **Hourly overtime rate,** calculated from the monthly salary
- **Fixed amount per late evening,** for example for a driver kept beyond 9 pm
- **Time off in lieu,** such as a half day off later in the week
- **Meal and transport allowance** when the worker finishes too late for public transport

Whatever you choose, be consistent and keep a note. Workers notice fairness.

## Leave and holidays

Full-time staff need regular rest and time with their families. A fair package in Islamabad usually includes:

- **One full day off each week,** fixed and respected
- **Annual paid leave** of around two weeks, often taken at Eid
- **Public holidays** off where possible, or paid extra if worked
- **Sick leave** for genuine illness, with pay for short absences
- **Emergency leave** for deaths and serious illness in the family

Plan Eid leave early. Most staff want to travel home at the same time, and if three workers leave together, the house struggles. Stagger leave where possible, and agree return dates in advance.

## Benefits that make a difference

Beyond salary and leave, small benefits greatly affect whether full-time domestic staff stay:

- **Meals during work hours.** Lunch and tea at minimum.
- **Eid bonus.** Half to one month's salary, once or twice a year.
- **Transport allowance** for workers with long commutes.
- **Uniforms or work clothes,** especially for drivers and guards, and warm jackets in winter.
- **Advances** for family emergencies, repaid in small instalments.
- **Medical help.** Paying for a doctor's visit or medicines when the worker is ill.
- **Support for children's education.** Some families help with school fees or books.

None of these are required, but families who offer them rarely struggle to keep good staff.

## The written agreement

Every full-time worker should have a short written agreement covering:

1. Names and CNIC numbers of employer and worker
2. Role and main duties
3. Working hours and day off
4. Salary, payment date and method
5. Overtime arrangement
6. Annual leave and holidays
7. Meals and any allowances
8. Notice period for either side
9. Date and signatures

Keep a copy each. Update it when salary or duties change.

## Paying salary properly

Pay full-time staff on the same date each month. Many families now pay by bank transfer or mobile wallet such as JazzCash or Easypaisa, which creates a record for both sides and is safer than cash. If you pay cash, ask the worker to sign a simple salary register. Never deduct for breakages or mistakes without agreement; it causes resentment and is rarely worth the small amount recovered.

## Yearly review

Once a year, sit down with each full-time worker for a short review. Discuss:

- What has gone well
- What could be better, on both sides
- Any change in duties or hours
- The salary increase for the coming year

A yearly increase of 10 to 15 percent, more in years of high inflation, keeps full-time staff in line with the market. Announce it before they have to ask.

## Helping full-time staff grow

Full-time staff who stay for years often want to grow. Supporting that growth benefits the household too:

- **A maid** can learn to cook, becoming a maid-cook with a higher salary
- **A cook** can learn continental, Chinese or baking from online videos or short courses
- **A helper** can learn to drive and get a licence
- **A driver** can learn basic car maintenance
- **A nanny** can learn first aid or basic English

Pay for a short course or licence fee and agree a fair salary increase once the new skill is used. Workers who grow with a family tend to stay with that family. We regularly see this in Islamabad: a helper who learned to drive with the family's support becomes their trusted driver, or a maid who picked up cooking from the old cook takes over the kitchen when the cook retires. Growth also makes the household more resilient, because when one worker is on leave, another can cover part of the job. Keep a note of each worker's skills and interests, and when a new need comes up, look inside the house before looking outside it.

## When full-time is not the right fit

Sometimes families hire full-time and find the worker has little to do for half the day. Signs include staff sitting idle in the afternoon or being given made-up tasks. In that case, consider:

- Combining two roles into one full-time job, such as maid and cook
- Switching to part-time help
- Adding duties that genuinely need doing, with a matching increase

It is better for both sides than paying for hours that are not needed, or keeping a worker bored.

## Ending full-time employment fairly

If you need to end the arrangement, give the notice agreed, or pay in lieu. Settle all salary and leave due. Return any documents. Write a short reference if the worker served well. Collect keys and change codes. A fair ending protects your reputation among staff in your area.

## Full-time or part-time: which suits your home?

Families sometimes hire full-time out of habit when part-time help would do, or struggle on with part-time help long after the house needs more. A simple test helps. Add up the hours of genuine work your home needs each week, including cleaning, laundry, cooking, driving and childcare. If the total is under twenty five hours, part-time help is usually enough. Between twenty five and forty five hours, one full-time worker fits well. Above that, you probably need two roles or a combination of full-time and part-time staff. Also consider timing: if help is needed at fixed times every day, such as school drops at 7:15 and pick ups at 1:30, full-time domestic staff Islamabad families employ are more dependable than part-time workers juggling several homes.

- **1:30** Lunch break
- **2:30** Ironing
- **3:30** Dusts the lounge and dining room, one deep cleaning task for the day
- **4:30** Folds and puts away clothes, final tidy
- **5:00** Leaves

The day is full but not rushed, with a proper break. If your planned day for a full-time worker looks far heavier than this, you may be asking one person to do two jobs.

## Working parents and full-time nannies

For working parents in Islamabad, a full-time nanny is often the most important hire. Match the nanny's hours to your working day plus travel time, and agree what happens when a meeting runs late or traffic on the Expressway or Kashmir Highway delays you. A small, agreed allowance for late evenings is fairer than assuming the nanny will stay. Keep a handover routine: a quick note in the evening about meals, naps, mood and anything unusual, and a short briefing in the morning about the day's plan.
