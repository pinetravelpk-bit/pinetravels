In Islamabad, the way most households find a domestic couple is still through personal contacts. A friend in F-11 knows a couple who worked for their cousin, someone at the mosque in G-10 has a number for a husband and wife looking for live-in work, or a relative relocating from another city passes their couple along. For a single staff member this is workable. For a couple, where you are bringing two people into your home at once to live in and manage the household between them, informal hiring carries risks that a single hire does not, and the families who have been through a bad couple placement are usually the ones who move to a verified agency the next time.

This is about why that switch happens, and what an agency actually does that a recommendation cannot.

## Why informal hiring is the default in Islamabad

Islamabad's sectors are tightly organised, but the way households find domestic staff is still relationship-based. Families live in the same sector for years, neighbours know each other, and a recommendation from someone in the same block carries real weight. When a household needs a couple to cover cooking, cleaning, laundry, and basic upkeep, the first move is almost always to ask around rather than call a service. It feels faster, it costs less, and the couple comes with someone's word behind them.

The problem is that a couple is a unit, and a recommendation usually describes one half of that unit at best. The person recommending may have only really worked with the husband, or only really noticed the wife's cooking. The dynamic between the two, which is what actually determines whether the placement works, is rarely something a casual recommendation captures.

## The real risks of hiring a couple on your own

The risks with informal couple hiring are specific to bringing two people in at the same time.

**Both identities unchecked.** When you hire a couple through a contact, you rarely verify both CNICs. Couples sometimes present as married when they are not, and in some cases one partner is using documents that do not belong to them. If the placement goes wrong and they leave, you are left with two names you cannot trace.

**References that only cover one person.** A spoken recommendation for a couple usually comes from someone who mainly noticed one of them. The other half, often the one who is alone in the house at odd hours doing laundry and kitchen prep, is the one you know least about. That is exactly backwards from how the risk is distributed.

**No joint interview.** Informal hiring rarely involves sitting down with both people together and watching how they interact. Who answers first, who defers, whether they contradict each other on basic facts like previous employers, these are the details that predict how they will behave inside your household. A phone call with one of them tells you none of this.

**No health screening for either.** A live-in couple is in constant proximity to your family and your kitchen. Informal hiring almost never includes recent health screening for both individuals, and you only find out there was a gap when someone falls ill.

**Double the disruption when it fails.** When a single staff member leaves, you lose one role. When a couple leaves, you lose two at once, often with no notice, and you are back to square one for both cooking and cleaning. There is no pool of already-checked couples to draw from, and the family is left covering both roles themselves while they start over.

## What a verified agency does differently

When an Islamabad household comes to RX Direct for a domestic couple, the process is built around checks that informal hiring skips, and both individuals are checked, not just the one who does most of the talking.

**1. CNIC and address verification for both individuals.** We check each person's CNIC separately and confirm the address and family details on each document. We also verify both CNICs against each other, confirming the relationship they claim is consistent with what the documents show. If the documents do not match the story, the couple does not move forward, no matter how warm the recommendation was.

**2. Reference checks with previous employers.** We speak with previous households where the couple has worked as a unit, not just separate references for each person. Working as a couple is a skill of its own, and a previous employer who has seen them do it together can tell you things a single-person reference cannot. We ask how they divided responsibilities, whether they had any conflict that affected the household, and why they left.

**3. Joint interview.** We interview the couple together, in person. A joint interview reveals dynamics that separate interviews hide. Who answers first, who defers, whether they contradict each other on basic facts like working hours or previous employers, and how they handle a difficult question when they have to answer in front of each other. These are small observations, but they are the ones that predict how the couple will behave inside your household.

**4. Health screening.** Because a live-in couple is in constant proximity to the family and the kitchen, we require a recent health screening for both individuals, including basic bloodwork and a chest X-ray. This is basic protection for the household and a reasonable expectation for any live-in placement.

None of this is skipped, even when a household is under pressure to fill the roles quickly. The families who push us to move faster are the ones who, in our experience, are most relieved later that we did not.

You do not restart the search from zero, and you do not re-run the checks on the next couple, because they have already cleared them.

This is what informal hiring cannot offer. When a recommended couple leaves, you are on your own for both roles. When a verified placement leaves, we already have the next checked couple ready. The verification we do upfront is exactly what makes the guarantee possible.

## The time you actually save

Households often assume an agency is slower than asking a contact. For a couple placement, the opposite is true. A recommendation might arrive the same day, but the checks you should be doing, verifying both CNICs, calling at least two previous employers, sitting down with both people together, arranging health screening for each, easily take two to three weeks if you do them properly on your own. Most households do not do them properly, which is how mismatches slip through.

With a verified agency, the checks are already done before you see the candidates. You receive a shortlist of couples who have cleared CNIC verification for both partners, reference checks, a joint interview, and health screening. Your time goes into choosing between checked options, not into running the checks yourself. For a household that is already stretched managing daily life, that difference is the real reason to use an agency, not the paperwork.
