Inviting someone to work inside your home is different from hiring for an office. They will know when you leave and return, where the keys are kept, what your children eat and how your parents take their medicines. That is why choosing a home staff provider in Islamabad is really a decision about trust. Skills can be taught. Trust has to be checked, built and protected from both sides.

This guide focuses on that side of hiring. It explains which checks matter most before someone enters your home, how to set house rules that are fair and clear, how to handle privacy, valuables and children's safety, and how to make the first week go well. It also covers what staff need from you, because trust that only runs one way does not last.

## Why trust is the first requirement

When families in Islamabad tell us about bad experiences with staff, the stories are rarely about poor cooking or a dusty shelf. They are about a worker who disappeared with jewellery, a driver who used the car for his own errands, a maid who let strangers in while the family was away, or someone who treated a child or an elderly parent harshly when nobody was watching.

Almost all of these problems can be reduced with three things:

1. **Proper checks before hiring**, done by someone who actually verifies them
2. **Clear rules from day one**, agreed and understood by both sides
3. **Fair treatment**, so the worker has every reason to protect your home rather than harm it

A home staff provider in Islamabad should help with all three, not just the first.

## The checks that matter before anyone enters your home

### Identity

The worker's original CNIC must be seen and matched to the person. A photocopy alone proves very little, because photocopies are easily borrowed or altered. We examine the original, compare the photo, and record the CNIC number, father's or husband's name and permanent address.

### Where they live now

Knowing the permanent address in a village in Punjab or Khyber Pakhtunkhwa is useful, but you also need to know where the worker lives in Islamabad or Rawalpindi today. We confirm current addresses in areas such as Bhara Kahu, Tarlai, Golra, Sohan, Dhok Kala Khan, Khanna and the sectors' own staff quarters.

### History

Previous employers are the best guide to future behaviour. We call them and ask specific questions: how long did the worker stay, were they trusted with keys and cash, how did they behave with children, why did they leave, would you hire them again? Vague answers or references that cannot be reached are a warning sign.

### Police registration

Islamabad Police ask employers to register domestic workers. This creates an official record of who works in your home. We provide copies of the documents needed and explain the current process.

## House rules that are fair and clear

Every home is different, so rules should fit your family. What matters is that they are written down, explained on the first day and applied consistently. A simple one page list works well. Here are rules most Islamabad families find useful:

- **Visitors.** No friends or relatives inside the house without permission. Family visits for live-in staff can be arranged at agreed times, in the quarters.
- **Phones.** Personal calls during breaks, not while cooking, driving or watching children.
- **Rooms.** Which rooms staff enter, and when. Many families keep the master bedroom, study and safe off limits unless asked.
- **Cash and shopping.** Every grocery purchase comes with a receipt and change returned.
- **Keys.** Who holds which keys, and that keys are never copied or lent.
- **Leaving the house.** Live-in staff tell a family member when they go out and when they will be back.
- **Food.** What staff can eat from the kitchen, and when meals are provided.
- **Children.** No smacking, shouting or threatening, ever. Problems are reported to parents.

Explain the reasons for each rule. Workers follow rules they understand far better than rules that feel like suspicion.

## Valuables, cash and keys

Reduce temptation and confusion with a few simple habits:

- Keep jewellery, cash and important papers in a locked cupboard or safe, not on dressing tables
- Give staff a separate float for groceries and petrol, and reconcile it weekly with receipts
- Keep a written list of who holds which keys
- Change locks or gate codes when a worker with keys leaves
- Avoid discussing large purchases, travel dates or bank matters in front of staff

None of this is about assuming the worst. It protects honest workers too, because when something goes missing in a house with clear systems, nobody is wrongly blamed.

## Privacy, cameras and respect

Many Islamabad homes now have CCTV at the gate and in common areas. Cameras can be useful, especially when children or elderly parents are at home with staff. If you use them, follow some basic rules of fairness:

- Tell staff where cameras are. Hidden cameras damage trust when discovered.
- Never place cameras in staff quarters, bathrooms or changing areas.
- Use footage to protect your family, not to scold staff for every small thing.

Privacy runs both ways. Staff should respect your family's private conversations and never share details about your home on social media or with other households. We brief workers on this before placement.

## Keeping children safe

For families with young children, the nanny or maid who looks after them is the most important hire in the house. Agree these points clearly:

- Children are never left alone, even for a few minutes, near water, stairs, balconies, the kitchen stove or the road
- No medicines are given without a parent's instruction
- No visitors are allowed in while parents are out
- Children are never taken outside the house without permission, not even to the market or a neighbour
- Parents are called immediately about any fall, fever, rash or unusual behaviour
- No screen time beyond what parents allow

Ask the nanny to keep a short daily note of meals, naps and anything unusual. It helps you stay informed and shows that the nanny takes the job seriously. Our [nanny service in Islamabad](/services/babysitters-nannies/islamabad) covers how we test these skills.

## Caring for elderly family members

If staff will look after an elderly parent, add a few rules: medicines given only as written on the chart, falls and changes in appetite or mood reported the same day, and the parent spoken to with patience and respect. A good [caretaker](/services/caretakers/islamabad) will understand this without being told, but writing it down still helps.

## The first week

The first week sets the pattern for everything that follows.

**Day one.** Show the worker around the house, explain the duty list and the house rules, show where cleaning materials and equipment are kept, and introduce them to other staff. Give them time to settle into their room if they are live-in.

**Days two to four.** Work alongside them where possible. Show how you like things done, such as how you fold clothes, which products to use on marble floors or how spicy the family likes food.

**Day five to seven.** Step back and observe. Give feedback calmly and specifically. "Please wipe the counter after cutting vegetables" works better than "the kitchen is always dirty".

**End of week one.** Sit down for ten minutes. Ask how they are finding the work, what is unclear and whether they need anything. Tell them honestly what is going well and what needs to change.

## What staff need from you

A home staff provider in Islamabad can send you the right person, but whether that person stays depends a lot on the family. Workers we place tell us that these things matter most:

- Salary paid on the same date every month, in full
- A real weekly day off, without being called back
- Respectful language, especially in front of guests and children
- Enough food and a clean, safe place to sleep for live-in staff
- Warm clothing and a heater in Islamabad's cold winters
- Time and permission to pray
- Leave for family emergencies and Eid
- Being listened to when they raise a concern

Families who provide these consistently find that staff protect their home as if it were their own. Many of the longest placements we have made in Islamabad, some running for more than five years, began with an ordinary worker and a family that simply treated them well from the first week. Trust grew from small things: an advance given for a child's school fees, a day off for a sick parent, a word of thanks after a busy wedding week.

## Drivers and the family car

A driver spends more time alone with your family than almost anyone else. Agree a few points at the start: the car is used only for family duties, the logbook or fuel record is kept up to date, the driver waits in the car or at an agreed place rather than wandering off, and nobody else rides in the car without permission. For school runs, give the school the driver's name and photo so teachers know who is allowed to collect the children. If the driver keeps a spare key, note it on your key list. A short monthly check of fuel receipts and mileage keeps everything transparent without making the driver feel watched.

## When the family travels

Many Islamabad families travel for summer holidays, weddings in other cities or visits abroad. Leaving staff in an empty house needs a plan. Decide who stays, what they are responsible for and who they report to. Lock away valuables and give a trusted relative or neighbour the authority to visit and check. Agree a simple daily message or photo of the house. Pay salaries before you leave so staff are not left without money. A home staff provider in Islamabad can also arrange a temporary caretaker or guard if your regular staff are going home on leave at the same time.

## When something goes wrong

If you suspect theft or misconduct, stay calm and avoid accusations in front of others. Check carefully first; many "missing" items turn up misplaced. If you are sure there is a serious problem, contact us and, where appropriate, the police. Never hold back a worker's CNIC or salary as leverage, and never use force. Handling problems properly protects you legally and keeps the situation from getting worse.

If the issue is about performance rather than honesty, give clear feedback and a fair chance to improve. If it still does not work, we arrange a replacement within the guarantee period.

## Frequently asked questions

### How does a home staff provider in Islamabad check that workers are honest?

By verifying the original CNIC, confirming current and permanent addresses, calling previous employers and interviewing the worker about their history and expectations. No check can guarantee behaviour, but proper checks reduce risk greatly.

### Should I install cameras before staff start?

It is your choice. If you do, tell staff where cameras are, keep them out of private areas and use them to protect your family rather than to watch every move.

### Can I set my own house rules?

Yes, and you should. Write them down, explain them on the first day and apply them fairly. We brief workers to expect clear rules and to follow them.

### What if I do not feel comfortable with the worker after a few days?

Tell us. During the trial and within six months of placement, we arrange a replacement if the worker is not right for your home.
