Peshawar's established academic and professional families in University Town, along with larger Hayatabad households, generate steady demand for reliable, long-term household helpers.

## Trust and tenure matter most in Peshawar placements

University Town's academic families and Hayatabad's larger households tend to prioritize long-term trust over quick turnaround, many are looking for a helper who will stay with the family for years, sometimes eventually taking on light elder-care support as parents age, rather than someone cycling through in a few months. That changes what we screen for: we lean harder on multi-year reference checks and family comfort with the candidate's background than on how fast we can fill the role.

## What we check before placing a helper

Every helper we place goes through CNIC and address verification, reference checks with previous employers, a personal interview and a health screening. Given how many Peshawar households are hiring for the long term, we place particular weight on verifying tenure with past employers rather than just the most recent placement.

## What a typical booking looks like

Peshawar families usually reach out with the household size, area and whether they're thinking live-in or live-out from the start, since so many of our placements here are meant to last years, that upfront clarity matters more than it might elsewhere. We shortlist candidates with strong multi-year references, share profiles for the family to review, and arrange interviews before anyone commits. Once a helper is placed, we stay in touch over the following weeks, partly because University Town and Hayatabad households tend to want reassurance that the person joining their home long-term is genuinely settled in, not just filling a gap.

## Where duties typically start and stop

General helper work in Peshawar placements covers cleaning, laundry and day-to-day tidying. Some long-tenured helpers do gradually take on light elder-care tasks as trust builds over years, helping an aging parent with mobility around the house, for instance, but that's not assumed from day one and should be discussed openly as it comes up, not left unspoken. Cooking and driving remain separate roles unless specifically arranged. Being clear about this at the outset avoids awkward assumptions later, especially in households planning for a helper to stay long-term.

## Trial period and replacement policy

Even with an emphasis on long-term placements, we don't expect a family to be locked into the first candidate if the fit isn't right. The early period after placement is treated as a chance for both sides to confirm the arrangement works, if a household or helper decides it isn't the right match, tell us and we'll look at alternative candidates rather than pushing a placement that isn't working for either side.

## Full-time live-in vs. part-time help

While Peshawar's University Town and Hayatabad requests lean heavily toward live-in, long-term arrangements, not every household in the city needs that scale of commitment. Smaller families or those without the space for live-in staff sometimes prefer a part-time helper for a set daily window instead. We ask about household size and long-term intentions early, since it changes which candidates in our pool are the right fit, someone looking for years of live-in tenure isn't necessarily suited to a short part-time role, and vice versa.

## Questions we get asked most in Peshawar

**We want someone who'll genuinely stay for years, not just say so in an interview, how do you screen for that?** We weight this heavily in reference checks, specifically asking past employers how long the candidate actually stayed and why they left, rather than relying on what the candidate tells us. A pattern of multi-year placements is a much stronger signal than an interview answer.

**Our household speaks mostly Pashto at home, does that limit our options?** Not at all. We have candidates across different language backgrounds, and for University Town and Hayatabad households where Pashto is the everyday language, we prioritize matching candidates who are already comfortable in it rather than assuming Urdu or English will do.

**If we're eventually going to need light elder-care support, should we mention that now or wait?** Mention it now, even if it's a few years off. It changes which long-term candidates we consider, since not every helper is comfortable eventually taking on elder-care tasks, and it's easier to match for that upfront than to renegotiate the role later.
