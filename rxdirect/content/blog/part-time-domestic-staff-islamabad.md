Not every home needs someone from morning to night. A working couple in a two bedroom apartment in E-11, a retired professor living alone in F-8, a student flat near the universities in H-11 or a small family in Bahria Enclave may only need a few hours of help a day, or a few days a week. For these homes, part-time domestic staff Islamabad agencies supply are often the smartest choice: lower cost, no need for quarters, and help exactly when it is needed.

Part-time work has its own challenges, though. The worker has several homes to visit, limited time in each, and may be tempted to rush. This guide explains which roles work well part-time, how to plan the schedule, what to pay and how to get consistent, careful work.

## When part-time staff make sense

Part-time help usually suits:

- **Apartments and small houses** where daily cleaning takes two to three hours
- **Working couples** who are out all day and need cleaning, laundry or dinner preparation
- **Single professionals and students** who need a weekly clean and ironing
- **Retired people** who manage most things themselves but need help with heavy cleaning or cooking
- **Families with live-out full-time staff** who need extra help a few days a week, such as a cleaner for deep cleaning on weekends
- **Homes with gardens but no full-time gardener**

Part-time help suits less well when there are infants, elderly parents who cannot be left alone, or a large house that needs constant attention.

## Planning the schedule

The key to good part-time help is a fixed schedule. Workers with several homes plan their day around fixed slots. If your slot keeps changing, the worker either arrives late, leaves early or eventually drops your home.

Agree:

- **Days:** for example Monday, Wednesday and Saturday, or every weekday
- **Arrival and departure time:** for example 9 am to 12 noon
- **What happens on holidays:** whether the worker comes on public holidays and Eid
- **Notice for changes:** both sides tell each other at least a day ahead if a visit must move

In Islamabad, morning slots are in highest demand because many families want cleaning done after they leave for work and school. Evening slots for cooks are also popular. If you can offer a less popular slot, such as early afternoon, you may find a better worker more easily. Weekend slots are the hardest to fill, because many part-time workers keep Friday or Sunday for their own families. If you need weekend help, say so at the start and expect to pay a little more. Also plan for the monsoon and winter fog season, when public transport slows down and part-time workers covering three or four homes across the city run behind schedule. A little flexibility of fifteen or twenty minutes on those days keeps a good worker loyal.

## How to get the most from a few hours

Part-time time is limited, so priorities matter.

### Write a task list for each visit

For a part-time maid visiting three hours a day, a list might be:

**Every visit:** sweep and mop all floors, clean kitchen counters and stove, wash dishes, clean bathrooms, take out rubbish.

**Monday:** laundry. **Wednesday:** ironing. **Friday:** change bed sheets, dust shelves and furniture. **Saturday:** clean fridge, windows or balcony in rotation.

A list like this lets the worker plan their time and lets you see what was done.

### Prepare before they arrive

Leave dishes in the sink rather than spread around the flat, put laundry in one basket, and keep cleaning products stocked. Every minute the worker spends searching for things is a minute not spent cleaning.

### Keep the same order

Workers become faster and more thorough when they follow the same routine. Avoid changing priorities every visit.

### Check at the end

A quick look around before the worker leaves, at least in the first weeks, helps catch anything missed while it is still easy to fix.

## Paying part-time staff fairly

- **Pay on a fixed date,** monthly or weekly as agreed
- **Pay for agreed visits,** even if you cancel one at short notice
- **Do not cut pay for public holidays** if the worker was told they had the day off
- **Give an Eid bonus,** usually proportional to the monthly pay
- **Review pay once a year**

Part-time workers depend on several households for their income. If one household pays late or cuts visits often, they will replace it with another.

## Keys, access and security

Part-time workers often come when nobody is home. Decide how they will get in:

- **Family member at home:** the simplest option, if someone can be there
- **Key with the worker:** convenient, but record it and change locks if the worker leaves
- **Key with the guard or neighbour:** common in apartment buildings and gated streets
- **Building access cards:** check building rules for visitors and staff

Keep valuables locked away and avoid leaving cash around. Many families install a camera at the entrance so they can see arrivals and departures.

## Checks for part-time staff

It is tempting to skip checks for someone who only comes a few hours a week. Do not. Part-time workers see inside your home, often when nobody else is there. At minimum, verify the original CNIC, confirm the current address and speak to at least one other household where they work. ## Making a part-time cook's visit go further

A part-time cook who visits for two or three hours in the evening can cover far more than one dinner if the visit is planned well. Many working families in Islamabad use the visit to cook dinner, prepare lunch boxes for the next day and make one curry or salan that keeps well in the fridge for the following evening. Agree a weekly menu on Sunday, have the groceries ready before the cook arrives, and keep basics such as chopped onions, ginger garlic paste and boiled rice in the fridge so less time is spent on preparation. Ask the cook to label and date anything stored. Families who plan this way often find that four visits a week feed them for six or seven days.

## Part-time help for elderly parents

Many older residents of the F and G sectors live independently but find some tasks harder each year: bathing safely, changing bed sheets, cooking full meals, or going to the market. A part-time caretaker for a morning or afternoon shift can cover exactly those tasks while the parent keeps their independence for the rest of the day. It is also a good first step for families who are not yet sure a full-time caretaker is needed. Ask the caretaker to note anything worrying, such as a missed meal, confusion or a fall, and to message a family member the same day.

## Extra hours for busy periods

Part-time domestic staff Islamabad families already know can also cover short busy periods without a new hire. Ask your regular part-time maid or cook whether they can add hours before a family wedding, during Ramadan when iftar preparations take longer, when relatives from abroad come to stay, or during school exam weeks when parents need more quiet time. Agree the extra pay in advance. Using a worker who already knows your home is usually far better than bringing in a stranger for a few weeks.

## Part-time babysitters for evenings out

Parents in Islamabad often want a few hours to attend a wedding, a work dinner or simply to go out together. A regular part-time babysitter who knows the children, their bedtime routine and the house rules makes this easy. Leave clear written instructions: dinner, bath, bedtime, emergency numbers and any allergies. Agree when you will be home and how the babysitter will get back to their own home late at night, especially in winter. Paying for a ride home is both safe and fair.

## Common problems with part-time help and how to fix them

**The worker keeps arriving late.** Check whether your slot fits their other commitments. Agree a realistic time, and be clear that repeated lateness is not acceptable.

**Work is rushed.** The task list may be too long for the hours. Either add time or reduce tasks.

**The worker cancels often.** Ask why. If they are taking on too many homes, they may drop yours anyway. Consider offering more hours or a better slot.

**Things go missing.** Check carefully first, then speak calmly. Keep valuables locked, and if the problem continues, contact us for a replacement.

**The worker wants to bring a helper.** Some part-time maids bring a relative to work faster. Only allow this if you have checked the second person too.

## When to move from part-time to full-time

Signs that you have outgrown part-time help:

- The house is never quite clean by the end of the week
- You keep adding tasks and the worker cannot finish
- You have a new baby, an elderly parent moving in, or more guests than before
- You need someone at fixed times, such as for school pick ups

At that point, a full-time maid or a combination of a full-time worker and a part-time cleaner may work better.
