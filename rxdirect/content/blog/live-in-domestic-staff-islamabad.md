For many families in Islamabad, live-in staff are what make a household work. The cook who starts breakfast at six before the school rush, the nanny who is there when the baby wakes at two in the morning, the caretaker who sleeps within call of an elderly father, the couple who look after the house when the family travels. Live-in domestic staff Islamabad households rely on become part of the rhythm of the home in a way that live-out staff rarely do.

But living in is a big arrangement for both sides. The worker gives up a home of their own, and the family gives up some privacy. When it is set up carefully, it can last for many years. When it is not, it often breaks down within months. This guide explains how to set it up well.

## Who should consider live-in domestic staff Islamabad families hire?

Live-in arrangements suit some households much better than others. They work well when:

- **There is a newborn or young children** and help is needed early, late or overnight
- **An elderly parent needs someone nearby** at night, for example after a fall, stroke or with dementia
- **Both parents work long hours** and need help before they leave and after they return
- **The family entertains often** and dinners run late
- **The house is large**, with a garden and quarters, and needs someone present
- **The family travels often** and wants the house looked after
- **Public transport to the area is poor**, as in some of the newer societies, farmhouses and outlying areas

Live-in arrangements suit less well when the home is small with no separate room for staff, when the family values complete privacy in the evenings, or when the work genuinely needs only a few hours a day.

## Staff quarters: the foundation of a good live-in arrangement

The single most important factor in whether live-in domestic staff stay is where they live. Many Islamabad houses have servant quarters behind the kitchen, above the garage or at the back of the plot. Their condition ranges from comfortable to unacceptable.

A fair standard includes:

- **A private room** with a door that locks from inside
- **A window** for light and air
- **A ceiling fan** for summer and a **safe heater** for winter
- **A proper bed, mattress, pillow, sheets and quilt**
- **A cupboard or shelf** for clothes and belongings
- **A bathroom** with a working toilet, tap and access to hot water
- **Electricity and a socket** to charge a phone
- **Separate accommodation** for male and female staff who are not related

If staff quarters have been unused for a while, check for damp, leaks, insects and electrical safety before anyone moves in. A small investment in paint, a new mattress and a reliable heater pays for itself many times over. Before a candidate accepts, show them the room during the interview. Workers who see their quarters in advance know what to expect, and the ones who are uncomfortable can say so before anyone has invested time in a trial. Quarters near the generator, water pump or open drains are worth improving, because noise, fumes and mosquitoes make rest impossible and a tired worker cannot do a good job.

## Islamabad winters need special care

Islamabad is colder than many workers expect, especially those from southern Punjab or Sindh. From December to February, nights regularly drop close to freezing. Quarters are often the coldest part of the house.

Make sure live-in staff have:

- A safe heater, ideally electric, with proper ventilation if gas is used
- Warm bedding, including a thick quilt and blanket
- Warm clothes, or an allowance to buy them
- Hot water for bathing

Gas heaters in small, closed rooms can be dangerous. Make sure the room has ventilation and explain carbon monoxide safety. Every winter there are tragic cases in Pakistan of people dying from gas heater fumes in closed rooms.

## Food

Live-in staff need proper meals every day. Families usually choose one of two approaches:

1. **Staff eat the same food as the family**, either cooked together or set aside. This is common and is usually appreciated.
2. **Staff receive groceries or a food allowance** and cook for themselves. This suits staff with different food preferences or a separate kitchen in the quarters.

Whichever you choose, be generous and consistent. Leftovers only, or noticeably poorer food, cause quiet resentment. Tea, sugar and milk should be freely available.

## Hours and rest

The biggest risk with live-in domestic staff is that the job has no end. Because the worker is always in the house, it is easy to call them for one more cup of tea at 11 pm, one more errand on their day off.

Set clear working hours, even for live-in staff. A typical arrangement:

- **Working hours:** for example 7 am to 2 pm and 5 pm to 9 pm
- **Daily rest:** at least two to three hours in the afternoon
- **Night rest:** uninterrupted sleep, except for roles like nannies or caretakers where night duty is part of the job and agreed in advance
- **Weekly day off:** one full day, fixed, with freedom to leave the house

For roles with night duties, such as a newborn nanny or a caretaker for a patient who wakes often, agree how much night work is expected and give rest during the day to make up for it.

## Days off and leave

Live-in staff need time away to see family, meet friends and rest. Agree:

- **A weekly day off,** such as every Friday or Sunday, from morning to evening or overnight
- **Annual leave,** usually around two weeks, often taken at Eid
- **Emergency leave** for illness or a death in the family

Many live-in staff in Islamabad come from distant villages and prefer to save their leave for longer trips home. Some families allow staff to combine leave into one longer visit each year.

## Privacy on both sides

Living together closely needs boundaries.

**For the family:**

- Decide which rooms staff can enter and when
- Keep valuables and private papers secure
- Have private conversations away from staff areas
- Be clear about whether staff may use the family kitchen, TV lounge or garden after hours

**For the worker:**

- Their room is their private space; enter only with permission or in an emergency
- Do not search their belongings without serious cause and their presence
- Respect their phone calls, prayers and rest time
- Allow them to receive family visits by arrangement

Mutual respect for privacy is what makes a live-in arrangement comfortable rather than tense.

## Visitors and going out

Agree rules about visitors and leaving the house. A common approach in Islamabad:

- Staff inform a family member before going out and when they expect to return
- Visitors are met at the gate or in the quarters by prior arrangement, never inside the main house without permission
- No overnight guests in the quarters without permission
- A reasonable curfew on normal days, with flexibility on the day off

Explain the reasons, especially security. Workers usually accept rules that are explained fairly.

## Live-in or live-out: a quick comparison

| | Live-in | Live-out |
|---|---|---|
| Availability | Early mornings, evenings, sometimes nights | Fixed daytime hours |
| Cost to family | Salary plus room, food and utilities | Salary, sometimes a transport allowance |
| Privacy | Less for both sides | More for both sides |
| Commute problems | None | Lateness on bad traffic or rainy days |
| Best for | Infants, elderly care, large houses, frequent guests | Small homes, predictable routines |
| Turnover risk | Low if quarters and hours are fair | Low if the commute is short |

Many families start with live-out staff and switch to live-in domestic staff Islamabad agencies supply once a baby arrives or a parent moves in. Others do the reverse when children grow up. There is no single right answer, only the one that fits your home at this stage.

## Ending a live-in arrangement fairly

Sometimes a live-in arrangement has to end, because the family is moving abroad, the parent being cared for has passed away, or the fit is simply not right. Because the worker's home is tied to the job, ending it needs extra care. Give as much notice as you can, ideally a month, or pay in lieu. Settle the full salary and any leave owed. Give the worker time to arrange somewhere to stay, and help with transport for their belongings. Return any documents you hold and write a short reference if the worker served well. A fair ending protects your reputation among staff in the area, which matters when you hire again.

## Live-in staff with families

Some workers have spouses or children. A domestic couple can live together in the quarters, which often works well. Children living in the quarters are a larger commitment and need careful thought about space, schooling and noise. Discuss it openly at the start rather than discovering it later.

## Making the first month work

The first month of a live-in arrangement is the hardest for the worker. They are in a new home, often in a new city, away from family. Help them settle:

- Show them the quarters before they move in and fix anything needed
- Explain the house routine, rules and duties clearly on the first day
- Introduce them to other staff
- Show them the nearest market, mosque and transport
- Check in at the end of the first week and the first month

A worker who feels welcomed in the first month is far more likely to stay for years.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do live-in domestic staff in Islamabad earn less because they get food and a room?

Not usually. Live-in staff are available for longer hours, so the cash salary is often similar to or higher than live-out staff in the same role.

### How many hours should live-in staff work?

Agree clear working hours, typically ten to eleven hours spread across the day with a long afternoon break, plus uninterrupted sleep at night unless night duty is part of the role.

### Can I hire a live-in maid if my house has no servant quarters?

Only if you can provide a private, lockable room with access to a bathroom. Sharing a room with children or sleeping in the kitchen is not acceptable.
