Askari housing societies, developed by the Defence Housing Authority network and army welfare trusts across Islamabad, Rawalpindi, Lahore and Karachi, are home to serving officers, retired military personnel and their families. It's a community where security protocol isn't an afterthought, and where the standard for who gets let into a home is understandably higher than average.

That's exactly the standard RX Direct was built around.

## What Askari residents typically expect from domestic staff

From the requests we get across Askari sectors, a few things come up consistently:

- **Documented, verifiable identity**, a CNIC on file isn't optional, it's the baseline.
- **A clean, checkable employment history**, vague references or unverifiable past employers are a dealbreaker.
- **Punctuality and discipline**, households used to structured routines expect staff who show up on time, every time.
- **Comfort with security checkpoints**, Askari sectors typically have their own gate security, and staff need to be able to produce ID and explain their purpose of visit without friction.

## How we screen for Askari placements

Every candidate we place in an Askari housing scheme goes through the same core process we use everywhere, CNIC and address verification, reference checks with at least two previous employers, an in-person interview, and a police character certificate for security-related roles, but we specifically prioritize candidates with prior experience working in cantonment or gated-community settings, since they're already familiar with the entry procedures these societies use.

## Gate procedures: what staff need to know before day one

Every Askari sector has its own version of the same routine, and it trips up staff who haven't worked in cantonment areas before:

- Visitor and staff entries are logged at the gate, often with CNIC recorded against the specific house number.
- Some sectors require advance notification from the resident before a new domestic worker is allowed in for the first time, we brief clients on this so there's no delay on placement day.
- Staff are expected to carry original CNIC (not a photocopy) at all times when moving in and out.
- Guards at Askari gates are typically ex-servicemen themselves, and they tend to notice inconsistencies, a candidate who's nervous about basic questions at the checkpoint is a signal worth taking seriously, which is part of why we screen for comfort with this kind of scrutiny before we ever send someone for an interview.

## Household types we place into most

Askari sectors aren't uniform, the households range from young officers' families in smaller houses to senior retired officers in larger, more established homes. A few patterns show up repeatedly:

- **Serving officers' families** who relocate on posting and need staff set up quickly, often within days of moving in.
- **Retired officers' households**, frequently long-settled, where staff turnover is low and replacements are hired to match an existing routine rather than build a new one.
- **Joint families** spanning two generations, where a cook and a helper are hired together to cover a larger home.

## What a typical Askari placement looks like

A resident messages us with their sector and city. We ask a few clarifying questions, live-in or live-out, language preference, any dietary requirements for a cook, whether the role needs a police character certificate. Within a short window we send a shortlist of two or three pre-verified candidates with their documentation already checked, the resident interviews by phone or in person, and once a choice is made we coordinate the gate entry details directly with the family so the first day isn't held up at security.

## Questions army families ask us most

**Does a candidate need prior cantonment experience to be considered?**
No, but we're upfront about the tradeoff, a first-timer may need a day or two to adjust to gate logging and ID checks, so if you'd rather avoid that adjustment period entirely, tell us and we'll prioritize candidates who've already worked in Askari or DHA sectors.

**What happens when a serving officer's family is posted out and a new family moves in?**
This is common in Askari sectors, and staff continuity is often a priority for the outgoing family. If existing staff are staying on for the incoming household, we can still run a verification check on them even though they weren't originally placed by us, so the new family has documentation on file from day one.

**Can you place staff quickly for a family relocating on short notice?**
Yes, postings often come with limited lead time, and we treat these as priority placements. The more notice we get, the more choice you'll have in the shortlist, but we can move quickly when the timeline is tight.

**Do guards placed in Askari sectors need any qualification beyond a police character certificate?**
Many households also look for prior security or military-adjacent experience, which we factor into the shortlist when the role is specifically for gate or property security rather than general household duties.

## What a typical booking looks like

Most Askari placements start with a WhatsApp message naming the city, sector, and the role needed. We ask a handful of clarifying questions, live-in or live-out, any language preference, whether a police character certificate is required for the role, and return a shortlist of pre-verified candidates, usually within 48 hours. The family interviews by phone or in person, confirms a choice, and we coordinate the gate paperwork so entry isn't held up on the first day. A short trial period follows before the arrangement is treated as long-term, and if the fit isn't right, we arrange a replacement rather than leaving the family to restart the search.
