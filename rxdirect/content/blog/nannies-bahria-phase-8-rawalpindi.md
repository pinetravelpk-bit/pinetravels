Bahria Town Phase 8, also known as Safari Valley, has become one of the more popular sections for young families relocating within Rawalpindi, which means it's also one of our most active areas for nanny and babysitter requests.

## What families in Phase 8 typically need

Requests generally fall into three patterns:

- **Full-time childcare** for working parents, usually covering a set daytime schedule.
- **After-school support**, supervision, homework help and play for school-age children in the afternoon and evening.
- **Occasional evening babysitting** for parents who need coverage for a few hours here and there, rather than a daily arrangement.

## How we screen nannies and babysitters

Given the nature of the role, we go further than our standard process: CNIC and address verification, reference checks specifically focused on childcare experience, an in-depth in-person interview, and a health screening. We prioritize candidates with direct experience working with the age group your household needs, infant care is a different skill set than after-school supervision for a ten-year-old.

## Full-time, part-time or occasional

Let us know your schedule upfront. We match full-time live-in or live-out nannies, part-time daily arrangements, and candidates available for occasional evening bookings.

## What a typical booking looks like

Most Phase 8 inquiries start with a WhatsApp message covering your children's ages and the hours you need covered, full daytime, after-school, or occasional evenings. We follow up with a short shortlist of candidates matched to that age group, usually within a day or two. From there, most families do a brief phone or video introduction before meeting the candidate in person, ideally with the children present so you can see how they interact before committing. For full-time roles, we recommend the in-person meeting happen at your home so the candidate gets a sense of the household routine as well.

## Trial period and replacements

We suggest treating the first couple of weeks of any Phase 8 placement as a trial period, since the day-to-day fit, how a nanny handles your child's routine, how she communicates with you, how she gets along with other household staff, only really becomes clear once things settle in. If something isn't working, let us know and we'll find a replacement rather than leaving your family to make do.

## Questions we get asked most in Phase 8

**Can we request a nanny who lives locally in Bahria Town rather than commuting from elsewhere in Rawalpindi?** We'll factor this into the shortlist if it's a priority, though the strongest match for your children's ages sometimes lives outside the immediate area.

**How do you handle school pickup coordination for after-school candidates?** We confirm pickup timing and location details as part of the placement so there's no ambiguity on day one, and encourage a short trial run during the first week.

**Is occasional evening babysitting available from the same pool as full-time nannies?** Not always the same individuals, some candidates prefer fixed full-time roles while others specialize in flexible evening bookings, so let us know which you need upfront.

## Documents and verification you should expect to see

Before a candidate is placed in your Phase 8 home, you should expect to see a copy of her CNIC, a reference contact specifically tied to prior childcare work, and confirmation the health screening is complete. We'd rather a family call the listed reference directly than take our summary at face value, a quick conversation with a previous employer often reveals things about routine, patience, or communication style that don't come through in a written reference check. If any of this hasn't been shared with you before a placement is finalized, ask, it should be standard, not an extra step you have to request.

## Language and communication preference

Phase 8's mix of long-time Rawalpindi families and newer arrivals from other cities means household language habits vary more than you'd expect within a single society. Some families want a nanny fluent in Urdu and comfortable with English for homework help; others, especially households with grandparents living in, prioritize Punjabi. We ask about this specifically when building your shortlist rather than assuming, a candidate who's strong on experience but doesn't communicate comfortably with the rest of the household tends to be a harder fit than the CV alone would suggest.
