Hayatabad is Peshawar's largest and most organized planned township, developed across multiple numbered phases. Given its scale, most homes rely on their own dedicated guard rather than a single township-wide security perimeter, which makes individual vetting essential.

## Our screening process for Hayatabad placements

Hayatabad's numbered-phase layout means two houses on paper-adjacent streets can have very different security context depending on how close they sit to the market areas, the sports complex, or the quieter residential phases further out. Because there's no single township-wide perimeter to lean on, we spend extra time during screening walking each candidate through how they'd handle a specific phase's layout, gate positioning, likely foot traffic, and nearby commercial spillover, rather than treating it as generic residential duty. This sits on top of our baseline checks: CNIC verification, a police character certificate, reference checks with previous employers on reliability and any past incidents, and a physical fitness check.

## Shift options

Households in phases nearer Hayatabad's markets tend to prefer a guard who's most alert during business hours and evening foot traffic, while those in the quieter outer phases usually prioritize night coverage. Full 24-hour rotations, typically covered by two guards working 12-hour shifts each, remain available for either, let us know which phase you're in and we'll factor that into the shift recommendation.

## Beyond the standard checks

Because Hayatabad households rely so heavily on their own guard rather than a shared perimeter, we go a step further than our baseline verification for placements here. That means confirming a candidate's familiarity with the specific phase they'll be working in where possible, checking references not just for reliability but for how they handled unfamiliar visitors or vendors at a previous posting, and walking through basic emergency scenarios, what they'd do if someone tried to bypass the gate, or if a family member needed help quickly. It's a more conversational part of the screening than a paperwork check, but it tells us a lot about how a candidate will actually behave on a quiet residential street.

## Questions we get asked most in Hayatabad

**Do you place guards for houses near the market phases specifically?** Yes, we factor proximity to Hayatabad's markets and commercial spillover into which candidates we recommend, since that context changes what a guard needs to watch for.

**Can one guard cover both a house and a small adjoining office?** In some cases, yes, depending on the layout and how much separate foot traffic the office gets. Tell us the setup and we'll advise whether one guard is realistic or whether you need two.

**Is a background check really done for every guard, or just references?** Every placement goes through CNIC verification, a police character certificate, and reference checks with past employers, not just an informal reference call.

**How quickly can we get someone if we're in one of the outer phases?** Outer phases don't usually take any longer than central ones; the 48-hour shortlisting window applies across Hayatabad.

## What a typical booking looks like

Most Hayatabad inquiries start with a WhatsApp message naming the specific phase, property type and preferred shift. Because phase context matters so much here, we usually follow up with a question or two about how close the property sits to a market area or main road before shortlisting. We then share two or three candidate summaries with their background details, arrange a short introductory meeting, and confirm placement once you're comfortable. Most requests move from first message to a guard on duty within 48 hours.

## Uniform, equipment and reporting

Guards arrive uniformed from their first shift, nothing for the household to arrange separately. Standard equipment includes a torch for night duty and a visitor/gate log, which we recommend keeping even in the quieter outer phases, since it becomes a useful record if anything ever needs to be checked later. On reporting, guards are trained to raise anything unusual directly with the household, an unfamiliar vehicle parked near the gate, a visitor who doesn't check out, unusual activity spilling over from a nearby market phase, rather than deciding on their own that it isn't worth flagging. If you'd prefer a more structured daily handover note, let us know during placement and we'll brief the guard accordingly.
