Islamabad and Rawalpindi function as one labor market for domestic staff, many cooks who work in Islamabad's F-sectors or DHA live in Rawalpindi, and vice versa. That's exactly why our local team covers both cities seamlessly, without treating the twin cities as two separate hiring pools.

## What households in both cities ask for most

- **Daily home cooks** focused on Pakistani meals, the single most common request across both cities.
- **Live-in cooks** for larger joint-family households, often paired with light helper duties.
- **Event-ready cooks** for households that entertain often and need comfort with larger quantities and continental menus.

## One verification standard, two cities

Whether the placement is in Islamabad's Bahria Town or Rawalpindi's Askari sectors, every cook goes through the same core process: CNIC and address verification and reference checks with previous employers. Rawalpindi's cantonment societies add an extra step, candidates need to clear the cantonment board's gate registration and CNIC check before they can work inside, so we prepare that paperwork in advance for Askari and cantonment-area placements rather than leaving it to be sorted after a start date. Islamabad placements, particularly in the diplomatic enclave and F-sectors, more often call for continental or international menu experience alongside Pakistani cooking, so we weight the cooking-skills interview accordingly.

## Questions we get asked most

**Can we get a live-in cook who also helps with light cleaning?** In larger joint-family households, particularly in Rawalpindi's older sectors, yes, it's a common combination, but we confirm the split of duties with the candidate upfront so expectations are clear on both sides before placement.

**How does cantonment gate registration affect our start date?** For Askari and other cantonment-area placements, the candidate's CNIC and gate paperwork needs to clear the cantonment board before they can begin work on-site. We start that process as soon as a candidate is shortlisted rather than after an offer, so it rarely adds meaningful delay.

**Do you place cooks who can handle both Pakistani and continental menus?** It's one of the more frequent requests from Islamabad's F-sectors and diplomatic enclave, and we specifically screen for that dual capability rather than assuming a candidate strong in one style is automatically comfortable in the other.

**Is a trial period possible before we commit long-term?** Yes, we encourage it. A short trial lets the household confirm taste, punctuality and kitchen habits match expectations, and if it isn't the right fit, we'll look at a replacement rather than leaving the family to restart the search alone.

## Live-in vs live-out arrangements

Live-in placements suit joint-family homes that want a cook available across the full day, often with some light helper duties folded in. Live-out is more common for smaller households or where the family prefers a fixed schedule with a clear boundary between work and personal time. Both arrangements are common across Islamabad and Rawalpindi, and we ask which one a household wants before shortlisting, since it changes which candidates are actually a fit.

## Household types across the twin cities

Islamabad's F-sectors, diplomatic enclave and newer Bahria Town phases tend to draw corporate and diplomatic households that entertain more formally and often want continental menu experience alongside Pakistani cooking. Rawalpindi's older sectors, Askari areas and Scheme 3 tend to have more joint-family households with an established kitchen routine, where the priority is usually finding a cook who can slot into that routine rather than build a new one from scratch. We factor this in when building a shortlist, since a candidate who's thrived in one setting doesn't automatically suit the other.

## What a typical booking looks like

Most requests start with a WhatsApp message naming the city or society, household size, and whether the role is live-in or live-out. We ask a few follow-up questions, dietary needs, cuisine preference, whether the household entertains often, and return a shortlist of two or three verified candidates, usually within 48 hours. Families typically do a first interview by phone, then meet the top choice in person before confirming. A trial period follows before anything is treated as a long-term arrangement, and if the fit isn't right, we go back to the shortlist rather than leaving the household to restart the search.
