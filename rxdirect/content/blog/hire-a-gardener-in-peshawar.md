Peshawar has a sharper seasonal swing than the cities of central Punjab, with hot dusty summers, a real winter that drops into single digits, and a spring window that dictates most of the year's planting. Homes in Hayatabad, University Town, Cantt, and the Defence phases tend to have well-tended lawns and shaded courtyards, and the climate here means a gardener is working against dust, dry winds, and a shorter growing season rather than just routine upkeep. A mali who understands local soil, knows which grasses hold up through the loo winds, and can time the winter flower rotation to Peshawar's earlier cold is worth far more than a general handyman with a mower. That is the kind of gardener RX Direct places across the city.

The requests we get from Peshawar households and businesses generally fall into a few patterns:

## Trial period and replacement, explained simply

Every placement starts with a trial period, and it isn't there because we doubt our own screening. It exists because a gardener who interviews well and passes the skill assessment can still turn out to be a poor fit for a specific garden's conditions or a household's expectations. Those first few weeks are where you see whether they keep to the agreed days, how the lawn responds to their care, and whether their approach to pruning and watering matches what you want. If something isn't working, tell us sooner rather than later. We go back to the shortlist and arrange a replacement candidate, so you're not restarting the search from scratch. An early flag is always easier to act on than one raised weeks in.

## Seasonal and local considerations in Peshawar

Peshawar's climate shapes the gardening calendar here more than in the cities further south. Summer brings sustained heat and dry winds that coat plants in dust and stress lawns, so a watering schedule tuned to early morning and a regular rinse of ornamental beds matters more than people expect. The monsoon reaches Peshawar lighter and later, but the humidity it brings can still trigger fungal issues in beds that stay damp. Winter here is genuinely cold, arriving earlier than in Lahore or Multan, and the seasonal flower rotation, pansies, calendula, and stocks, gets planted sooner as a result. Hedges and deciduous trees need their pruning timed to the dormant window before the spring flush. If your booking starts near a seasonal shift, mention it. We prioritise candidates who have already worked through a full Peshawar year, because the dust and the cold catch out gardeners new to the city.

## Questions Peshawar households ask us most

**How do gardeners deal with the dust that coats everything in summer?**
A gardener with local experience rinses ornamental beds and hardy shrubs on a regular cycle rather than just watering the roots, and schedules lawn work for early morning when dust settles. We confirm a candidate already plans this way during the interview.

**Can the gardener handle a larger Defence property with hedges and multiple beds?**
We ask about the full scope upfront. For bigger plots we shortlist candidates with proven experience on similar properties, because hedge maintenance and multi-bed care are a different workload from a single city lawn.

**What happens to the schedule during winter when growth slows down?**
A gardener with local experience shifts to pruning, bed prep, and winter planting rather than routine mowing, since the lawn barely grows in the cold. We flag candidates who already plan their year this way.
