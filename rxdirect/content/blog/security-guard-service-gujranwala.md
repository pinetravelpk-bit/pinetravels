## Our screening process

Gujranwala is known for its hardware, ceramics and utensil manufacturing units, many of which sit close to residential streets, so it's common for a homeowner here to also want gate coverage that can handle the occasional supplier vehicle or staff member arriving outside normal hours. Alongside CNIC verification and a police character certificate, we screen candidates on their comfort with basic visitor logging and gate-pass style procedures, not just static residential duty. Reference checks with past employers and a physical fitness check are standard for every placement.

## Shift options

Most Gujranwala households ask for either a single day-shift guard covering deliveries and household traffic, or a night-focused guard once the street quiets down after the local markets close. For properties that want continuous coverage, we run full 24-hour rotations with two guards working 12-hour shifts each, let us know your street's typical activity pattern and we'll suggest the right mix.

**Do guards come in uniform, or do we need to arrange that?** Guards arrive in uniform from day one, you don't need to source or launder anything separately.

**What happens if the guard doesn't show up one day?** Reliability is checked during reference calls, but if a no-show does happen, tell us immediately and we'll arrange stand-in coverage while we sort out the placement.

**Do you place guards for small factories, not just homes?** Yes, a number of our Gujranwala clients run a hardware or utensil unit alongside their residence, and we screen specifically for comfort with that mixed environment.

Before any candidate reaches your shortlist, we complete CNIC verification, obtain a police character certificate, run a physical fitness check and speak with at least one previous employer about reliability and conduct. For Gujranwala specifically, we also ask past employers about the candidate's comfort with gate-pass style procedures and visitor logging, since that comes up often given the number of homes here that sit close to manufacturing units. We share a summary of this background with you before you meet the guard, so you're not making a decision on a first impression alone.

## Uniform, equipment and reporting

Every guard we place arrives in uniform from their first shift, you don't need to arrange or launder anything. Basic equipment typically includes a torch for night duty and a visitor/gate log, which we recommend even for households that don't see much regular traffic, simply because it creates a record if anything ever needs to be checked later. On reporting, guards are trained to flag anything out of the ordinary, an unfamiliar vehicle idling near the gate, a supplier arriving outside the usual window, a visitor who can't be placed, directly to you rather than quietly deciding it isn't worth mentioning. If you'd prefer a more structured daily handover, let us know during placement and we'll brief the guard accordingly.

## Residential vs. mixed-use placements

A guard suited to a purely residential street isn't always the right fit for a home that shares a gate or boundary with a small manufacturing unit, workshop or shop, a common setup in parts of Gujranwala. The first calls mainly for attentiveness to family routine and visitor courtesy. The second needs someone comfortable questioning a supplier vehicle, logging deliveries, and staying alert during hours when commercial traffic picks up. Tell us upfront if your property falls into the second category so we can weight the shortlist accordingly rather than assuming a standard residential profile will do.
