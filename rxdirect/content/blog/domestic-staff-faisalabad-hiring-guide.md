Faisalabad's growing residential market, from established areas like Madina Town and Jinnah Colony to newer developments like Wapda City and Citi Housing, has traditionally relied on word-of-mouth hiring for domestic staff. That's changing as more households look for a verified, structured alternative.

## What Faisalabad households ask for most

- **Cooks** for daily Pakistani home cooking, most commonly requested from established family areas.
- **Helpers and cleaners**, often on flexible part-time schedules for working households.
- **Guards**, particularly in newer gated communities like Wapda City and Citi Housing Faisalabad.
- **Full household teams** for families setting up a new home in one of the city's growing societies.

## Our verification process

Every candidate we place in Faisalabad goes through the same core screening used nationwide: CNIC and address verification, reference checks with previous employers, an in-person interview, and role-specific checks (a police character certificate for guards, a hygiene orientation for cooks).

## Documents to ask for before hiring

Before any candidate starts work, ask for their original CNIC (and keep a photocopy on file), a recent passport-size photo, their current home address with a landmark, and the contact number of at least one previous employer. If the role involves a security gate pass, common in Wapda City and Citi Housing, you'll also need this paperwork ready in advance, since gated societies often won't issue a pass without it. Don't rely on a verbal address; ask the candidate to write it down themselves so you can cross-check it against their CNIC.

## Structuring a trial period

A short trial period, commonly two to four weeks, protects both sides. Agree in advance on working hours, specific duties, and a fixed check-in point (say, the one-week and one-month mark) to discuss how things are going. For live-in staff especially, use the trial to observe punctuality, how they handle unsupervised time, and whether their stated skills (cooking, driving, childcare) match reality before committing to a longer-term arrangement.

## How to actually check a reference

A written reference letter is easy to produce and hard to verify. A phone call is far more reliable. When you call a previous employer, ask specific, open-ended questions rather than yes/no ones: How long did they work for you? Why did they leave? Would you rehire them? Did you ever have concerns about honesty or reliability? A genuine former employer will answer these naturally; vague or rehearsed-sounding answers are a signal to dig further or ask for a second reference.

## Red flags during the interview

Watch for candidates who are reluctant to share a traceable address, can't name a previous employer who'll take a call, or give inconsistent answers about work history when asked the same question two different ways. In Faisalabad specifically, where a lot of hiring still happens through informal referrals, it's worth being extra cautious with candidates who arrive with no independent contact details beyond the person who referred them.

## How we structure a trial period

Once a candidate is selected, we recommend a two- to four-week trial before treating the arrangement as permanent. Agree in writing (even a simple note both sides keep) on hours, core duties and a day off, and set a check-in at around the one-week mark to catch any issues early rather than letting them build up silently. For live-in roles, use the trial window to observe things an interview can't reveal, punctuality, how someone handles unsupervised time, whether their claimed skills hold up in practice.
