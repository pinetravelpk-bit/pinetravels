Ramadan reshapes how a Gujranwala household moves through the day, and the change shows up at the gate before it shows up anywhere else. During the rest of the year, a home in DHA Gujranwala, Citi Housing, or Palm City sees a fairly predictable pattern of traffic: morning deliveries, the occasional supplier vehicle, and a quiet street after the local markets close. In Ramadan that pattern inverts. The daytime is quiet as families rest after sehri, the late afternoon brings a rush of iftar-related deliveries and visitors, and the real activity starts after tarawih, when relatives and friends call on each other late into the night. For a household that relies on a private security guard, that means the coverage that worked in Shaaban rarely fits Ramadan without some adjustment. Planning it ahead is far easier than fixing it mid-month, and this guide sets out how Gujranwala households are handling it.

## How a guard's shift shifts during Ramadan

A standard Ramadan shift for a Gujranwala guard is rarely the same as the rest of the year. Day shifts become lighter in terms of traffic but harder on the guard, because summer Ramadan means long fasting hours in heat, and static duty at a gate is physically demanding when water is not an option until iftar. Night shifts, by contrast, become busier and more important, because the household is up later, guest traffic runs well past midnight in the last ten days, and a guard who logs visitors and keeps an eye on the street is genuinely useful when more cars are coming and going than usual.

What works for most Gujranwala families is one of a few adjusted arrangements. Some keep a single day guard but push the shift later, so coverage runs from late afternoon through the iftar rush and into the evening, when it matters most. Others move from a day-only guard to a full 24-hour rotation with two guards on 12-hour shifts each, so the gate is covered through the late-night visiting hours and the household is not relying on community security alone. For homes that sit close to a manufacturing unit or workshop, a common setup in parts of Gujranwala, the late-afternoon shift also matters because supplier vehicles and delivery runs often cluster just before iftar, and a guard who can manage gate-pass logging during that window takes a real load off the household.

## Temporary extra help through the month

The single most common Ramadan request we get from Gujranwala is for temporary extra guard coverage, and it usually comes in two forms. The first is a second guard for the evening and night stretch, added on top of a regular day guard so the gate has continuous coverage during the busiest hours. The second is temporary cover for the last ten days of Ramadan and the run-up to Eid, when guest traffic peaks, many households host large iftar and dinner gatherings, and the regular guard may want time off for tarawih or ithikaf.

Temporary help also covers the days your regular guard cannot be at the post. A fasting guard doing a long summer day shift is more likely to need an occasional relief break or a day off, and rather than leaving the gate unattended, a verified stand-in for a few shifts keeps the coverage steady. The earlier you book this, the better, because the same verified guards are in demand across DHA Gujranwala, Citi Housing, and Palm City, and the last-ten-days window fills before anything else.

## Managing your existing guard during Ramadan

Your regular guard is fasting through the same long days as everyone else, and for a static outdoor role that is a real physical ask. A few practical steps keep the placement steady through the month. If your guard is on a long day shift in the heat, consider shortening the shift or arranging a short relief break, and make sure there is a shaded spot at the post and that iftar and sehri are available on site when the timing falls within their shift. If you are moving to a 24-hour rotation, talk the new pattern through with your guard directly and confirm it with us so the handover between shifts is clean. Be explicit about what you want flagged during Ramadan, late-night visitors, unfamiliar cars near the gate after tarawih, or supplier vehicles arriving outside the usual window, because more traffic means more judgement calls and your guard should know your preferences rather than guessing. And if your guard asks for time off in the last ten days, take it seriously and tell us early so we arrange verified cover instead of the gate going unmanned for a stretch.
