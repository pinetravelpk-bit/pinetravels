Askari and cantonment-area housing tends to follow a distinct pattern: wider plots, more consistent lawn and landscaping upkeep, and stricter gate and visitor protocols than many newer commercial housing societies. Two roles come up especially often as a result, gardeners and drivers, and both need staff who are comfortable working within that structure.

## Gardeners for Askari and cantonment homes

Larger lawns and more consistent landscaping standards mean many Askari and cantonment households want a gardener on a regular schedule rather than an occasional visit, weekly or twice-weekly visits are common, alongside seasonal planting and upkeep work. Every gardener we place goes through CNIC and address verification, reference checks, a personal interview, and a skills assessment.

## Drivers for Askari and cantonment homes

Drivers in these areas need to be comfortable with gate security procedures, producing ID on request, logging in and out consistently, and following whatever visitor protocol the specific sector uses. We verify license authenticity, review traffic violation history, and run a road test before shortlisting any driver for a placement in these communities.

## Entry and security procedures in Askari societies

Askari and cantonment sectors run tighter gate control than most residential areas, visitor logging at the main entrance, ID checks for anyone entering on foot or by vehicle, and in many sectors a requirement that domestic staff be pre-registered with the gate or society office before they're allowed in unaccompanied. Staff we place are briefed on this in advance: carrying CNIC at all times, expecting to sign in and out, and understanding that gate guards may call the household to confirm entry on a new staff member's first few visits. Drivers in particular need to be comfortable with vehicle checks at the barrier, since cantonment gates often log number plates alongside visitor details.

## Who typically hires in these societies

Households in Askari and cantonment sectors skew toward serving and retired military families, which shapes what they look for in staff. Many households have a routine built around a transferred or posted family member's schedule, so reliability and the ability to work with minimal supervision matter more than in a typical household, the primary decision-maker is often away for stretches at a time. Retired officers' households, meanwhile, often want a smaller, steady team (one gardener, one driver) rather than a large rotating staff, and value long-term, low-turnover placements over anything short-term.

## Most requested staff categories

Alongside gardeners and drivers, we regularly get combined requests from these households for cooks and cleaners, since many families prefer to set up a full household team in one round of hiring rather than filling roles one at a time. Guard requests are also common in sectors where the household sits near a boundary wall or a less-trafficked lane, even though the broader sector already has perimeter security.

## Questions we get asked most

**Do gardeners and drivers need society clearance before starting?**
Most sectors expect staff to be registered with the gate office or society management once hired, we provide CNIC and verification documents from our screening process to make that registration straightforward.

**Can the same driver work across two Askari sectors if we split time between properties?**
Yes, though the driver will need to go through entry registration separately at each sector's gate, so it's worth flagging this during the initial interview so they know what to expect.

**What happens if our regular gardener is unavailable for a scheduled visit?**
We keep backup staff on file for recurring placements and can arrange a substitute for a single visit if your regular gardener is sick or on leave, so your lawn schedule isn't disrupted.

## Typical scope of gardening duties

A "gardener" placement in Askari and cantonment housing usually covers more than mowing. Expect regular lawn maintenance (mowing, edging, watering schedules suited to the season), hedge and shrub trimming, seasonal flower bed planting and upkeep, and basic pest and weed control. Larger plots common in these sectors sometimes also call for help maintaining a kitchen garden or fruit trees, which is worth flagging during the initial conversation since it changes the skill level and time commitment needed compared to ornamental lawn care alone.

## Seasonal patterns in gardening work

Gardening demand in Askari and cantonment sectors shifts noticeably with the seasons. Spring and early summer bring the heaviest workload, planting, more frequent watering, and faster grass growth that pushes many households from a weekly to a twice-weekly visit. Winter tends to mean lighter, less frequent work focused on hedge shape and general tidiness. Households that only need a gardener seasonally (heavy spring planting, then a lighter maintenance schedule the rest of the year) should say so upfront, since it affects whether we match a full-time gardener or someone available on a part-time, visit-based basis.

## What a typical booking looks like

Most requests start with a message telling us the city, sector, and which role you need, gardener, driver, or both. For gardeners, we ask about lawn and plot size, how often you want visits, and whether there's seasonal or specialty work involved (a kitchen garden, fruit trees). For drivers, we ask about the household's routine and whether the sector has strict gate registration requirements we should prepare the candidate for. From there we shortlist from our already-verified pool, share profiles with verification notes, and arrange an introduction. Because many Askari and cantonment households value long-term, low-turnover placements, we pay particular attention to matching for fit the first time rather than expecting frequent replacements.

## Trial period and replacements

For both gardeners and drivers, we recommend a short trial, a couple of visits for a gardener, a few days of supervised routes for a driver, before committing to a long-term arrangement. If the fit isn't right, tell us early and we'll shortlist a replacement from the same verified pool rather than leaving you to restart the search. This matters especially in Askari and cantonment sectors, where re-registering a new staff member at the gate takes a bit of extra coordination, so getting the placement right early saves everyone repeat paperwork.
