Hiring domestic help is a standard part of managing a household in Pakistan. Whether you're a busy corporate professional needing a reliable [driver](/services/drivers), a working mother looking for a trustworthy [nanny](/services/babysitters-nannies), or a family seeking a skilled [cook](/services/cooks), domestic staff keep our homes running smoothly. But the process of finding, vetting and keeping the right person is where most households run into trouble, usually because it's done informally, on word-of-mouth, with no paperwork and no real background check. This guide walks through the common pitfalls, what proper verification actually looks like, and how to set up a hiring process that protects your family and your home.

## Why so many households run into trouble

Most bad hiring outcomes in Pakistan don't come from bad luck, they come from skipping steps under time pressure. A guest is arriving in three days and you need a cook immediately, so you hire the first candidate a neighbour recommends without checking anything. A driver quits with no notice and you replace him the same week with someone whose only reference is "my cousin knows him." None of this is unusual, but it's exactly how households end up with staff who have no verifiable history, no agreed terms, and no accountability if something goes wrong.

## Common pitfalls to avoid

**Skipping identity and address verification.** A CNIC copy isn't proof of anything on its own, it needs to be checked against the address and history the candidate describes. Inconsistencies here are one of the clearest early warning signs.

**Relying on a single reference, or none at all.** One reference, especially a written letter, is easy to fabricate or exaggerate. A phone call to at least two previous employers reveals far more than a letter ever will.

**Not agreeing terms in writing before the first day.** Working hours, off-days, monthly salary, live-in versus live-out, what happens during Eid or a family emergency back home, all of this should be settled and understood before the candidate starts, not negotiated after a disagreement.

**Skipping the trial period.** A candidate can interview well and still be the wrong fit once they're actually working in your home. A short paid trial, typically 3 to 5 days for most roles, gives both sides a real chance to confirm things are working before any long-term commitment.

**Hiring through an unregistered, informal middleman.** If something goes wrong, an informal broker has no accountability and usually disappears. A [certified, registered agency](/registration) is answerable for who it places.

**Underpaying for the role.** Offering well below the going rate doesn't just risk losing a good candidate to a better offer, it also correlates with higher turnover and less reliable staff overall.

**No plan for what happens if it doesn't work out.** Without an agreed replacement policy, a placement that fails means starting the entire search from zero, again.

## What proper verification actually looks like

At RX Direct, we believe safety isn't built on trust alone, it's built on verification. Before any candidate joins our roster to be placed in your home, they go through a defined screening process:

1. **CNIC and address verification**, cross-checked against the history the candidate describes.
2. **Reference checks by phone**, not just a written letter, with at least two previous employers. A phone call surfaces details a letter never will: why they left, whether the employer would rehire them, how specific issues were actually handled.
3. **A police character certificate**, essential for [security guards](/services/security-guards) and [office boys](/services/office-boys), useful for every role.
4. **An in-person interview**, to assess communication, professionalism and fit for your household's routine, not just a phone screening.
5. **Health screening**, relevant for cooks, helpers and any role working closely with family members.

Only candidates who clear every step are shortlisted for placement, which is also why we can offer a [replacement guarantee](/how-it-works) if a placement doesn't work out.

## Structuring a fair trial period

A trial period, typically the first 3 to 5 days for most roles and a bit longer for live-in positions, gives both sides a chance to confirm the fit before treating the arrangement as permanent. Use it to assess punctuality, communication, how the candidate handles your household's actual routine, and, for cooks, whether their cooking genuinely matches your family's taste. Agree upfront on how notice works on either side if the trial doesn't go well, so nobody is caught off guard partway through.

## Red flags to watch for when interviewing

None of these alone should end a conversation, but together they're a signal to slow down and verify more carefully:

- Inconsistent details between the CNIC and the address or work history described
- Reluctance to provide contactable references, or references that can't be reached
- Vague, evasive answers about why they left a previous role
- Unexplained gaps in employment history
- Pressure to skip the trial period or start immediately without documentation
