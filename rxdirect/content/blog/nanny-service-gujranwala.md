## A city split between old and new

Gujranwala's childcare requests tend to split along fairly predictable lines. In DHA Gujranwala and Palm City, where younger dual-income couples have settled into newer housing away from extended family, we see steady demand for full-time or near-full-time nannies who can effectively run the child's day. In established Model Town households, the pattern is different, families often already have support at home and are looking for someone to cover school hours and after-school time rather than a full live-in role. Understanding which category a family falls into upfront saves a lot of back-and-forth in the matching process.

## What we check before placing a nanny

Every candidate goes through CNIC and address verification, reference checks focused specifically on childcare experience, an in-depth in-person interview, and a health screening. For the newer-society placements where a nanny may be managing most of the day solo, we weight experience and independence more heavily; for shared-care households, we prioritize candidates who work well as part of a team rather than expecting sole responsibility.

## Full-time versus part-time patterns

The DHA Gujranwala and Palm City households we work with are more likely to need full-time coverage, a nanny who's present for most of the working day, managing meals, naps, and activities largely independently. Model Town families, where support at home already exists, more often want a part-time or after-school arrangement: someone who arrives before school lets out and stays through homework and dinner prep, then leaves once a parent is back. Occasional bookings, an evening out, a weekend event, come up in both areas, and we treat those as a separate, shorter-term match rather than trying to stretch a full-time candidate's availability to cover a one-off.

## How we match on age-group experience

A nanny who has spent years with toddlers isn't automatically the right fit for a ten-year-old who needs homework supervision and a bit of independence, and the reverse is true too. When we shortlist candidates for Gujranwala families, we ask specifically about the age ranges they've cared for previously and how recently, rather than relying on a general "childcare experience" line on a CV. For the newer-society full-time roles in particular, we also look at whether a candidate has handled a full day of solo responsibility before, since that's a different demand than working alongside a parent or relative who's home for part of the day.

## Language and communication fit

Most Gujranwala households communicate at home in a mix of Punjabi and Urdu, and we factor that into matching rather than assuming English fluency is the priority it might be in some other cities. For Model Town families with older relatives in the house, a nanny who's comfortable in Punjabi often settles in faster than one who isn't, even if her formal experience is otherwise similar. In DHA Gujranwala and Palm City households where parents want a child exposed to English early, we'll flag candidates who can comfortably switch between the two. Either way, tell us what matters for your household, it changes who ends up on the shortlist.

## What the first two weeks actually look like

Once a nanny starts, we don't just disappear until something goes wrong. In the first week, we expect the family to notice the small things, whether the nanny follows the nap and meal routine you described, how she handles a child who's testing boundaries with someone new, whether she asks questions when unsure rather than guessing. For Model Town households sharing responsibilities with existing help, we also watch for how well she coordinates rather than works in isolation. If something feels off, flag it in week one rather than waiting, a scheduling mismatch or a communication style that isn't clicking is much easier to address early. "Not working out" doesn't have to mean a major incident; it's just as often a fit issue, and we'd rather hear about it and adjust than have a family quietly tolerate a bad match.
