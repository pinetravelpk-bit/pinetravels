Multan sits in the hottest band of southern Punjab, with summer temperatures that regularly push past the mid-forties and a gardening calendar shaped as much by survival as by aesthetics. Homes in Bosan Town, Cantt, Gulgasht, and New Multan tend to have generous lawns and shaded courtyards, and the surrounding orchard culture, mango above all, means a gardener here is often expected to know fruit tree care, not just ornamental beds. Keeping a lawn green in this climate, managing irrigation during long dry stretches, and timing seasonal planting around the brief winter window takes a mali with genuine local experience. That is the kind of gardener RX Direct places across Multan.

## What families/businesses in Multan usually need

The requests we get from Multan households and businesses generally fall into a few patterns:

1. **A visiting weekly gardener** for a family home in Bosan Town or Gulgasht, covering mowing, watering schedule, light pruning, and keeping ornamental beds alive through the heat.
2. **A gardener experienced with fruit trees** for households with mango, citrus, or guava trees on the property, where pruning, irrigation, and harvest-season care are part of the job.
3. **A grounds gardener for a clinic, school, or commercial premises** in Cantt or along Vehari Road that want their entrance and outdoor areas maintained on a fixed visit cycle.

## Our verification process for Multan placements

Every gardener we place in Multan goes through CNIC and address verification before anything else, so the person arriving at your gate is confirmed against their records and lives where they say they do. We then run reference checks with previous households, farms, or businesses a candidate has worked for, asking specifically about reliability and whether they understood the demands of Multan's climate. After that comes a personal interview where we talk through their hands-on experience with local soil, lawn grasses that hold up in extreme heat, and fruit tree care if the role needs it. Finally, we run a skill assessment so claims about mowing, pruning, and irrigation are tested rather than taken at face value. A candidate who can't clear each step doesn't move forward.

## Trial period and replacement, explained simply

Every placement begins with a trial period, and it isn't there because we doubt our screening. It exists because a gardener who looks right on paper can still turn out to be a poor fit for a specific garden's conditions or a household's expectations. Those first few weeks are where you see whether they actually keep to the schedule, how the lawn responds to their care, and whether their pruning approach matches what you want for your trees. If something isn't working, tell us early rather than waiting it out. We go back to the shortlist and arrange a replacement, so you don't have to restart the search from scratch. A flag in the second week is far easier to act on than one in the second month.

## Seasonal and local considerations in Multan

Multan's climate is the single biggest factor in any gardening placement here. Summer is long, dry, and brutal, with sustained heat from April well into September that stresses even hardy lawns and demands an early morning watering schedule, a midday soak in this heat does more harm than good. A gardener with real local experience knows this and adjusts without being told. The monsoon reaches Multan later and lighter than the northern cities, but the humidity it brings can trigger fungal issues in beds that stay damp, so drainage matters even when rainfall is modest. Winter is the real planting window here, with marigolds, calendula, and seasonal flowers going in as the weather cools in November, and fruit tree pruning falling in the dormant period before the spring flush. Mango harvest season in summer changes the workload on orchard plots, and a gardener used to that cycle plans the rest of the garden work around it. If your booking begins near a seasonal shift, tell us, we prioritise candidates who have already worked a full Multan year.

## Questions Multan households ask us most

**Can the gardener handle our mango trees as well as the lawn?**
Often yes, but we ask about fruit trees upfront because orchard care is a specific skill. We shortlist candidates with proven mango, citrus, or guava experience for those roles rather than treating tree care as something any mali can do.

**How does the watering schedule work in peak summer when water pressure is low?**
A gardener with local experience plans around the times water is actually available, often early morning, and flags supply issues to you rather than just letting beds dry out. We confirm a candidate is comfortable adapting to your home's supply pattern during the interview.

**Will the same gardener keep coming through the hottest months of summer?**
Most do, and we check a candidate's summer reliability record during reference calls. If a placement is starting right before peak heat, we make a point of confirming the candidate has already worked through a Multan summer rather than someone new to the climate.
