## What to expect when hiring in Peshawar

Regardless of neighborhood, we apply the same standard: verified CNIC and address, contactable references from at least two previous employers, an in-person interview, and a trial period before any long-term commitment. For cantonment-adjacent areas specifically, a police character certificate is standard for any security guard placement.

## Documents worth collecting before day one

Ask for the candidate's original CNIC, a recent photo, and the phone number of at least one previous employer before finalizing any hire. In Hayatabad and DHA Peshawar specifically, society entry procedures typically require registered staff details before issuing a gate pass, so having this paperwork ready at hiring time, rather than scrambling for it after the candidate has already started, saves a week of back-and-forth with the society office.

## What a proper reference check looks like

A written letter tells you very little; a phone call tells you a lot. Call the previous employer and ask specific questions: how long the person worked there, the actual reason they left, whether there were any concerns about reliability or honesty, and whether the employer would hire them again. Genuine answers tend to come with small specific details, a real reference remembers particulars. Vague, generic praise with no detail is worth double-checking against a second reference.

## Setting up a trial period that actually works

A two- to four-week trial is standard and reasonable for most roles, longer for live-in positions. Put the basics in writing before the trial starts, hours, core duties, day off, and schedule a short check-in partway through rather than waiting until the end to raise concerns. This is also the window to confirm role-specific skills: a cook's actual cooking, a driver's comfort with Peshawar's traffic patterns near Ring Road and Cantt, a helper's fit with the household's daily routine.

## Interview red flags

Be cautious with candidates who can't give a consistent, checkable address, who resist providing a previous employer's contact details, or whose account of their work history shifts when you ask about it a second time in a different way. These aren't automatic disqualifiers, but they're reasons to slow down and verify more carefully before finalizing a placement.

## Questions we're asked most about hiring in Peshawar

**Do candidates from outside Peshawar need extra verification?**
We apply the same CNIC and address verification regardless of where a candidate is originally from, the standard doesn't change, though we do note permanent address on file alongside current Peshawar residence.

**Can staff be shared between households in the same street in Hayatabad?**
Yes, this is common for part-time roles like cleaners and gardeners, and we can help coordinate scheduling if two or three neighboring households want to split a single person's time.

**What happens if a guard placement's police character certificate takes longer than expected?**
We start the certificate process as early as possible and keep the household updated on timing, in the meantime, we can usually suggest an interim option if the placement is urgent.

**Is a trial period negotiable if we need someone immediately?**
The trial period protects both sides, so we don't recommend skipping it even under time pressure, but we can shorten the check-in schedule and move faster on the interview and paperwork stages.

## How references should actually be checked

Don't stop at a written letter from a previous employer, pick up the phone and call them directly. A letter can be written by anyone and is impossible to verify on the spot; a conversation lets you ask follow-up questions and hear whether the answers come naturally. Ask how long the person actually worked there, the real reason they left, whether the employer had any concerns about honesty or reliability, and whether they'd hire the candidate again. Specific, detailed answers are a good sign; vague or rehearsed-sounding ones are worth checking against a second reference before you finalize anything.
